Overview

Monetary policy pivots from '87 crash response to anti-inflation hikes defending the dollar · Election year

1988 was the opening year of the "building momentum" era. The aftershocks of the October 1987 crash had not fully dissipated, and the early-year focal point was the dollar — which had depreciated since the Plaza Accord of 1985, particularly during 1987. In early January, governments intervened to buy $2–3 billion of dollars to support the FX rate, combined with two cuts in the federal funds target rate to 6.5%; a first modest rally ran from late January through mid-March.

Monetary policy pivoted at end-Q1. The fed funds target was raised from 6.75% on March 29 to 8.125% by August 9, with the discount rate raised to 6.5% the same day. The hikes were aimed not at inflation but at supporting the dollar — 3-month T-bill yields rose from 6.0% to 8.5% on the year, while the 10-year held flat at 9.0%, narrowing the term spread.

Tightening paused in late August and the market saw its largest rally leg of the year. On November 8, George H.W. Bush defeated Dukakis to become the 41st President, pledging continuity with Reagan; mid-month, Greenspan reiterated that tightening would continue, and the market pulled back. Early December's "Gorbachev rally" — the USSR's announcement of a unilateral 500,000-troop reduction — rekindled enthusiasm. The S&P 500 finished +12.4% on the year.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 4
Multi-country governments enter FX market to buy dollars
The US and other countries reportedly purchased $2–3 billion in FX markets to support the dollar. Markets generally read the floor as preventing a break below USD/JPY 120.
Jan 28
Fed funds target rate 6.8125% → 6.625%
Post-'87-crash easing continued. On February 11, a further cut to 6.5%.
Mar 29
Fed funds target rate raised to 6.75%
Monetary policy pivoted from '87-crash aftermath management to supporting the dollar — the start of the 1988 hiking cycle. The policy statement stressed the economy was not overheating and inflation pressures were manageable.
Apr 17
US Navy strikes Iran
Iran fell into a two-front position facing both Iraq and the US, paving the way for the Iran-Iraq ceasefire in July.
Jun 19
14th G7 Toronto Summit
Reagan's final G7 summit; agenda centered on trade, debt, and FX coordination.
Jul 8
Unemployment falls to 14-year low of 5.2%
Labor Department data. The strong jobs reading was paradoxically read by markets as strengthening the case for continued Fed hikes.
Jul 18
Democratic National Convention convenes
Held in Atlanta, nominating Massachusetts Governor Michael Dukakis for President and Senator Lloyd Bentsen for Vice President.
Aug 9
Discount rate 6.0% → 6.5%
The same day, the fed funds target was raised to 8.125%. This marked the peak of the 1988 hiking cycle; the Fed then paused for three months.
Sep 17
Seoul Olympics open
Hosted by South Korea. 159 nations and territories participated, ending the Cold War boycotts of the 1980 and 1984 Games.
Nov 8
George H.W. Bush elected 41st US President
Republican George H.W. Bush defeated Democrat Michael Dukakis, pledging continuity with Ronald Reagan's spirit and policies.
Nov 16
Greenspan: tightening will continue until deficits improve
Same-day data showed September trade deficit of $10.46 billion, materially narrower than August, but the Fed Chair's hawkish stance overshadowed the positive print.
Dec 7
Gorbachev announces unilateral 500,000-troop reduction
Speaking at the 43rd UN General Assembly session, the Soviet General Secretary announced a 500,000-troop reduction over two years and substantial cuts in conventional weapons. Investors interpreted this as easing future US fiscal deficit pressure and opening room for monetary easing.
Dec
Savings and Loan (S&L) Crisis continues to escalate
The Federal Savings and Loan Insurance Corporation (FSLIC) was insolvent, and the number of problem institutions kept accumulating, paving the way for the 1989 Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act (FIRREA).
