Overview

Nasdaq peaks 5048 on March 10 · Fed hikes to 6.5% · Election standoff + regulatory vacuum

2000 was the inflection point after five consecutive years (1995-99) of S&P 500 gains exceeding 20%. On January 10, AOL announced its merger with Time Warner, viewed as the peak deal of the internet bubble; subsequently Nasdaq and Dow trajectories diverged as "the market crowded" into tech. On March 10, Nasdaq peaked at 5,048 and began declining.

On April 3, the federal district court ruled Microsoft had violated antitrust law; on June 7, Judge Jackson ruled for breakup, sending tech capital fleeing. The Fed continued its hiking cycle begun in June 1999, hiking on February 2, March 21, and May 16 to push the fed funds rate to 6.5% — the cycle's final blow. From late May through late August, the three indices rebounded, but from September several earnings warnings combined with oil prices hitting a 10-year high sent Nasdaq back into decline.

On November 7, the election fell into the Florida standoff; on December 12, the Supreme Court's Bush v. Gore decision 5-4 ended the recount, locking in Bush's victory. On December 21, Clinton signed the Commodity Futures Modernization Act (CFMA), exempting OTC derivatives from SEC and CFTC oversight — a key institutional precursor to the 2008 crisis. Full year: S&P -10.14%, Dow -6%, Nasdaq -39.3%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 10
AOL announces merger with Time Warner
Combined market cap over $350 billion, the largest corporate merger in history, viewed as the peak deal of the internet bubble.
Feb 2
Fed hikes 25bp to 5.75%
Fifth hike of this cycle (begun June 1999).
Mar 10
Nasdaq Composite peaks at 5,048
The internet bubble peak. Nasdaq began declining and showed a brief style reversal vs. the S&P 500 and Dow.
Mar 21
Fed hikes 25bp to 6.0%
Sixth hike of this cycle.
Apr 3
Microsoft antitrust case: federal district court ruling of violation
Viewed as a landmark event in the internet financial bubble's collapse; tech capital fled rapidly, triggering global equity market declines.
May 16
Fed hikes 50bp to 6.5%
Seventh and final hike of this cycle; rates reached 6.5% (the highest level for nearly 7 years).
Jun 7
Judge Jackson rules for Microsoft breakup
Ordered Microsoft split into a Windows OS company and an application/IE browser company. The same month, the appellate court partially overturned the browser ruling, and Microsoft ultimately avoided breakup.
Jun 26
Human Genome draft published
Clinton and UK PM Blair jointly announced completion of the human genome working framework.
Oct
Clinton signs Permanent Normal Trade Relations (PNTR) with China
Cleared the US side's obstacles to China's 2001 WTO accession. Clinton signed October 10.
Oct 12
USS Cole destroyer attacked in Yemen by suicide bombers
17 US sailors killed; al-Qaeda claimed responsibility — the worst attack on US targets before 9/11.
Nov 7
Presidential election day · Florida standoff erupts
Bush vs. Gore vote totals extremely close; Florida election results disputed, entering a 5-week recount stalemate.
Dec 12
Supreme Court Bush v. Gore rules to end recount
The Supreme Court ruled 5-4 that Florida's recount violated the Equal Protection Clause and halted the recount, locking in Bush's victory.
Dec 21
Clinton signs the Commodity Futures Modernization Act (CFMA)
Exempted OTC derivatives (including CDS) from SEC and CFTC oversight, establishing the regulatory vacuum viewed as the institutional precursor to the 2008 crisis.
