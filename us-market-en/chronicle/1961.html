Overview

Kennedy inaugurated · Economy emerges from late-stage recession · Cold War escalation and Berlin Crisis

1961 was the opening year of the Kennedy administration. On January 20, 43-year-old Kennedy was sworn in as the youngest president in US history. The macro economy was still in the final phase of the 1960 recession, with industrial production YoY bottoming at -8.6% in January. But market rates kept declining; in early February the Fed authorized the New York Fed to purchase 1-year-plus Treasury securities, further loosening monetary conditions, and equities rallied for five consecutive months.

The economy began improving in March, and the NBER-defined recession ended in February. On March 4, the Deutsche mark revalued 4.76% against the dollar with the Dutch guilder following suit, easing US trade imbalances. The Bay of Pigs invasion on April 17 saw the CIA-trained Cuban exile force destroyed within three days, triggering a limited mid-to-late April pullback. From late May to late July, the Third Berlin Crisis and a cluster of secondary stock offerings in June-July drove a roughly 4% two-month correction.

Construction of the Berlin Wall on August 13 paradoxically locked tensions in place. In September-October, US and Soviet nuclear testing resumed, weighing on markets again. From November the macro entered peak recovery, with continuous positive data and improving sentiment. The Dow closed at 731, up 18.7%; the S&P 500 returned +23.13%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 3
US Breaks Diplomatic Relations with Cuba
Full rupture in US-Cuba relations, setting the stage for the Bay of Pigs and the Cuban Missile Crisis.
Jan 14
Eisenhower Issues Gold Control Order
Prohibited US residents from holding gold anywhere in the world, to counter mounting gold outflows.
Jan 20
Kennedy Sworn In as President
At 43, Kennedy became the youngest US president in history. After taking office he firmly affirmed the dollar's value, temporarily closing the October 1960 dollar crisis.
Early Feb
Fed Authorizes NY Fed to Purchase Long-Term Treasuries
The New York Fed subsequently bought significant amounts of 1-year-plus Treasuries, effectively easing monetary policy further and laying the groundwork for the twin-rate policy.
Feb
NBER-Defined Recession Ends
Industrial production YoY bottomed at -8.6% in January and began recovering. From March, macro conditions improved substantially.
Mar 4
West German Mark Revalued 4.76% vs. Dollar
Official rate adjusted from DM 4.2 per dollar to DM 4.0; the Netherlands followed, easing US trade imbalances.
Apr 17
Bay of Pigs Invasion
A CIA-backed Cuban exile force landed at the Bay of Pigs and was destroyed by Castro's forces within three days. The military defeat damaged US international standing.
Apr 12
Soviet Cosmonaut Gagarin Becomes First Human in Space
Vostok 1 carried Gagarin on one orbit of Earth, fully launching the space race.
May 5
Shepard Completes First US Manned Suborbital Flight
Mercury-Redstone 3 carried Alan Shepard into space, marking the US response to Soviet space gains.
May 25
Kennedy Announces Moon Landing Program
In a special address to Congress, Kennedy called for landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely within the decade, launching the Apollo program.
Jun 3-4
Kennedy-Khrushchev Vienna Summit
Khrushchev raised the Berlin issue and demanded US-UK-French withdrawal from West Berlin; Kennedy flatly refused. The Third Berlin Crisis began.
Early Jul
Soviet Union Suspends Demobilization, Defense Spending +33%
Kennedy requested Congress raise defense spending, call up reservists and National Guard, and expand civil and air defense. The Third Berlin Crisis peaked.
Jun-Jul
Wave of Secondary Equity Offerings
Bethlehem Steel, Gulf Oil, McKesson, A.H. Robins and others ran concentrated secondary offerings, impacting short-term liquidity.
Aug 13
Construction of the Berlin Wall Begins
East Germany began constructing barriers along the East-West Berlin border; tensions were locked in but short-term anxiety eased.
Sep
US and Soviet Union Resume Nuclear Testing
The Soviet Union resumed atmospheric nuclear tests in September; the US followed, taking the arms race to a new peak.
