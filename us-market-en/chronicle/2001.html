Overview

George W. Bush inaugurated · 11 rate cuts on the year · 9/11 and Enron deliver a double blow

2001 was the second year after the dot-com bust and the core of the NBER recession cycle (starting in March, ending in November). Newly elected President George W. Bush was sworn in on Jan 20. The Federal Reserve cut rates 50 bp inter-meeting on Jan 3 and another 50 bp on Jan 31, continuing 50-bp easing through the first half. The Fed cut 11 times in total, taking the federal funds target from 6.5% all the way down to 1.75%, the lowest since 1961. Fiscal policy joined in: on Jun 7 Bush signed the Economic Growth and Tax Relief Reconciliation Act of 2001 (EGTRRA), launching the largest tax-cut package since 1981.

The market traveled a three-stage path of rebound, second leg down, and recovery. Q1 tech earnings broadly missed; by early April the S&P 500 was down 19% and the Nasdaq down 41% year-to-date. From April onward, tax-cut expectations and the easing cadence drove the S&P and Dow up nearly 19% and the Nasdaq 41%. But beginning in late May, earnings warnings from EMC, Nortel, JDSU and others sent the market back down. On Sep 11, New York and Washington were hit by terrorist attacks, shutting US equity markets for four trading days—the longest closure in 80 years. After the Sep 17 reopen, markets fell again; the S&P dropped 26% and the Nasdaq 38.5% in Q3.

Markets bottomed in late September and rallied into year-end, with tech leading on combined fiscal and monetary support. Tail risks remained: the Enron scandal surfaced in November and Enron filed for bankruptcy on Dec 2, with $49.8 billion of assets setting a new record. The same month Argentina suspended payments on $130 billion of sovereign debt, the largest default on record at the time. On Dec 11 China formally joined the WTO. The S&P 500 finished -13.04%, the Dow -7%, and the Nasdaq -21% for the year.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 3
Fed delivers 50 bp inter-meeting emergency cut
Fed funds target 6.5% → 6%; discount rate 6% → 5.75%. Opens the 2001 easing cycle.
Jan 20
George W. Bush sworn in as the 43rd US President
Republicans retake the White House, launching tax cuts and the war on terror as the twin policy themes.
Jan 31
Fed cuts another 50 bp to 5.5%
100 bp of cuts in one month. Another 50 bp on Mar 18 brings Q1 total cuts to 150 bp.
Mar
NBER dates the US recession from March
Official recession start; lasts eight months through November. The real-economy fallout from the dot-com bust.
Mar 5
Fortune publishes "Is Enron Overpriced?"
First public challenge to Enron's financial structure; the scandal begins to take shape.
Jun 7
Bush signs the Economic Growth and Tax Relief Reconciliation Act of 2001
EGTRRA delivers $1.35 trillion of tax cuts over ten years, the largest package since the Reagan cuts of 1981.
Sep 11
New York and Washington hit by terrorist attacks
The Twin Towers collapse and the Pentagon is struck. US equity markets close for four trading days, the longest shutdown in eight decades.
Sep 17
US markets reopen; Fed delivers another 50 bp emergency cut
Fed funds rate drops to 3%, discount rate to 2.5%. Q3 sees the S&P down 26% and the Nasdaq down 38.5%.
Oct 7
US and UK forces launch military operations in Afghanistan
Operation Enduring Freedom begins, targeting the Taliban regime and al-Qaeda. The start of a twenty-year war.
Oct 23
Apple unveils the first-generation iPod
$399 launch price, 5 GB hard drive. The product that transformed Apple from a PC company into a consumer-electronics empire.
Oct 26
Bush signs the USA PATRIOT Act
Dramatically expands federal law-enforcement powers over investigation, surveillance, and financial review. The most significant domestic legislation after 9/11.
Dec 2
Enron files for bankruptcy protection
$49.8 billion of assets set a new record for US corporate bankruptcy. The accounting fraud comes to light.
Dec
Argentina suspends payments on $130 billion of sovereign debt
Equivalent to 25% of total emerging-market debt at the time and the largest sovereign default in history to that point.
Dec 11
Fed funds rate cut to 1.75% · China joins the WTO
The 11th cut of the year; discount rate drops to 1.25%, the lowest since July 1961. Same day, China becomes the WTO's 143rd member.
