Overview

Watergate dominates the year · Stagflation deepens · One of the worst years since the Great Depression

1974 was one of the worst years for US equities since the Great Depression. The macro backdrop was textbook stagflation: CPI climbed above 11%, Q2 GDP was revised to -6.3%, and the commercial bank prime lending rate marched from 8.75% to above 12% — mid-year the level was the highest since the 19th century. In March, Arab states lifted the oil embargo against the US, but the removal brought little relief.

On the political side, Watergate moved in rapid succession: the federal grand jury's indictment in March; the July 24 Supreme Court order for Nixon to surrender the tapes; the House Judiciary Committee's adoption of articles of impeachment on July 27; the release of the key tape on August 5; Nixon's resignation announcement on August 8; Ford's swearing-in on August 9; and Ford's unconditional pardon of Nixon on September 8. On the legislative side, the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA) was signed, laying the foundation for private-pension protection. The dollar continued to weaken after the collapse of Bretton Woods.

Monetary policy ran in two directions: tightening in H1 — the Fed lifted the discount rate from 7.5% to 8.0% on April 24 — then pivoting in October. The Sep 4 removal of the supplemental reserve on large CDs and the Dec 6 cut of the discount rate to 7.75% opened a new easing cycle. In October, the failure of Franklin National Bank became the largest US bank collapse to date. The Dow closed the year at 616, having touched a low of 578 on December 6.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 2
Fed cuts stock-margin requirement from 65% to 50%
A modest easing at the start of the year, a margin adjustment within Regulations T and U.
Mar 15
Federal grand jury names Nixon in Watergate cover-up conspiracy
The grand jury found that the president had participated in covering up White House officials' involvement in the DNC break-in.
Mar 18
Arab states lift oil embargo against the US
The first phase of the First Oil Crisis ended, but the lifting of the embargo brought little relief.
Apr 24
Fed discount rate raised from 7.5% to 8.0%
Tightening continued. From March to May the prime rate rose 11 times from 8.75% to 11.5%.
Jul 24
Supreme Court unanimously orders Nixon to surrender White House tapes
The court ordered Nixon to turn over the recordings to the Watergate special prosecutor — the judicial turning point that ended his presidency.
Jul 27
House Judiciary Committee adopts articles of impeachment
The committee approved impeachment of President Nixon.
Aug 5
Release of the key tape
The tape revealed Nixon directing aides to have the CIA obstruct the FBI's Watergate investigation. Congressional support collapsed quickly.
Aug 8
Nixon announces he will resign the next day
The final chapter of Watergate — the first US president to resign mid-term.
Aug 9
Ford sworn in as US president
The Dow stood at 777 when Nixon left office. He became the first president since Hoover whose entire tenure saw the stock market fall.
Sep 4
Fed removes the 3% supplemental reserve on CDs above $100,000
An early signal of modest monetary easing.
Sep 8
Ford grants Nixon an unconditional pardon
A pardon for "all offenses Nixon committed or may have committed" during his term.
Sep 2
Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA) signed
Established minimum standards and a protective framework for US private pensions, creating the embryonic IRA system.
Oct 8
Franklin National Bank declares bankruptcy
Assets of roughly $5 billion; the 12th-largest US bank — the largest bank failure in US history up to that point.
Nov 12
Nationwide coal-miners' strike begins
That month: Ford visited Japan on Nov 18; on Nov 20 the DOJ filed an antitrust suit against AT&T.
Dec 6
Fed discount rate cut from 8.0% to 7.75%
The formal turning point of the tightening cycle, marking the start of a new easing phase.
