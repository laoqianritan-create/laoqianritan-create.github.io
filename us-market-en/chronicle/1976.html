Overview

200th anniversary of US founding · Recovery and falling rates · Carter defeats Ford

1976 was a year of breathing space in the middle of the stagflation decade. After the consolidation that followed in H2 1975, the three core macro variables turned together: industrial production growth recovered from a low of more than -12% YoY in May 1975 back to positive growth by year-end; 3-month and 10-year Treasury yields and the prime rate all fell visibly from October 1975; full-year CPI was 5.8%, well off the 11% peak of 1974. The rally fired up at the start of the year: on January 16 the Fed cut the discount rate from 6.0% to 5.5%, by late January the Dow was up about 14%, and on March 11 it re-crossed 1,000 for the first time in three years.

From late April to early June, a rapid rebound in short rates raised tightening fears and produced the year's first correction. Around the July 4 bicentennial celebration the market made a second high, then weak retail data triggered a second pullback. After the Dow set a new high of 1,014 on September 21, the leading economic indicators print of -1.5% MoM for August raised fears of a slowdown and produced the deepest correction of the year, with the Dow falling to 924 by November 10, a drawdown of 8.9%.

On November 2, Democrat Jimmy Carter defeated incumbent President Ford, winning the 39th presidency by 297 to 240 electoral votes — the closest election since 1916. On November 19 the Fed cut the discount rate again to 5.25%, and OPEC's December price hike came in below expectations, reigniting a year-end rally. The Dow closed at 1,005, +17.9% for the year, with the S&P 500 +19.15%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 8
IMF Interim Committee reaches the Jamaica Agreement
Legalized floating exchange rates, abolished the official price of gold, completely demonetized gold from currency, and established a diversified international reserve system led by the dollar. Bretton Woods was formally laid to rest.
Jan 16
Fed discount rate cut from 6.0% to 5.5%
First cut of the year. Later in the month the prime rate was cut twice in a row, from 7.25% to 6.75%.
Jan 30
NYSE single-day volume hits 38.51 million shares
An all-time record in the exchange's 184-year history.
Apr 1
Apple Computer founded in Cupertino, California
Co-founded by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne; the first product, Apple I, launched the same year.
Mar 16
UK Prime Minister Wilson abruptly resigns
The sterling crisis flared up again. In early March the House of Commons rejected a public-spending cuts white paper; FX markets reacted sharply, and Wilson resigned citing exhaustion.
Jul 4
US Bicentennial celebrations
Nationwide festivities; New York Harbor hosted an international sailing review; President Ford presided over the ceremony at Independence Hall in Philadelphia.
Jul 17
Montreal Olympics open
The US delegation finished third in the medal table, behind the Soviet Union and East Germany.
Sep 28
August leading economic indicators -1.5% MoM
The first decline in 18 months, raising concerns about an economic slowdown.
Oct 4
Ford signs the Tax Reform Act
Expanded the investment tax credit, revised estate and gift taxes, and adjusted the alternative minimum tax — the most complex of the postwar tax overhauls.
Nov 2
Jimmy Carter elected 39th US president
The Democratic nominee and former governor of Georgia defeated incumbent President Ford by 297 to 240 electoral votes — the closest US election since 1916.
Nov 19
Fed discount rate cut from 5.5% to 5.25%
Second cut of the year. On December 17 the Fed further reduced member-bank reserve requirements.
Dec 16
OPEC reaches a two-tier price-hike agreement
Saudi Arabia and the UAE raised crude prices 5%, while other members raised 10% — both below Wall Street expectations.
