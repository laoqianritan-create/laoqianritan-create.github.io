Overview

Systemic compression of elevated valuations · Government-business clash overlaps with Cold War nuclear crisis · Dow -10.8% for the year

1962 was the mid-cycle correction year of the second postwar bull market. In January, the Kennedy administration proposed a $92.5 billion FY1963 budget to Congress, with substantial expansion of defense and space R&D spending; the "defense dollar" supported the market in the first two months as the index moved sideways. The Revenue Act of 1962 introduced a 7% investment tax credit, lowering the effective corporate tax rate from 41% to about 39%.

The turning point came in mid-March. First, the UN disarmament conference opened in Geneva on March 14, raising concerns over defense order cuts. Then on April 10, US Steel announced a price hike, prompting Kennedy's public pressure and a DOJ investigation; steel stocks plunged first, dragging the broader market. May-June produced the main decline phase, known as the "Kennedy Slide": the Dow fell from a January high of 724 to a low of 525, a roughly 27% decline. Prior leaders saw valuations compressed sharply — IBM fell from $607 to $300, Polaroid from $262 to $81. The root cause: by end-1961 US equity PE had reached a level not exceeded for 30 years afterward, and 1962 PE compression was a systemic reset of prior valuations.

On July 9 the Fed cut the securities margin requirement from 70% to 50%, and on August 13 Kennedy first mentioned tax cuts publicly, stabilizing sentiment. On October 22, Kennedy delivered a radio address disclosing Soviet nuclear missiles in Cuba and announcing a naval quarantine; the Cuban Missile Crisis pushed the world to the brink of war. On October 28 Khrushchev agreed to remove the missiles, and the market bottomed and rallied through year-end. The Dow closed at 652, down 10.8%; S&P 500 -11.81%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 11
Kennedy SOTU + $92.5B FY1963 Budget
Military buildup, nuclear deterrence, and expanded defense and space R&D spending — launching the "defense dollar" theme.
Mar 14
UN Disarmament Committee Opens Geneva Conference
Aimed at a comprehensive disarmament treaty; markets feared cuts to "defense dollar" spending and corporate orders.
Apr 10
US Steel Announces Price Hike · Kennedy Forcefully Intervenes
Chairman Roger Blough informed the White House of price hikes exceeding government guidance; Kennedy publicly rebuked the move, and Attorney General Robert Kennedy opened an investigation into US Steel. Markets saw it as an anti-big-business signal.
During Year
Revenue Act of 1962 · 7% Investment Tax Credit
New equipment investment qualified for a 7% tax credit, lowering effective corporate tax burden from about 41% to about 39%.
Jul 9
Fed Cuts Securities Margin Requirement: 70% → 50%
A substantial margin cut, the most important policy support action of H2 1962.
Aug 13
Kennedy First Publicly Proposes Tax Cuts
Proposed cuts to corporate and individual income taxes — a key catalyst for the 1963 rally.
Oct 18
Fed Cuts Time Deposit Reserve Requirement
To prevent early-onset recession, monetary policy eased; OECD members also endorsed US expansionary policy.
Oct 22
Cuban Missile Crisis Breaks Out
Kennedy addressed the nation and the world disclosing Soviet nuclear missiles in Cuba and announcing a naval quarantine. Oct 23: Soviet refusal of interception; Oct 24: US formally blockaded Cuban waters. The world stood at the brink of war.
Oct 28
Khrushchev Agrees to Withdraw Offensive Missiles from Cuba
The Cuban Missile Crisis was resolved peacefully. By Nov 11, all 42 Soviet missiles were withdrawn; on Nov 20, Kennedy lifted the naval quarantine.
Early Nov
UK Announces Major Auto Purchase Tax Cut
Concurrent easing by major developed economies; to prevent a global downturn, major economies coordinated toward looser policy.
