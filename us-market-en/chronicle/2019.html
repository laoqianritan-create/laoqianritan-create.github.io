2019
Chronicle · Late-Bull · Policy-Pivot Year

The Fed Restarts Rate Cuts

Overview

Powell pivots from hikes to cuts · Trade war flares and fades · Curve inversion as a recession warning

2019 saw the S&P 500 close at 3231, +28.88% on the year. The dominant theme was a 180-degree turn in monetary policy. Heading into the year the Fed was still hiking and projected more hikes for 2019; in January Powell's "patient" language retracted the hawkish stance; on Jul 31 the Fed restarted cuts. Three cuts on the year took fed funds from 2.25–2.5% down to 1.5–1.75%—the first Fed cuts since the 2008 financial crisis, re-anchoring valuations.

The second key thread was the US-China trade war. In May Trump raised tariffs on $200 billion of Chinese goods from 10% to 25% and China retaliated, producing meaningful pullbacks in May and August. On Aug 14 the US 10-year/2-year curve inverted for the first time since 2007, a classic recession signal that drove a sharp Dow decline. In December a Phase One trade deal was reached and tensions eased.

On the political front, the House launched an impeachment inquiry in September and passed articles on Dec 18; Hong Kong protests ran through the second half; Boeing's 737 MAX was grounded globally from March after two crashes. Market reactions were brief; liquidity from cuts and recovering earnings carried the index to new highs. At year-end Wuhan reported pneumonia cases of unknown origin, foreshadowing the upheaval to come.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 3
Apple guidance cut
Revenue guidance is cut, blamed on weaker iPhone demand in China—an early concrete signal of trade-war damage to corporate fundamentals.
Jan 10
Fed "pause" pivot
Powell retracts the hawkish stance with "patient" language—a major policy shift versus the December 2018 dot plot.
Jan 20
Democratic 2020 primary launches
Multiple Democratic candidates announce campaigns, formally opening the 2020 election cycle.
Mar 10
Boeing 737 MAX grounded globally
After the Ethiopian Airlines crash, countries ground the 737 MAX in succession; the US formally grounds it on May 10. Boeing enters a prolonged crisis.
May 5
Trump raises tariffs on Chinese goods from 10% to 25%
Covers $200 billion of Chinese goods; the trade war clearly escalates.
Jun 4
Powell hints at "appropriate-action" cut
In public remarks Powell says the Fed will "act as appropriate" to sustain the expansion; markets quickly price cuts.
Jul 31
Fed delivers first cut since 2008, 25 bp
Fed funds drops from 2.25–2.5% to 2.0–2.25%—the first cut since the 2008 financial crisis.
Aug 5
China announces retaliatory tariffs
The same day the RMB breaks 7 per USD; the US labels China a currency manipulator.
Aug 14
10-year/2-year Treasury yield curve inverts
First 10Y-2Y inversion since 2007—a classic recession warning.
Sep 18
Fed delivers second cut to 1.75–2.0%
Second 25 bp cut of the year.
Sep 24
House launches Trump impeachment inquiry
Pelosi announces a formal impeachment inquiry centered on the Ukraine call.
Oct 30
Fed delivers third cut to 1.5–1.75%
Third 25 bp cut of the year; rates then hold through year-end.
Nov
Hong Kong protests continue to escalate
Protests intensify in the second half; the US passes the Hong Kong Human Rights and Democracy Act.
Dec 13
US-China Phase One trade deal reached
Suspends some tariff escalations and removes the market's worst-case trade-war scenario.
Dec 18
House impeaches Trump
Passes articles for abuse of power and obstruction of Congress; Trump becomes the third president in US history to be impeached by the House.
Dec 31
Wuhan reports cases of pneumonia of unknown origin
The Wuhan Municipal Health Commission publicly reports cases of viral pneumonia of unknown cause—foreshadowing 2020's global pandemic.
