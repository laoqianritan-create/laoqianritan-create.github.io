Overview

Bretton Woods cracks · American social fracture · Twin pressure from currency and war

1968 was the year Bretton Woods began to unravel. Aftershocks of the 1967 sterling crisis turned into a global gold run: London gold trading volumes hit 20x normal in January, with France leading dollar-to-gold conversion. On March 15 the London gold market was closed and the London Gold Pool was dissolved; on March 17 central banks launched the two-tier gold system — maintaining $35.20/oz between central banks while letting the free market float; on March 18 the US repealed its 25% gold backing requirement for currency issuance.

Domestic America was another kind of strained. Vietnam de-escalated after the January 30 Tet Offensive; Johnson announced on March 31 he would not seek re-election; Martin Luther King Jr. was assassinated on April 4, Robert Kennedy on June 5; the Democratic National Convention erupted in Chicago riots in August — the most socially fractured postwar year. Nixon defeated Humphrey for the presidency on November 5.

Monetary policy oscillated through tax hikes, rate cuts, and rate hikes. Two hikes in March-April pushed the discount rate to 5.5%; on June 28 the Revenue and Expenditure Control Act imposed a 10% surtax. On August 15 the Fed cut the discount rate to 5.25% as a "technical" move, but with inflation rising, the 10-year Treasury yield surpassed the pre-cut peak by November; the Fed was forced to hike again to 5.5% on December 17. The Dow closed at 944, +4.3%; S&P 500 +7.66%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 1
Presidential Balance-of-Payments Program
The US government required banks and other financial institutions to limit foreign credit extension in response to deteriorating balance of payments.
Jan 30
Viet Cong Tet Offensive
Viet Cong forces broke the New Year truce, attacking over 100 cities in South Vietnam. Television coverage galvanized US domestic anti-war sentiment.
Mar 14
Fed Discount Rate: 4.5% → 5.0%
First rate hike of the year.
Mar 15
London Gold Market Closed; London Gold Pool Dissolved
The UK closed the London gold market. Within one month from January, London gold trading hit 20x normal volume; France led the exit from the London Gold Pool, with members unwilling to continue funding US Vietnam War and social-security spending.
Mar 17-18
Two-Tier Gold System Launched + US Gold Backing Repealed
On March 17 central banks announced the two-tier gold system: $35.20/oz official rate between central banks with free-market price floating. On March 18 the US repealed the 25% gold reserve requirement for currency issuance.
Mar 31
Johnson Address: Vietnam De-escalation, No Re-election Bid
Reduced bombing ahead of peace talks with Vietnam, declining to seek re-election and backing Vice President Humphrey. Hawkish Defense Secretary McNamara had been removed earlier.
Apr 4
Martin Luther King Jr. Assassinated in Memphis
The civil rights leader's assassination sparked riots in over 100 US cities.
Apr 18
Fed Discount Rate: 5.0% → 5.5%
Second rate hike of the year.
May
France's "May Storm" of 1968
Students and workers joined in a general strike, with over 200,000 protesters in Paris — one of the largest mass protests in postwar Europe.
Jun 7
Securities Margin Requirement: 70% → 80%
Fed tightened equity-market liquidity.
Jun 5
Robert Kennedy Assassinated in Los Angeles
JFK's brother and former Attorney General was assassinated the night he won the California Democratic primary, dying in the early morning. Johnson promptly backed Humphrey.
Jun 28
Revenue and Expenditure Control Act of 1968 Signed
Imposed a 10% surtax on individuals and corporations through June 30, 1969, and deferred auto and telecom excise tax reductions. FY1969 saw a fiscal surplus — the next would not come until Clinton in 1998.
Aug 15
Fed Discount Rate: 5.5% → 5.25%
The Fed called it a "technical" move offsetting the tax hike and spending controls. The real backdrop was that the economy and inflation were still rising.
Aug 20
Soviet Invasion of Czechoslovakia
Warsaw Pact forces entered Prague, crushing the Prague Spring. Cold War alliance fissures deepened.
Aug
Chicago Riots at Democratic National Convention
Anti-war protesters clashed with police for days on Chicago streets, televised nationwide, deepening voter alienation from Democratic governance.
Oct 11
Apollo 7 Launches
The first manned flight of the Apollo program, paving the way for the moon landing.
Nov 5
Nixon Defeats Humphrey for the Presidency
With Democrats damaged by Johnson's withdrawal, RFK's assassination, and the Chicago riots, Richard Nixon won the election comfortably.
Dec 17
Fed Discount Rate: 5.25% → 5.5%
Just four months after the August cut. With 10-year Treasury yields exceeding pre-cut highs by November, the Fed was forced into a full reversal.
