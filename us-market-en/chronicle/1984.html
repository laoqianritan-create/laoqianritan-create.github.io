1984
Chronicle · Final Year of Reagan's First Term

Fiscal Deficit Quagmire

S&P 500 +1.40% Dow Jones -3.7% Year-End 1212 Main Theme Deficits + High rates + Election year
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Robust GDP +7.2% · record federal deficit · wide range, flat finish in an election year

1984 real GDP grew 7.2% year over year — one of the strongest postwar recovery years. Yet the year's market storyline was not the boom itself but the fiscal deficit. Through Reagan's first term, deficits expanded from $79 billion in 1981 to $208 billion in 1983, with the FY1985 budget projecting $180 billion. The budget Reagan submitted to Congress in late January 1984 reignited fears of an unanchored long end, with 10-year Treasury yields briefly approaching 14%.

In early January, the S&P 500 took just three sessions to hit a year-high of 169 before turning lower, falling nearly 9% by late February. Inflation rebounded in March–April, the bank prime rate was raised twice, and on April 6 the Fed raised the discount rate to 9%. In May, Continental Illinois Bank suffered a run; on May 17, FDIC implemented emergency rescue — the largest FDIC intervention in history and the moment "too big to fail" first took shape as a concept. Equities continued declining through late July, with the S&P 500 troughing at 147 — 13% off the early-year high.

On July 25, Volcker's congressional testimony signaled tightening would not intensify further; the market rebounded quickly. Combined with Reagan's landslide reelection on November 6 carrying 49 states, the index touched the year-high of 170 that day. November saw the Deficit Reduction Act of 1984 signed and the Fed cut rates twice to 8% (November 21 and December 21). Markets oscillated around the tax-reform proposals, closing essentially flat after a wide-range year.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 24
Apple launches the Macintosh
The first graphical-interface personal computer aimed at the mass market. Marketed by the iconic "1984" Super Bowl ad on January 22.
Late Jan
Reagan submits FY1985 budget to Congress
$180 billion budget deficit, with FY1986/1987 projected at $177B/$180B. Markets feared rising long-end yields.
Mid-Mar
Reagan proposes 3-year, $150 billion deficit reduction plan
Included $57 billion in military spending cuts plus tax increases. Market sentiment toward tax hikes reversed, with hikes now seen as positive.
Apr 6
Fed raises discount rate to 9.0%
Followed two bank prime rate hikes in March and April, reinforcing expectations of further tightening.
May 17
FDIC emergency rescue of Continental Illinois Bank
The seventh-largest US bank and Chicago's largest suffered a run; FDIC injected emergency financial aid, followed by sustained rescue on September 26. The largest FDIC bank rescue in history — and the moment "too big to fail" was formalized as a concept.
Early May
Treasury issues $16.5 billion of debt
The large-scale issuance reawakened market concerns about deficits and rates.
Jul 25
Volcker congressional testimony
The Fed Chair said "tighter control over money and credit is not appropriate," signaling no further tightening.
Jul 28 – Aug 12
Los Angeles Summer Olympics
Hosted by the US; boycotted by the USSR and Eastern Bloc nations. The first Olympics entirely funded by commercial operations.
Oct 17
Bank prime rate cut to 12.5%
Cut from 12.75%, alongside UK, Norway, and Nigeria cutting crude oil prices; OPEC convened an emergency meeting to respond.
Nov 6
Reagan reelected as President
A landslide victory carrying 49 of 50 states, with VP George H.W. Bush continuing as running mate.
Nov 21
Fed cuts rates 9.0% → 8.5%
First rate cut since December 1982. The Deficit Reduction Act of 1984 was signed the same month.
Nov 27
Treasury releases tax reform proposal
Tax bracket structure substantially simplified, individual rates reduced, corporate rates raised. Treasury estimated individual taxes paid would fall 8.5% while corporate taxes rise 25%, with total revenue unchanged.
Dec 21
Fed cuts rates again 8.5% → 8.0%
Second cut of the year.
