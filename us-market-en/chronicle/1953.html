Overview

Optimistic start · Monetary tightening lifts rates · Equities lead the bottom amid recession

1953 opened with markedly optimistic sentiment. Truman's annual economic report on January 14 described the year as one of "unprecedented prosperity"; Eisenhower took office as the 34th president on January 20, and as a Republican was expected to reduce government intervention, control spending, and push tax cuts. In early February he lifted nearly all wage and many price controls, and on February 20 the Fed cut the stock margin requirement from 75% to 50%. But equities didn't buy the story: the Dow was roughly flat from January to March, and then fell 9.6% from late March to mid-June.

Two main forces drove the decline: first, continued monetary tightening — the Fed raised the discount rate from 1.75% to 2.0% on January 16, and by June both Treasury and commercial paper yields hit record highs; second, commodity prices kept falling from 1952 levels, squeezing corporate earnings. With no inflation pressure visible in PPI, CPI, or commodity indices at the time, the 1953 tightening was later widely viewed as a policy mistake and a key trigger of the 1953–1954 recession.

The Fed cut reserve requirements on June 24, with equities stabilizing from mid-June; the Korean War Armistice was signed at Panmunjom on July 27, sparking a sharp pulse rally. Markets fell again from August on news that Eisenhower would not raise the $27.5 billion statutory debt ceiling. NBER dated the recession to July 1953, but equities bottomed in mid-September and recovered through Q4. The Dow closed at 281, down 3.8%; the S&P 500 ended -6.62%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 14
Truman Annual Economic Report Forecasts "Unprecedented Prosperity"
High employment and price stability combined; both government and markets were optimistic about 1953.
Jan 16
Fed Discount Rate: 1.75% → 2.0%
First tightening of the year. By June, both Treasury and commercial paper yields had hit record highs.
Jan 20
Eisenhower Inaugurated as 34th President
As a Republican, markets expected him to reduce government intervention, control spending, and pursue tax cuts to lift corporate profits.
Early Feb
All Wage Controls and Many Price Controls Lifted
Eisenhower partially delivered on liberalization expectations after taking office.
Feb 20
Stock Margin Requirement: 75% → 50%
The Fed rolled back the 75% requirement raised on January 17, 1951.
Mar 5
Soviet Leader Stalin Dies
A major variable in the international order. Power transition within the Cold War camp.
Jun 24
Fed Cuts Reserve Requirements
Large commercial banks cut 2%, city and country banks cut 1%. Monetary policy turned toward easing.
Jul 27
Korean Armistice Signed at Panmunjom
After two years of negotiations a final agreement was reached on July 26; chief representatives formally signed on July 27.
Jul
NBER: US Economy Officially Enters Recession
The cycle peak later dated by NBER. The 1953–1954 recession began here.
Mid-Sep
Government Announces Defense Stimulus and Tax Cuts
Eisenhower proposed a major defense stimulus program; Treasury Secretary Humphrey said excess-profits tax on corporations would be terminated by year-end and individual income taxes lowered.
