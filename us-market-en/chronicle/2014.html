Overview

Yellen takes over · QE officially ends · Oil halves · Dollar dominance

2014 didn't open smoothly. The December 2013 Taper, EM currency depreciation, and severe North American winter weather depressed PMI, and the S&P 500 was down 4% in early February. On Feb 3 Janet Yellen became Fed Chair, the first woman to lead the Fed in its century-long history; her Feb 11 congressional debut promised policy continuity, reassuring the market.

The index turned higher in mid-February and made repeated highs from May through July. Unemployment returned to pre-crisis levels, Q2 earnings beat handily, the ECB launched negative rates in June, and the US economic-surprise index bottomed in early April and rebounded. Three later pullbacks (early August on a hawkish Fed, September–October on European weakness and Ebola, December on the oil crash and the ruble crisis) were each quickly reabsorbed by strong fundamentals. On Oct 29 the Fed officially ended QE, with the balance sheet at $4.5 trillion. WTI crude fell from $107 in June to $53 by year-end—roughly halved.

The S&P 500 finished +11.39%, hitting new highs in the sixth year of the bull. Capital flowing into the US, a surging dollar index, Treasury yields paradoxically falling, and the lone-wolf strength of US equities—the seemingly contradictory signals all pointed to the same clearest theme of 2014: America.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Feb 3
Janet Yellen sworn in as Fed Chair
The first woman to lead the Fed in its century history. Her Feb 11 congressional debut promises policy continuity.
Mar 18
Russia annexes Crimea
Putin signs the treaty admitting Crimea to the Russian Federation; the EU and US impose sanctions; the Ukraine crisis officially begins.
Feb 28
Bitcoin exchange Mt. Gox files for bankruptcy
The largest bitcoin exchange at the time loses around 850,000 BTC—the first major trust crisis of the crypto world.
Jun 5
ECB cuts to negative rates
The deposit rate is cut to -0.1%—the first major Western central bank to deploy negative-rate policy.
Jun 13
ISIS captures Mosul in northern Iraq
Iraq's second-largest city falls; ISIS reaches its peak territorial extent and Middle East risk surges.
Late Jun
WTI crude tops $107 and starts to crash
A shale-driven supply surplus and OPEC's decision not to cut take crude to $53 by year-end—a 50% drop in six months.
Aug 6
Fed continues to taper purchases
Sixth cut to monthly QE3 purchases, from $35 billion to $25 billion; the Jul 30 minutes are on the hawkish side.
Sep 19
Alibaba IPOs on NYSE, raising $25 billion
The largest IPO in history; opens up 36% on day one and briefly tops $230 billion in market cap.
Sep 26
Hong Kong's Occupy Central movement
Street protests over the 2017 chief-executive electoral framework last 79 days, cleared in mid-December.
Sep 30
First Ebola case detected in the US
The first confirmed case outside Africa, combined with weakening European PMI and accelerating oil declines, deepens the October pullback.
Oct 29
Fed formally ends quantitative easing
Monthly QE3 purchases hit zero; the Fed balance sheet has grown from $900 billion to $4.5 trillion, ending six years of post-crisis easing.
Nov 4
US midterms: Republicans take the Senate
The last two years of Obama's second term begin under divided government, with Republicans controlling both chambers.
Dec 17
US and Cuba normalize relations
Obama and Raúl Castro simultaneously announce a restoration of diplomatic relations, ending 53 years of hostility.
Dec 16
Russian ruble crisis
Crashing oil and Western sanctions push the ruble down 11% in a day; the central bank hikes rates 650 bp to 17% on an emergency basis.
Dec
Dollar index surges into year-end
The DXY rises roughly 13% on the year as capital flows into dollar assets—a mirror image of oil and EM currencies.
