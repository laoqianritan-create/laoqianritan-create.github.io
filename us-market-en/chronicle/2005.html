2005
Chronicle · Mid-Cycle Recovery · Hike Marathon

Eight Rate Hikes

S&P 500 +3.00% Dow Jones -0.6% Year-End 10718 Main Theme 8 Hikes + Oil + Katrina
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Fed funds rate 2.25% → 4.25% · Hurricane Katrina · RMB exchange-rate reform

2005 was the mid-point of the post-dot-com recovery. The macro storyline was eight Fed hikes through the year, lifting fed funds from 2.25% at the start to 4.25% at year-end—200 basis points in total. At the same time, oil rose from roughly $40/bbl, breaking out twice through prior highs and pressuring both the economy and corporate profits.

Markets traced two down-legs and two up-legs. From January through late April, a record trade deficit, a big payroll miss, the AIG accounting scandal, and a $1.1 billion Q1 loss at GM took the S&P 500 down about 7% by Apr 20. Late April through early August saw the NYSE-Archipelago merger, contained inflation, and bidding interest in GM combine to push the index nearly 10% higher. From August on, new oil highs and rate-hike expectations brought another pullback.

The second half delivered concentrated political and natural shocks: on Aug 29 Hurricane Katrina hit New Orleans, killing more than 1,800 and causing over $125 billion of damage. On Jul 21 China's RMB reform ended the dollar peg. On Oct 24 Bush nominated Bernanke to succeed Greenspan. Markets stabilized in late October and finished with a year-end rally. The S&P 500 closed +3.0%, the Dow -0.6%, and the Nasdaq +1.4%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
Bush inaugurated for a second term
Bush is sworn in for his second term at the Capitol, delivering his "Freedom Agenda" address.
Jan 30
Iraq holds first post-war free election
Iraq's first multi-party election in half a century, selecting a 275-seat transitional national assembly.
Feb 1
Fed hikes 25 bp to 2.5%
First hike of 2005, opening this year's eight-hike sequence.
Mar 22
Fed hikes 25 bp to 2.75%
Statement highlights "rising price pressures" for the first time, read by the market as a hawkish shift.
Apr
2005 Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention and Consumer Protection Act signed
Bush signs the law, sharply tightening personal-bankruptcy thresholds—seen as a major win for the credit-card and mortgage industries.
May 3
Fed hikes 25 bp to 3.0%
The "measured" language remains in the statement, signaling an unchanged pace.
Jun 30
Fed hikes 25 bp to 3.25%
Fourth hike of the year; the discount rate is also lifted.
Jul 7
London transit bombings
Homegrown extremists linked to al-Qaeda detonate four suicide bombs on the London Underground and a bus during morning rush hour, killing 52.
Jul 21
China's RMB reform: end of the single-dollar peg
The PBOC announces that the RMB will no longer be pegged solely to the US dollar, moving to a managed float against a basket of currencies; the RMB appreciates 2.1% on the day. The change reshapes global trade and capital flows.
Aug 9
Fed hikes 25 bp to 3.5%
Fifth hike of the year.
Aug 29
Hurricane Katrina makes landfall in New Orleans
A Category-5 storm hits Louisiana; New Orleans's levees fail and 80% of the city is flooded. More than 1,800 die and damage exceeds $125 billion—one of the worst natural disasters in US history. WTI crude jumps to $70.8 on the day.
Sep 20
Fed hikes 25 bp to 3.75%
The Fed proceeds as planned despite Katrina, signaling that inflation takes priority over disaster response.
Oct 24
Bernanke nominated as next Fed Chair
Bush nominates Council of Economic Advisers chair Ben Bernanke to succeed Greenspan, whose term ends in early 2006.
Nov 1
Fed hikes 25 bp to 4.0%
Seventh hike of the year.
Nov
US home prices approach historic peak
The Case-Shiller National Home Price Index reaches its 2H 2005 peak range, laying the asset-side groundwork for the subprime crisis.
Dec 7
Chevron completes its acquisition of Unocal
CNOOC's premium bid for Unocal had been blocked by the US Congress on national-security grounds; Chevron ultimately closes the deal—the first time a major Chinese cross-border M&A runs into a political wall.
Dec 13
Fed hikes 25 bp to 4.25%
Eighth and final hike of the year; fed funds has doubled from year-start.
Dec 19
Fifth round of Six-Party Talks on North Korea
China, the US, Russia, Japan, South Korea, and North Korea meet in Beijing on North Korea's nuclear program, setting the stage for the DPRK's first nuclear test the following year.
