Overview

Postwar expansion continues · Korean War rewrites expectations · Tax hikes and rate hikes in tandem

1950 extended the uptrend that began in the second half of 1949. Three macro factors supported equities early in the year: US industrial production growth turned positive year-over-year and continued to accelerate, inflation remained low, and markets broadly expected postwar tax cuts. The Dow climbed steadily from about 199 in early January, peaking near 228 in early June for a roughly 15% half-year gain.

The turning point came on June 25 — the Korean War broke out, triggering a sharp sell-off; by July 13 the Dow had fallen to 197.4, erasing the entire year-to-date gain. The second half was rewritten by three forces: wartime mobilization, monetary tightening to fight inflation, and further acceleration in the economy. In September the Defense Production Act authorized the government to control prices, wages, and consumer credit; the Revenue Act of 1950, signed on September 23, raised the top corporate income tax bracket from 38% to 45%, turning tax-cut hopes into a tax-hike reality overnight; in December Truman declared a national emergency.

Yet price controls, tax hikes, and tight money failed to hold back equities because the real economy was too strong — industrial production broke its June 1944 wartime peak in August, with year-over-year growth approaching 30% by October. After bottoming in July, the Dow stepped progressively higher, retook the June peak, and closed the year at 235.4, with the Dow +17.6% and the S&P 500 +21.78%.

US Industrial Production YoY Growth Turns Positive
After rapidly recovering from H2 1949, the index officially turned positive in February 1950 and continued sharply higher.
Jun 25
Korean War Breaks Out
North Korean forces crossed the 38th parallel; the US intervened immediately and the UN Security Council authorized military support for South Korea.
Aug 18
Fed Discount Rate: 1.5% → 1.75%
With wartime inflation accelerating, a tightening cycle began.
Aug
Industrial Production Breaks June 1944 Wartime Peak
The real economy accelerated to a new all-time high.
Sep 8
Congress Passes the Defense Production Act
Established defense production priorities, materials and facility allocation, and authorized the President to control prices, wages, and consumer credit.
Sep 9
Fed Tightens Housing Credit Policy
Nine days later, consumer installment credit was also tightened.
Sep 23
Revenue Act of 1950 Enacted
Eliminated certain individual deductions and raised the top corporate income tax bracket from 38% to 45%.
Oct
Industrial Production YoY Growth Nears 30%
Despite tax hikes and tight money, the economy kept accelerating and corporate profits surged.
Dec 16
Truman Declares National Emergency
The highest level of wartime mobilization by year-end.
Dec 29
Demand Deposit Reserves +2%, Time Deposit Reserves +1%
The fourth tightening step in H2 — rate hike, housing credit, consumer credit, and reserve requirements — completed a full tightening cycle within one year.
