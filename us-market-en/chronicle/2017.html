Overview

A near-perfect year · Tax-cut expectations run through the year · Earnings and multiples rise together

2017 was a near-perfect year in the late stage of the decade-long slow bull. The market was broadly anxious about Trump's inauguration at the start of the year, but tax-cut and deregulation expectations quickly took over pricing: Q1 PMI hit a high since 2015 and the S&P 500 rose nearly 5% in Q1, with an even steep slope on a full-year view. The signing of the Brexit law on Mar 16, Moody's downgrade of China on May 24, continued North Korean nuclear threats, and Trump's May 9 firing of FBI Director Comey produced only minor pullbacks, quickly absorbed by strong Q1 earnings—the first broad Wall Street upward revision of full-year EPS since 2012.

The Fed hiked 25 bp in March and June and announced a year-end QT start at the June meeting. Three rounds of QE since 2008 had taken the Fed balance sheet from $1 trillion to $4.5 trillion; the formal start of QT in October was a key turning-point signal of this long bull. But low rates and earnings beats (especially in banks) left the market unfazed. From late August into September, hurricanes Harvey, Irma, and Maria caused huge damage; markets focused more on the ISM Manufacturing Index hitting a high since 2004 and rising long-end rates.

In December the Fed delivered its third hike of the year to 1.25–1.5%; on Dec 22 Trump signed the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA)—the largest tax overhaul since Reagan, cutting the corporate rate from 35% to 21%. The S&P 500 closed the year having gone 67 straight weeks without a weekly pullback of 2% or more, the longest such streak since 1965. Apple, Amazon, Microsoft, Facebook, and Alphabet hit fresh highs in succession, and the S&P Information Technology index broke through its dot-com peak.

Timeline of Events

Jan 20
Trump sworn in as 45th US President
A campaign centered on tax cuts, deregulation, and infrastructure; capital markets remain uncertain about the policy path.
Jan 23
Trump signs executive order withdrawing from TPP
The first major trade-policy action of the new administration; "America First" trade policy is launched.
Mar 15
Fed hikes 25 bp to 0.75–1.0%
First hike of the year. The UK Queen approves the Brexit law on Mar 16.
May 9
Trump fires FBI Director James Comey
Firing the FBI Director investigating "Russiagate" sparks a constitutional debate.
May 17
DOJ appoints Robert Mueller as Special Counsel
An independent investigation of Russian interference in 2016 and potential Trump-team collusion.
Jun 1
Trump announces US withdrawal from the Paris Agreement
The US becomes the only major economy outside the agreement; the climate and clean-energy policy cycle is reset.
Jun 14
Fed hikes 25 bp and previews year-end QT
Fed funds target moves to 1.0–1.25%; the FOMC publishes a QT roadmap.
Aug 12
White-nationalist rally clashes in Charlottesville, Virginia
The "Unite the Right" rally turns violent; Trump's "both sides" response triggers a national political storm.
Aug–Sep
Hurricanes Harvey, Irma, and Maria hit in succession
Harvey devastates Houston, Irma sweeps across Florida, and Maria destroys Puerto Rico's grid; total economic losses exceed $300 billion.
Oct
Fed begins quantitative tightening (QT)
The first active balance-sheet contraction after three rounds of QE, starting from $4.5 trillion. Long viewed as one of this bull cycle's most important policy turning points.
Oct 1
Mass shooting at Las Vegas outdoor music festival
58 dead and more than 500 injured—the deadliest single-shooter mass shooting in modern US history.
Dec 6
Trump recognizes Jerusalem as Israel's capital
Breaks decades of US Middle East policy; announces the relocation of the US embassy to Jerusalem.
Dec 13
Fed hikes 25 bp to 1.25–1.5%
Third hike of the year and Yellen's last FOMC.
Dec 22
Trump signs the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA)
The largest tax overhaul since Reagan: corporate income tax drops from 35% to 21%, individual rates are cut broadly, and a one-time repatriation rate is set on overseas earnings.
Dec
Bitcoin nears $20,000; ICO mania
Crypto and ICO funding explodes through the year; bitcoin briefly approaches an all-time high of $20,000 in December.
