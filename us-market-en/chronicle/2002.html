Overview

Fundamentals recovering, confidence collapsing · A wave of accounting scandals · Emergency legislative repair

2002 had a split macro narrative. On one side, economic data reversed off the recession lows—PMI crossed the expansion threshold in February, and retail and corporate earnings bottomed and turned higher in Q1. On the other, trust in the foundations of the capital markets continued to collapse. With Enron's aftershocks still spreading, WorldCom, Tyco, and Andersen blew up in succession, repeatedly puncturing investor confidence in listed-company financials.

Three down-legs over the year shared a single thread. From January through Feb 21, the unfolding Enron story and Argentina's abandonment of its currency peg pulled indices down nearly 6%. On Mar 20, PIMCO publicly questioned GE, and WorldCom entered the SEC's sights, igniting the second and deepest leg down—a 31% cumulative drop into Jul 23 that returned the S&P to 1997 levels. On Jul 21 WorldCom filed for bankruptcy protection, the largest corporate bankruptcy in US history at the time. On Jul 30 the Sarbanes-Oxley Act was signed into law, widely viewed as the most important securities legislation since the 1933 and 1934 Acts. From Aug 20 to Oct 7, PMI rolled over again and the index slid to its low for the year.

From October on, Q3 earnings beat expectations, with IBM, Citi, and Microsoft leading. On Nov 6 the Fed cut 50 bp in a single move, taking fed funds from 1.75% straight to 1.25%; year-end rallied roughly 12%. The S&P closed near 880, with all three majors down for a third straight year and the year's peak-to-trough drawdown from the 2000 high reaching -49%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 29
Bush State of the Union introduces the "Axis of Evil"
Labels Iran, Iraq, and North Korea as state sponsors of terror. The starting point of the Iraq war narrative.
Jan
Enron delisted from the Dow · DOJ opens criminal probe
On Jan 15 NYSE removes Enron from the Dow and halts trading; on Jan 19 the Justice Department opens a criminal probe of Enron's accounting fraud; auditor Arthur Andersen is indicted in parallel.
Feb 14
Sarbanes-Oxley Act submitted to House Financial Services Committee
A sweeping overhaul of the 1933 Securities Act and 1934 Exchange Act, and the government's rapid legislative response to Enron.
Mar 20
PIMCO publicly questions GE
Bill Gross accuses GE of sustaining growth through acquisitions and heavy debt. WorldCom enters the SEC's sights, igniting the second wave of accounting scandals.
Jun 25
WorldCom admits to $3.8 billion accounting fraud
Under SEC pressure WorldCom discloses that at least $3.8 billion of expenses had been booked to inflate cash flow and profit; $1.4 billion of 2001 and $130 million of Q1 2002 profits were fabricated.
Jul 21
WorldCom files for bankruptcy protection
Files in the Southern District of New York, becoming the largest US corporate bankruptcy on record at the time.
Jul 30
Sarbanes-Oxley Act signed into law
Passed by both chambers with overwhelming majorities and signed by Bush. New rules on corporate governance, accounting-profession oversight, and securities-market regulation mark a shift in US securities law from "disclosure" toward "substantive regulation".
Aug
Tyco International scandal escalates
Conglomerate Tyco is accused of overstating 1996–2002 results by at least $1 billion; former CEO Dennis Kozlowski and an aide are charged with looting $600 million from the company.
Aug 31
Arthur Andersen abandons its US audit practice
Arthur Andersen's US arm formally exits the audit business after 89 years. The Big Five become the Big Four.
Sep 10–11
First anniversary of 9/11
Memorials nationwide; Bush calls for action on Iraq at the UN General Assembly.
Oct 3
Congress authorizes the use of force against Iraq
Both chambers pass the Authorization for Use of Military Force Against Iraq Resolution, clearing the legal path for the 2003 war.
Nov 6
Fed cuts 50 bp in a single move
Fed funds target dropped from 1.75% straight to 1.25%, addressing the tail end of recession and the crisis of market confidence. Rates fall to a 40-year low.
Nov 5
Republicans rebound in midterm elections
Republicans win both chambers, an unusual midterm victory for a sitting president's party, cementing the Bush administration's congressional base.
Nov 25
Department of Homeland Security formally established
Consolidation of 22 federal agencies into the Department of Homeland Security (DHS)—the largest federal reorganization since the creation of the Department of Defense in 1947.
