Overview

Trump's second inauguration · Tariffs 2.0 and the "Liberation Day" shock · AI capex cycle · Three rate cuts

2025 was the opening year of Trump's second term. He signed multiple executive orders on Jan 20, including withdrawals from the WHO and the Paris Agreement. In late January China's DeepSeek R1 was released, prompting a short-term repricing of the US AI valuation chain. From February, DOGE drove large-scale federal-agency layoffs, making fiscal and public-sector restructuring a year-long policy theme.

Tariffs were the year's most systemic policy variable. On Feb 1, 25% tariffs were announced on Canada and Mexico and 10% on China; markets pulled back when these took effect on Mar 4. On Apr 2 "Liberation Day tariffs" added a 10% baseline tariff on the world plus extra layers on China; on Apr 4 the S&P 500 fell 6% in a single day and the drawdown accelerated into Apr 8. On Apr 9 Trump paused some tariffs for 90 days, opening a negotiating window. In May Moody's downgraded the US from AAA to Aa1, ending the last of the three majors' top rating.

On Jun 13 and 22, Israel and the US successively struck Iranian nuclear facilities, briefly amplifying geopolitical risk. In July Trump signed the omnibus "Big Beautiful Bill". The Fed cut 25 bp in September, October, and December, taking the corridor to 3.5–3.75% by year-end. In November Nvidia briefly crossed $4 trillion in market cap, and industry AI capex topped $400 billion on the year, providing the index-level valuation support. A December government-shutdown crisis carried through year-end. The S&P 500 finished +16.39%, closing the year on the twin tracks of tariff shocks and the AI capex cycle.
