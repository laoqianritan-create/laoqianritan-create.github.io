Overview

Truman's "Fair Deal" launches · Choppy first half · Equity bottom leads economic trough from June

On January 5, 1949, President Truman unveiled the "Fair Deal" platform, an expansion and continuation of FDR's New Deal that expanded government intervention in economic life and implied higher fiscal spending. Equities rallied briefly on the announcement but were soon dragged down by falling commodity prices, with the Dow troughing at 171 in February.

Starting in March, monetary policy eased repeatedly: the Fed loosened consumer installment credit, cut the securities margin requirement from 75% to 50%, and lowered bank reserve requirements multiple times. But from May the industrial production index dropped sharply, and the market belatedly registered recession (NBER would later date the recession back to November 1948), with the Dow falling from 177 in early May to 160 by mid-June.

Commodity prices bottomed in mid-June and turned higher, with the equity bottom leading the economic trough, opening a sustained uptrend with little pullback. September brought the Housing Act of 1949, sterling's 30.5% devaluation, and the Soviet atomic bomb test. From October the US economy emerged from recession amid low inflation, and equities closed the year at their highs. The Dow ended at 200, up 12.9%; the S&P 500 returned +10.26%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 5
Truman Unveils the "Fair Deal" Platform
An expansion and continuation of the New Deal, covering employment law, healthcare, social security, minimum wage, and public housing.
Mar 2
Fed Eases Consumer Installment Loan Restrictions
Maximum loan term raised from 15–18 to 21 months; minimum down payment on non-auto consumer goods lowered from 20% to 15%.
Mar 28
Securities Margin Requirement: 75% → 50%
A key step in monetary easing.
Apr 4
12 Nations Sign the North Atlantic Treaty
The NATO alliance was established, formally taking effect on August 24 after ratification. The Western half of the Cold War order took shape.
Apr 22
Fed Further Eases Consumer Credit
Minimum down payment on non-auto goods cut to 10%; maximum loan term extended to 24 months.
Apr 28
Fed Cuts Commercial Bank Reserve Requirement
Monetary policy continued to ease.
May 12
Berlin Blockade Lifted
The first major Cold War standoff came to an end.
Jun 29
Fed Cuts Reserve Requirement Again
From July, data in the two pillar industries — steel and autos — began to improve.
Aug 5
Another Reserve Cut as Tax-Cut Talks Heat Up
From mid-August, debate intensified over abolishing double taxation of dividends, lowering top income tax rates, and expanding tax credits.
Sep 15
Housing Act of 1949 Enacted
A major piece of "Fair Deal" legislation, marking the start of postwar urban renewal: slum clearance, public housing construction, and public-private development models.
Sep 18
Sterling Devalued 30.5%, Others Follow
Sterling fell from $4.03 to $2.80, with 24 nations across the sterling area plus France, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Canada following suit.
Sep 23
Soviet Union Successfully Tests Atomic Bomb
The end of the US nuclear monopoly opened the Cold War nuclear arms race.
