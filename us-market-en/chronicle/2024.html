The Easing Cycle Begins

Overview

AI leaders carry the flag · September 50 bp opens the easing cycle · Trump returns in November

2024 was the "soft landing delivered" year of the post-pandemic cycle. US inflation continued to drift down from a 9% peak in 2022 toward 2.5–3% by year-end, the labor market cooled gradually without breaking, and full-year real GDP held at a steady 2%+. The macro storyline rotated from "one more hike?" to "when do cuts start and how big". Early in the year the market repeatedly wobbled on rate-cut pricing, compressing from initial expectations of six-plus cuts down to just two or three by the second half.

On Jan 10 the SEC formally approved spot bitcoin ETFs, with 11 products listing the next day and opening an institutional channel into crypto. From February, AI-compute leaders like Nvidia continued to beat; on Jun 18 Nvidia briefly became the most valuable company in the world. In mid-July a style rotation lifted small caps briefly; on Aug 5 the Japan carry-trade unwind sent the Nikkei down 12.4% in a day, with the S&P down 3% and the VIX spiking to 65. The market quickly repaired in late August. On Sep 18 the Fed cut 50 bp, its first cut since March 2020, formally opening this easing cycle; further 25 bp cuts in November and December left fed funds at 4.25–4.5% at year-end.

On the political side, Trump survived an assassination attempt in Pennsylvania on Jul 13; on Jul 21 Biden exited the race; on Nov 5 Trump was re-elected and brought broad Republican governance into view. The AI theme and the "Trump trade" jointly drove the index higher through November. The S&P 500 closed at 5882, +23.31%, posting a second straight year of 20%+ gains—rare since the late 1990s.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 10
SEC approves spot bitcoin ETFs
The SEC approves 11 spot bitcoin ETFs at once; they list the next day (Jan 11) on NYSE, Nasdaq, and Cboe, opening an institutional channel into crypto.
Jan 15
Red Sea crisis escalates
Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping continue; major liner companies reroute around the Cape of Good Hope, materially raising global shipping costs.
Feb 21
Nvidia beats Q4 and crosses $2 trillion in market cap
Data-center revenue grows more than 4x year-on-year, reaffirming AI-compute demand; the company crosses $2 trillion for the first time.
Mar 6
US House passes TikTok forced-divestment bill
Requires ByteDance to divest TikTok's US business by a set deadline or be banned in the US; moves to the Senate.
Mar 20
Bank of Japan returns to positive rates after 17 years
The BoJ raises the policy rate from -0.1% to 0–0.1%, formally exiting negative rates and ending the YCC framework.
Apr 13
Iran launches retaliatory strike on Israel
Iran fires more than 300 missiles and drones at Israel—its first direct military strike on Israel since the 1979 Islamic Revolution; Middle East geopolitical risk spikes.
May 15
Biden announces escalated China tariffs
Tariffs on Chinese EVs raised to 100%, semis to 50%, EV batteries to 25%, solar products to 50%—covering roughly $18 billion of goods.
Mid-May
OpenAI releases GPT-4o
A natively multimodal model with end-to-end text, voice, and image processing—pushing market expectations for AI compute even higher.
Jun 18
Nvidia briefly the most valuable company in the world
Nvidia's market cap rises to $3.34 trillion, briefly surpassing Apple and Microsoft to become the most valuable listed company—the AI-compute theme reaches a stage peak.
Jul 13
Trump shot at a Pennsylvania rally
Trump is shot at a Butler, PA, rally; the bullet grazes his ear and he survives. The event materially shifts the election narrative.
Jul 21
Biden withdraws from the presidential race
Biden abandons his re-election bid and endorses VP Harris as the Democratic nominee.
Aug 5
JPY carry trade unwinds; Nikkei down 12.4% in a day
A BoJ hike combined with weakening US jobs triggers a concentrated unwind of the JPY carry trade; the Nikkei 225 falls 12.4% in a day, global risk assets sell off, and the VIX spikes to 65.
Sep 13
Nord Stream investigation findings emerge
Various investigations release forensic detail showing the Nord Stream 1 and 2 pipelines were sabotaged; the case begins to point toward identified actors.
Sep 18
Fed cuts 50 bp, opening the easing cycle
Fed funds target moves from 5.25–5.5% to 4.75–5.0%—the first cut since March 2020 and a direct 50 bp move.
Nov 5
Trump wins the presidency, returning to the White House
Republicans also win both chambers, producing a "red sweep"; markets price in tax cuts, deregulation, and a new tariff cycle.
Nov 7
Fed cuts another 25 bp
Fed funds target drops to 4.5–4.75%.
Dec 18
Fed cuts 25 bp but the dot plot turns hawkish
Policy moves to 4.25–4.5%, but the 2025 dot plot narrows from four to two cuts; Powell's tone is hawkish.
Dec
South Korean president briefly declares martial law, triggering a political crisis
Yoon Suk-yeol declares martial law late at night; the National Assembly overturns it within hours, leading to impeachment proceedings; the won and Korean equities swing sharply.
