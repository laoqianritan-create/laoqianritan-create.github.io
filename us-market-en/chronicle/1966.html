Overview

Vietnam spending lifts inflation · Monetary tightening + credit crunch · Dow tags 1001 early then rolls over

1966 was the inflection point in the 1958-1967 expansion. The economy approached full employment, leaving little room for further unemployment declines; meanwhile, Vietnam War spending rose rapidly, CPI accelerated to 2.9%, and inflation pressure became systemic. In mid-January Johnson requested $12.8 billion in additional Vietnam funding from Congress; in late February Goodyear raised tire prices citing "anticipatory costs"; 10-year Treasury yields broke through 5%, surpassing previous record highs.

The Fed tightened in parallel. On June 24 the discount rate was raised from 4.0% to 5.0%, and reserve requirements were raised again on August 17. From June through August interbank lending grew tight, and the term "credit crunch" formally entered the financial lexicon — markets focused on how much further credit could tighten and how high rates could go. Combined with airline and dock-worker strikes and defense spending crowding out private investment, the Dow fell from a January peak of 1001 to an October low of 736.

Policy began reversing in mid-September: the BIS "Group of Ten" expanded mutual credit lines to address potential dollar and sterling crises; Congress authorized the Fed broader powers over deposit rate ceilings; on September 21 the Fed cut the deposit rate ceiling. The November midterm elections saw Republicans gain 47 House seats, reflecting a conservative shift. The Fed cut reserve requirements on December 6. The Dow closed at 786, down 18.9%; S&P 500 -13.09%. Gold reserves fell to $13.16 billion.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Mid-Jan
Johnson Requests $12.8B Vietnam Supplemental from Congress
Fiscal deficits widened further, with inflation and rate pressures intensifying.
Late Feb
Goodyear Raises Tire Prices 1-3% Citing "Anticipatory Costs"
Corporate inflation pass-through was viewed as a landmark signal; the same month, 10-year Treasury yields broke through 5%, exceeding prior record highs.
Mar 10
De Gaulle Announces French Withdrawal from NATO Military Structure
Required US forces to leave France by April 1, 1967, exposing fissures inside the Cold War Western camp.
Early Apr
Johnson Proposes 5-7% Tax Hike to Cool the Economy
Modest individual and corporate tax hikes were placed on the agenda to address overheating and inflation.
Jun 24
Fed Discount Rate: 4.0% → 5.0%
A 100 bp single hike — the signature tightening move of 1966.
Aug 17
Fed Raises Reserve Requirements Again
Bank lending grew visibly tight from June to August; "credit crunch" entered the financial-history lexicon.
Mid-Sep
BIS "Group of Ten" Significantly Increases Mutual Credit Lines
The Fed, Bank of England, and other G10 central banks expanded mutual credit via the BIS to address potential dollar and sterling crises.
Sep 21
Fed Cuts Maximum Deposit Rate Ceiling
Implemented after Congress expanded Fed powers, aimed at curbing "irrational" interbank deposit competition and easing the credit crunch.
Nov 8
Midterm Elections: Republicans Gain 47 House Seats
House Democrats fell from 295 to 248; Republicans rose from 140 to 187. Public discontent with the Great Society agenda and rising conservative sentiment.
Dec 6
Fed Cuts Reserve Requirements
Monetary policy began to ease at the margin, though not dramatically given still-high inflation.
