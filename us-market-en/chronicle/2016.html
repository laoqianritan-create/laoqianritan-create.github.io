Overview

Rough open + oil bottom · Brexit · Trump elected · Earnings cycle restarts

2016 marked the start of a new earnings-upcycle, but the surface narrative was dominated by two black swans: the Brexit referendum and Trump's election. The year opened with the late-2015 selloff intact—on Jan 4 China A-share circuit breakers tripped and US stocks opened sharply lower; by Feb 11 the S&P 500 was down roughly 9% YTD. WTI crude bottomed at $26/bbl the same day. Combined with the Fed's March pivot dovish (the dot plot cut from four hikes to two), markets stabilized.

On Jun 23 the Brexit referendum passed unexpectedly; the S&P 500 fell 5% over three sessions and quickly recovered—fund managers had carried the highest cash levels since 2008 into the vote. After Brexit, robust jobs and PMI carried equities slowly higher into early September; July–August PMI softness then drove a modest pullback. On Nov 8 Trump defeated Clinton; his tax-cut and fiscal-stimulus agenda ignited a year-end "Trump trade"—in December the Dow approached 20,000 and all three indices set new highs.

On Dec 14 the Fed delivered its sole hike of the year to 0.5%–0.75%, signaling three hikes for 2017. The S&P 500 finished at 2238, +9.54%; the Dow +13.4%. The year swung between "recovery doubts" and "hike fears", but listed-company earnings entered a fresh upcycle and fundamentals outweighed rate concerns.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 4
China A-share circuit breakers trip; US opens sharply lower
CSI 300 trips both 5% and 7% breakers, ending trading early. The Nikkei 225 falls more than 3% and Europe's three main indices fall in tandem.
Jan 29
BoJ unexpectedly adopts negative interest rates
A short-term positive market reaction is overwhelmed by weak ISM PMI and jobs data, keeping US equities under pressure.
Feb 11
WTI crude bottoms at $26/bbl
Ends a 20-month decline; January PCE jumps to 0.97%, the highest since December 2014. Global risk appetite begins to recover.
Feb 13
Justice Scalia dies
The conservative standard-bearer's unexpected death deadlocks the Supreme Court nomination; Senate Republicans block Obama nominee Merrick Garland, leaving the seat for the next president.
Late Feb
G20 finance ministers reach the "Shanghai Accord"
Major economies informally coordinate FX and monetary policy to curb competitive devaluation; the dollar enters a sideways range.
Mar 16
FOMC cuts the year's expected hikes from four to two
The dot plot is sharply revised down, signaling a slower hike path. The ECB had cut rates and expanded QE on Mar 10.
Apr 3
Panama Papers exposed
The ICIJ releases 11.5 million confidential Mossack Fonseca documents revealing offshore structures linked to multiple heads of state and business leaders.
Jun 23
Brexit referendum passes
52% to 48% in favor of Leave; Cameron resigns the next day. The S&P 500 falls about 5% over three sessions; sterling has its largest single-day drop on record.
Jul 14
Bastille Day truck attack in Nice
A truck plows into a crowd watching fireworks, killing 86 and further straining European security.
Jul 15
Failed military coup in Turkey
A faction of the military attempts a coup that night; Erdogan subsequently purges the military, judiciary, and civil service, accelerating authoritarian consolidation.
Aug
Rio de Janeiro Summer Olympics open
South America's first Olympics, held while Brazil was engulfed in Rousseff's impeachment and a deep recession.
Nov 4
Paris Climate Agreement enters into force
The first universal, binding climate agreement covers nearly 200 parties with a goal of keeping warming this century below 2°C.
Nov 8
Trump defeats Clinton, elected 45th US President
Tax-cut and fiscal-stimulus pledges ignite market expectations and launch the year-end "Trump trade", with financials, industrials, and energy leading.
Dec 14
Fed hikes 25 bp to 0.5%–0.75%
The year's only hike; the dot plot signals three more in 2017, above the previous "two" expectation; the 10-year yield hits a seven-year high.
