1975
Chronicle · A Breather in the Middle of Stagflation

A Sharp Recovery

S&P 500 +31.55% Dow Jones +38.3% Year-End 851 Main Theme Easing Cycle + Recession Bottoms + End of Vietnam
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Easing cycle underway · Recession bottoms in March · Vietnam War ends

1975 was a rare, powerful rally in the middle of the stagflation decade. The macro backdrop was the easing cycle the Fed had launched in December 1974 — the discount rate fell from 7.75% to 6.25% during Q1 1975, while the commercial bank prime rate was cut 12 times in a row, from 10.5% at the start of the year to 7.5% by late March. As rates fell rapidly, the economy was still at its trough: Q1 1975 GDP was -2.3% YoY, and the NBER recession ended in late March.

Markets reacted quickly: by late January the Dow was up roughly 15%; by mid-March the gain had widened to about 25%. On March 29 President Ford signed the Tax Reduction Act of 1975 (about $22.8 billion), using rebates to spur consumption. On April 30 Saigon fell and the Vietnam War ended. On May 15 the Fed cut the discount rate again to 6.0%, and that same month the US repealed its ban on private gold ownership. The rally continued to July 15, when the Dow touched its 1975 high of 889.

The rhythm shifted in H2: from July the prime rate began to rise for the first time in the year, while wholesale prices +14.4% YoY and a $2 billion Treasury issuance pushed the market into a sideways consolidation. New York City's fiscal crisis flared in October but ended in a federal rescue. The Dow closed the year at 851, +38.3%, with the S&P 500 +31.55% — the second-best post-WWII year, surpassed only by 1954.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 3
Fed discount rate cut from 7.75% to 7.25%
Continuing the easing cycle started in December 1974 — the first cut of 1975.
Jan 20
Detroit's Big Three cut car prices
After the oil shock, slumping auto sales and spreading unemployment forced a rare across-the-board price cut by the Big Three — a defining moment in the worst auto-industry slump since WWII. The Fed also lowered reserve requirements on the same day, releasing about $1.1 billion in liquidity.
Feb 4
Fed discount rate cut from 7.25% to 6.75%
Second of Q1's consecutive cuts.
Mar 7
Fed discount rate cut from 6.75% to 6.25%
Third cut of Q1. Over the same period the prime rate fell from 10.5% at the start of the year to 7.5% by late March.
Mar 29
Ford signs the Tax Reduction Act of 1975
Roughly $22.8 billion in size, with a one-time rebate of about $130 per taxpayer household — the most important anti-recession fiscal action of the year.
Apr 4
Microsoft founded in New Mexico
Bill Gates and Paul Allen incorporated Micro-Soft in Albuquerque to write a BASIC interpreter for the Altair 8800.
Apr 30
Fall of Saigon ends the Vietnam War
The US embassy completed Operation Frequent Wind, and the South Vietnamese government surrendered. President Ford declared that the US was "no longer at war" — the close of the Vietnam Era.
May 1
US repeals ban on private gold ownership
For the first time since 1933, US citizens could freely hold gold — echoing the monetary order being rebuilt after the collapse of Bretton Woods.
May 15
Fed discount rate cut from 6.25% to 6.0%
The final cut of the cycle for the year. The US external accounts also improved markedly in Q1 and Q2.
Jul 18
Commercial-bank prime rate rises for the first time in the year
Up from 7.0% to 7.25%, then further to 7.5% by month-end and 7.75% by mid-August. The reversal triggered a market re-pricing of inflation and deficits.
Sep 5 / 22
President Ford survives two assassination attempts within 17 days
In Sacramento on Sep 5 and San Francisco on Sep 22, both by women assailants; the president was unharmed.
Oct 16
New York City sinks into a fiscal crisis
With $453 million of debt due the next day and only $34 million in cash, the city was on the brink of bankruptcy. President Ford initially took a hard line and vetoed the "emergency fiscal assistance" bill.
Dec 30
Federal government signs a credit agreement with New York City
The Treasury was authorized to extend short-term loans to NYC, capped at $2.3 billion. The city, in turn, raised resident income taxes and tapped municipal-employee retirement funds.
