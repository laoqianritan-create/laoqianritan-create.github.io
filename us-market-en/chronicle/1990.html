Overview

Economic hard landing · Gulf invasion ignites oil crisis · NBER recession officially confirmed

1990 saw the collapse of the Cold War order and a US hard landing occur simultaneously. The year opened with the carryover of Q4 1989's GDP-growth slowdown to 2.74%, plus spillover from Nikkei and European market crashes, sending the three major US indices down about 10% in January. From February, better-than-expected January employment plus Greenspan's expansion-supportive remarks anchored a rebound to mid-July, when the Dow broke 3,000 for the first time. High inflation and rising rate fears persisted throughout.

The turning point came when Iraq invaded Kuwait on August 2. Crude monthly average prices surged from about $17 to $36 in October, formally igniting the Third Oil Crisis. Combined with rising unemployment, weakening retail, and a softer dollar, fundamentals deteriorated against the war backdrop; from mid-July to mid-October, the S&P 500 and Dow fell nearly 20%, and Nasdaq fell 31%. NBER subsequently dated the recession start to July.

In mid-October, oil prices peaked and rolled over; on October 29, the Fed cut rates 25bp, beginning sustained easing. The Fed cut the fed funds target four times in November-December and cut the discount rate 50bp on December 19. The three major indices rebounded over 10% from their lows. Full-year Dow closed at 2,634, S&P 500 -6.56%, Nasdaq -18% — the worst year since 1981. Political maps also shifted dramatically: Mandela freed in February, USSR abandoned single-party rule in March, German reunification in October.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Feb 11
South Africa releases Nelson Mandela
Mandela was freed after 27 years in prison, starting the countdown to dismantling apartheid.
Mar
USSR ends Communist Party single-party rule
The Congress of People's Deputies amended Article 6 of the Constitution, repealing the CPSU's "leading role"; Gorbachev was elected the USSR's first President. Eastern European transformation accelerated.
May
RTC continues resolving S&L institutions
The Resolution Trust Corporation, under the 1989 FIRREA, took over hundreds of insolvent S&L institutions; the S&L crisis entered peak resolution.
Jul 13
Fed cuts fed funds target 25bp
First cut of 1990; target rate lowered from 8.25% to 8.0%.
Aug 2
Iraq invades Kuwait
Iraqi forces crossed the northern border in the predawn hours and occupied most of the Kuwaiti capital. The Third Oil Crisis ignited. Crude monthly average prices climbed from ~$17 to ~$36 in October.
Aug 6
UN Security Council imposes comprehensive sanctions on Iraq
Resolution 661 prohibited trade with Iraq. The next day, Defense Secretary Cheney visited Saudi Arabia and "Operation Desert Shield" began.
Jul
NBER subsequently dated recession start
Subsequently identified July 1990 as the recession's start, which ended in March 1991.
Oct 3
German reunification
East Germany formally joined the Federal Republic of Germany, ending the post-WWII division. The Cold War entered its closing phase.
Oct 29
Fed cuts rates 25bp
Long-end rates fell sharply; markets anticipated more cuts. Fed funds target cut again on November 13, December 7, and December 18.
Nov
Bush and Gorbachev advance START negotiations
At the Paris CSCE meeting, the US and USSR advanced Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty negotiations; the same month they signed the Conventional Forces in Europe Treaty. Cold War arms control architecture took shape.
Dec 19
Fed cuts discount rate 50bp
Four cumulative cuts to the fed funds target on the year, plus the 50bp discount rate cut, formally confirming entry into an easing cycle.
