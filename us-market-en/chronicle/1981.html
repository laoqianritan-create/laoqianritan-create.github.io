Overview

Reagan takes office + supply-side tax cuts · Volcker pushes discount rate to 14% · market moves strictly with rates

1981 marked the final year of the 1968–1981 Great Stagflation era and the dawn of a policy paradigm shift. On January 20, Ronald Reagan was sworn in as the 40th President; on the same day, Iran released the 52 American hostages held for 444 days. On February 18, Reagan submitted his "Program for Economic Recovery" to Congress, framed around four pillars: large-scale tax cuts, spending restraint, monetary control, and deregulation — regarded as the most original economic policy framework since the New Deal.

The market's dominant driver this year was not fundamentals but interest rates. From late January to late March, 3-month T-bill yields fell more than 300 basis points and the prime rate was cut seven consecutive times totaling 450 basis points; equities rebounded from a late-February bottom into late April. On May 4, the Fed hiked the discount rate to 14% — an all-time peak — and the market entered its primary downtrend. The Dow fell from 1,012 on June 15 to 824 on September 25, an 18.6% decline.

Starting in October, inflation re-entered a downtrend and Volcker pivoted: the discount rate was cut from 14% to 13% on October 30 and to 12% on December 3, with equities stabilizing and modestly rebounding off the lows. On August 13, Reagan signed the Economic Recovery Tax Act (ERTA), cutting taxes 25% over three years, providing the legal foundation for the supply-side revolution. The Dow closed at 875, down 9.2% on the year; the S&P 500 finished -9.73%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
Reagan inaugurated as 40th US President · Iran releases 52 American hostages the same day
70-year-old Ronald Reagan was sworn in. On the same day, the 52 American hostages held for 444 days were freed, formally ending the Iran hostage crisis.
Jan 28
Reagan signs his first executive order · Lifts domestic oil price controls
Gasoline price and allocation controls originally set to expire in September were terminated early, signaling a decisive shift toward a "conservative" policy stance.
Feb 18
Reagan submits "Program for Economic Recovery" to Congress
Four planks: cut personal income taxes 30% over three years, reduce federal spending, control money supply, and deregulate business. Regarded as the most original policy blueprint since the New Deal.
Mar 30
President Reagan shot outside the Washington Hilton
Gunman John Hinckley shot Reagan in the chest. The President recovered after surgery and returned to White House duties within about a month.
May 4
Fed discount rate 13% → 14%
Volcker held the line on tightening, pushing the discount rate to its all-time peak. Within the next month, the prime rate was raised six consecutive times, totaling 350 basis points.
Jun 7
Israeli airstrike destroys Iraq's Osirak nuclear reactor
"Operation Babylon." Israeli F-16s flew a long-range strike. Crude oil prices had by then already retreated from their year-to-date highs.
Jul
NBER later dated a new US recession beginning July · IBM PC launched
NBER confirmed on January 6, 1982 that the recession began in July 1981. In the same period, IBM launched the 5150 personal computer, opening the PC era.
Aug 3 – Aug 5
PATCO air-traffic-controller strike · Reagan fires 11,345 within 48 hours
On August 3, the PATCO union launched an illegal strike. Reagan ordered controllers back to work within 48 hours, and on August 5 formally fired 11,345 who refused to return, permanently banning them from federal employment. The balance of labor-vs-capital power inverted from that point on.
Aug 13
Reagan signs the Economic Recovery Tax Act (ERTA)
25% cumulative tax cut over three years (5% / 10% / 10%). Top marginal rate reduced from 70% to 50%, with accelerated depreciation for business equipment. The legal embodiment of supply-side tax reform.
Oct 30
Fed cuts rates · Discount rate 14% → 13%
With inflation re-entering a downtrend in October, the Fed cut the credit surcharge first on September 21 and October 9 (4%→3%→2%), then formally cut the discount rate on October 30 and removed the surcharge on November 16.
Dec 3
Fed cuts again · Discount rate 13% → 12%
The second concrete pivot of the tightening cycle. Long-end yields, however, ticked back up in December.
Dec 29
Reagan announces economic sanctions on the Soviet Union
Citing the Soviet role in Poland's martial-law crisis, Reagan announced immediate sanctions from the White House. The Cold War returned to a confrontational cycle.
