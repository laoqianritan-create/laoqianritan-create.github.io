Overview

NBER recession all year · Volcker pivots mid-year · Aug 13 Mexico default · Aug 17 S&P bottoms and rebounds

1982 was the second year of Reaganomics and also the endpoint of thirteen years of stagflation. Volcker tightening persisted through the first eight months: short rates rose from 12% to a peak of 15.5% in January–February, and combined with the lingering shock of the second oil crisis, industrial production declined year-over-year from -3.2% in March to -6.9% in August, while unemployment climbed to 10.8% — the highest postwar reading. NBER classified the entire year as recessionary. Inflation, however, fell sharply in parallel: CPI dropped from 8.9% at year-end 1981 to 6.8% in March 1982, and PPI recorded a rare month-over-month decline not seen in over a decade.

The turning point came in July. The Fed cut the discount rate twice in quick succession on July 19 and July 30 (12% → 11% → 10.5%) and twice more in August (10% → 9.5%), totaling seven cuts on the year and reaching 8.5% by year-end. On August 13, Mexico's finance minister notified the US government and IMF that the country could not service $80 billion of external debt, formally igniting the Latin American debt crisis. Brazil, Argentina, and Venezuela soon defaulted in turn, dealing severe blows to large US banks including Citi and Chase. Paradoxically, the crisis accelerated international capital flight back into US assets — US equities and Treasuries became safe havens.

The S&P 500 bottomed on August 12 at 102.42 and rebounded +4.8% in a single session on August 17. November's midterm elections expanded Democratic control of Congress, reinforcing market expectations for continued monetary easing. The S&P 500 closed the year at 140.64, up 14.76%. With hindsight, August 1982 marks the true starting point of the 1980s bull market.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 8
AT&T agrees to break up into 22 operating units
A settlement of the Department of Justice's antitrust case. On the same day, the DOJ announced a settlement with IBM, ending a 13-year antitrust suit.
Apr 2
Falklands War breaks out between UK and Argentina
Argentine forces landed on the Falkland Islands; Britain dispatched a task force in response. Argentina surrendered on June 14.
Jun 6
Israel invades Lebanon (Fifth Arab-Israeli War)
Large-scale Israeli offensive into southern Lebanon, aimed at expelling PLO forces. Middle East geopolitical tensions escalated.
Jul 19
Fed cuts discount rate 12% → 11.5%
Volcker's tightening regime begins to reverse. Eleven days later, another cut to 11.0% (July 30).
Aug 12 – 13
Mexico declares it cannot service external debt; Latin American debt crisis erupts
On August 12, Mexican finance minister Silva Herzog notified the US government and the IMF that Mexico could not service $80 billion in external debt. The event is regarded as the genesis of the 1980s developing-country debt problem. On the same day, the Fed cut the discount rate again to 10.5%.
Aug 16
Salomon Brothers' Kaufman publishes rate-cut forecast
Salomon Brothers' chief economist Henry Kaufman reversed his stance of recent months, forecasting that rates would fall further and advising clients to withdraw cash from banks and buy stocks and bonds aggressively. The call became legendary.
Aug 26
Fed discount rate 10.5% → 10.0%
Second cut in August, fourth of the year. The 3-month T-bill yield had fallen over 800 basis points from its early-year peak.
Oct 8
Fed discount rate 10.0% → 9.5%
International capital continued to flee emerging markets in the wake of the LatAm debt crisis and flow back into US assets, with US equities and Treasuries serving as safe havens.
Nov 2
US midterm elections: Democrats expand congressional majority
House Democrats netted 27 seats. Markets read the outcome as supportive of continued monetary easing.
Nov 19
Fed discount rate 9.5% → 9.0%
Sixth rate cut of the year.
Dec 13
Fed discount rate 9.0% → 8.5%
Seventh rate cut of the year, a cumulative 350 basis points of easing from the 12% starting point.
