2018
Chronicle · Final Year of the 9-Year Bull

Pullback in the Longest Bull

S&P 500 -6.24% Dow Jones -5.6% Year-End 23327 Main Theme Powell + Trade War + Q4 Plunge
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Final year of the nine-year bull · Powell takes over and the trade war begins · A deep Q4 pullback

2018 was the final year of the nine-year bull market that began in 2009. The year opened with 2017's momentum intact and the TCJA carrying the index to fresh highs. From February, however, the 10-year yield jumped roughly 50 bp into mid-February, sparking the first wave of volatility. Powell took over as Fed Chair and struck a slightly hawkish tone, prompting markets to re-price "four hikes" for the year.

On Mar 22 Trump signed the Section 301 investigation memorandum, formally opening the US-China trade war; the Facebook–Cambridge Analytica scandal broke around the same time, and tech sold off. From April through September, strong earnings and 7.1% nominal Q2 GDP pushed the index back into an uptrend; on Aug 1 Apple became the first $1 trillion company by market cap; in September the S&P 500 hit a new high above 2900. Mid-year, EGRRCPA was signed into law, lifting the SIFI threshold from $50 billion to $250 billion—a substantive easing of post-crisis regulation.

Q4 turned sharply lower. The 10-year yield jumped from 3.05% to 3.23% in six sessions, and the S&P 500 fell about 7% in October. December brought a yield reversal, the Meng Wanzhou arrest, a fourth hike on Dec 19, and a Dec 22 government shutdown; the index fell 2.7% on Christmas Eve. The S&P 500 finished -6.24% for the year, displaying a "slow grind up, fast drop" pattern—the long-bull trend was intact, but Q4 was the deepest pullback of the cycle.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Feb 5
Powell succeeds Yellen as Fed Chair
In his Feb 27 semi-annual monetary report Powell strikes a hawkish tone, signaling four hikes this year versus market expectations of three.
Mar 1
Trump announces 25% / 10% tariffs on steel and aluminum
The White House signs the orders on Mar 8, effective 15 days later. The opening shot of the trade-war cadence.
Mar 17
Facebook–Cambridge Analytica scandal exposed
The New York Times reveals that Cambridge Analytica obtained 50 million Facebook users' data ahead of the 2016 US election.
Mar 21
Fed hikes to 1.5–1.75%
First hike of the year.
Mar 22
Trump signs Section 301 memo; US-China trade war begins
Announces tariffs on $60 billion of Chinese imports and restrictions on Chinese investment in and acquisitions of US assets.
May 24
EGRRCPA signed, easing financial regulation
Raises the SIFI threshold from $50 billion to $250 billion—the most significant rollback of Dodd-Frank since the crisis.
Jun 1
Steel/aluminum tariffs imposed on the EU, Canada, and Mexico
Tariff exemptions expire and trade frictions spread to traditional allies.
Jun 12
Trump–Kim Singapore summit
The first postwar meeting between sitting US and North Korean leaders; a joint statement is signed.
Jun 13
Fed hikes to 1.75–2.0%
Second hike of the year.
Aug 1
Apple's market cap tops $1 trillion
The first tech company in history to cross $1 trillion in market cap.
Sep 26
Fed hikes to 2.0–2.25%
Third hike of the year. Two days later the 10-year yield begins to spike.
Nov 6
US midterms: Democrats retake the House
Republicans hold the Senate; a split Congress takes shape.
Dec 19
Fed hikes to 2.25–2.5% (the year's last)
Fourth hike of the year and the cycle's terminus. Trump had spent months attacking Powell on Twitter.
Dec 22
Federal government shuts down
A standoff over border-wall funding shuts the government for 35 days, through Jan 25, 2019—the longest shutdown in US history.
Dec 24
Treasury Secretary Mnuchin calls the heads of the six big banks
On Dec 23 Mnuchin calls the heads of the six largest banks to confirm ample liquidity; sentiment fails to settle. Trump keeps attacking Powell on Twitter; Q4 policy uncertainty peaks.
