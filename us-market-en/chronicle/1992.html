Overview

Hesitant recovery · Fed cuts three times · Black Wednesday and the pound exits ERM

1992 was a "build-up" year for the 1990s long bull. The macro backdrop was improvement but slow growth: unemployment rose, PMI and GDP improved, and corporate profits gradually emerged from the negative-growth band that had persisted since 1989. The market diverged early: the Dow and Nasdaq made new highs, but the S&P 500 declined from early year to April. The early-February FOMC kept rates unchanged and voiced concern about the outlook; combined with rising long-end rates, the three major indices weakened in tandem.

On April 7 the Fed surprised with a 25bp cut, triggering a two-month rebound. June through mid-September saw wide range trading: rising oil prices, overseas recession, and the Bundesbank's July hike to 8.75% hampered further Fed easing. The Fed cut 50bp on July 2 and another 25bp on September 4, compressing the fed funds rate to 3.0%. In mid-September, Europe erupted into the worst postwar currency crisis — the Italian lira devalued first, and on September 16 "Black Wednesday," the UK could not defend the pound even by hiking rates from 10% to 15%; the UK and Italy successively exited the European Exchange Rate Mechanism (ERM). US equities declined from mid-September through early October.

From October 12, as Q3 earnings reports rolled out, market focus returned home: Q3 earnings +18% YoY, GDP growth +3.67%, TTM earnings turned positive for the first time since 1989, and unemployment, retail, and consumer confidence all improved. On November 3, Clinton defeated George H.W. Bush to become the 42nd President. Year-end gains showed a clear growth tilt: Nasdaq +15% on the year, Dow and S&P each about +4%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Feb 5
Fed FOMC keeps rates unchanged
FOMC voiced concerns about the growth outlook; near-term further cuts looked unlikely. The 300bp of 1991 cuts reset expectations.
Apr 7
Fed surprise 25bp cut
Fed funds target cut 25bp. The previous day's March 31 FOMC had already adopted an easing bias.
Apr 29
Los Angeles riots erupt
The Rodney King verdict acquitting police triggered six days of rioting, leaving 60+ dead and over $1 billion in property damage.
Jun 3
UN Conference on Environment and Development (Rio Earth Summit)
172 heads of state in Rio signed the Framework Convention on Climate Change and the Convention on Biological Diversity. The first large-scale global governance agenda after the Cold War.
Jul 2
Fed cuts 50bp
Fed funds target compressed to 3.25%. The Bundesbank surprised by hiking to 8.75% the same period; USD/DEM subsequently fell to a 40-year low.
Jul 25
Barcelona Summer Olympics open
First post-Cold-War Summer Olympics; unified Germany and CIS states participated under new identities for the first time.
Sep 4
Fed cuts another 25bp
Fed funds target lowered to 3.0%, third cut of the year, hitting a low not seen since the 1960s.
Sep 8
Finnish markka unpegged from the Deutsche mark and floats freely
The first domino in the European currency crisis. On September 13, the Italian lira was forced to devalue 3.5%.
Sep 16
Black Wednesday — pound exits ERM
The UK raised its bank rate from 10% to 15% twice in one day but could not stop the pound's slide, forced to exit the ERM; the Italian lira exited in parallel. Soros' Quantum Fund earned over $1 billion shorting the pound. The worst European currency crisis since WWII.
Nov 3
Bill Clinton defeats George H.W. Bush to become President
Twelve years of Republican rule ended and the Democrats returned to the White House. Ross Perot won 19% of the popular vote as an independent but no electoral votes.
Dec 17
US, Canada, and Mexico leaders sign NAFTA
The last major economic agreement of George H.W. Bush's tenure. Pending ratification by the three legislatures, NAFTA took effect January 1, 1994.
