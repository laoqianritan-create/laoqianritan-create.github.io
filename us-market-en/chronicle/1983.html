Overview

Recovery confirmed + Strategic Defense Initiative + Volcker reappointed

1983 was the first full expansion year after the recession that ended in November 1982. NBER later dated the recession trough at November 1982; all of 1983 ran along a recovery-and-expansion trajectory: real GDP grew about 4.6%, CPI fell from 6%+ to 3.2% year over year, industrial production climbed throughout, and housing starts in January and June set successive highs (the best readings in the Commerce Department's series going back to 1959). Fundamentals provided the underlying drive for equities' full-year gains.

In mid-January, after nearly two decades of range trading, the S&P 500 broke out above its prior range and printed a new all-time high, followed by a brief consolidation. From February the primary uptrend resumed, hitting 153.67 on March 4 to complete the first leg. On March 23, Reagan unveiled the Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI, "Star Wars") in a televised White House address — a pivotal shift in the Cold War's military-technological balance. April earnings season and confirmation of recovery pushed cumulative gains past 15%; on June 22, the "Volcker reappointment" expectation fueled a so-called Volcker rally, with the S&P 500 reaching 170.99 for year-to-date gains of 21%.

The second half returned to rate-driven trading: equities pulled back as long-end yields rose from late June to early August, with the S&P 500 troughing near 159; rates eased again from mid-August to early October, and the index hit its year-high of 172.65 on October 10; both short and long yields then ticked back up and the market entered a sideways consolidation. Geopolitically, the USSR shot down Korean Air Lines Flight 007 on September 1, a Beirut Marine barracks bombing on October 23 killed 241 US servicemen, and the US invaded Grenada on October 25 — Cold War and Middle East tensions heated up simultaneously. The Fed held the discount rate at 8.5% from early year to mid-year, then nudged it modestly to 9.0% in H2. The S&P 500 closed at 164.93, +17.27%; the Dow finished at 1,258.64, +20.3%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 15
Bank prime rate 11.5% → 11.0%
Banks cut the prime rate on January 15, 1983, further reinforcing recovery expectations.
Jan 20
Volcker: fiscal deficits constrain monetary policy room
Fed Chair Paul Volcker told Congress that massive fiscal deficits made it difficult for the Fed to simultaneously support recovery and lower rates.
Jan 24
OPEC's Geneva meeting ends without agreement
The OPEC ministerial meeting failed to agree on a quota-and-price stabilization plan. Two months later, at the London meeting on March 14, OPEC agreed to its first price cut.
Mar
IBM PC XT launched
IBM launched the second-generation PC XT, with a hard disk as standard equipment, accelerating the PC era.
Mar 14
OPEC agrees to its first oil price cut
Saudi Arabian light crude benchmark cut from $34 to $29/barrel, a 15% reduction; the 13 members capped daily output at 17.5 million barrels. NYMEX launched WTI futures contracts the same year.
Mar 23
Reagan unveils the Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)
In a televised White House address, Reagan proposed "Star Wars" — building a defense system using new technologies to intercept intercontinental strategic missiles and spacecraft, opening a new phase of Cold War armament.
Apr 18
Car-bomb attack on the US Embassy in Beirut
63 killed, including 17 Americans. The Lebanese civil war spilled outward, deepening US military involvement in the Middle East.
Apr 26
"A Nation at Risk: The Imperative for Educational Reform" released
The Reagan-commissioned education report sharply criticized US K-12 education, triggering a nationwide education reform wave.
Jun 16
Housing starts reach new high again
The Commerce Department's housing starts data set another high not seen since 1959, confirming the strength of the recovery.
Jun
Reagan renominates Volcker as Fed Chair
Reagan renominated Paul Volcker as Fed Chair. Markets read the move as continuity of the anti-inflation commitment, in what came to be called the "Volcker rally."
Jun 18
Sally Ride becomes the first American woman in space
Space Shuttle Challenger's STS-7 mission launched — a landmark moment for the US space program.
Sep 1
Soviet fighter jets shoot down Korean Air Lines Flight 007
KAL 007, flying from New York to Seoul, deviated from its route into Soviet airspace and was intercepted and shot down by a Su-15, killing all 269 aboard, including one US Congressman. The Cold War returned to confrontational mode.
H2
McDonald's launches the "Happy Meal" nationwide
The children's meal-and-toy combo went nationwide for the first time, becoming an icon of US consumer culture.
Oct 23
Beirut US Marine barracks bombing
Hezbollah suicide truck-bomb attack killed 241 US servicemen. A simultaneous attack on French barracks killed 58 French servicemen. The multinational force withdrew from Lebanon two years later.
Oct 25
US invades Grenada ("Operation Urgent Fury")
The Pentagon called it the largest US military operation since Vietnam, aimed at toppling the neo-Marxist coup regime.
