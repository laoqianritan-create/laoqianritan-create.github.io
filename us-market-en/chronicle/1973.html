1973
Chronicle · Onset of the Great Stagflation

The First Oil Crisis

S&P 500 -17.37% Dow Jones -16.6% Year-End 851 Main Theme Bretton Woods Collapse + Oil Shock + Rate Hikes
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Rising rates + deteriorating fundamentals + the October OPEC embargo · The first true stagflation shock of the postwar era

The 1973 backdrop was a peak-and-roll in fundamentals coinciding with a loss of PPI control. Wage-and-price controls held the core CPI down, but PPI broke loose — wholesale prices surged from late 1972 onward, forcing the Fed into a catch-up. The discount rate was raised from 4.5% all the way to 7.5% over the year — the most intense tightening cycle since the 1950s.

The dollar crisis re-erupted in February: on Feb 10 the Tokyo, London, and Paris FX markets were forced to close; on Feb 12 Treasury Secretary Shultz announced a further 10% dollar devaluation, and major countries shifted to floating rates — the formal collapse of Bretton Woods. The Dow bottomed at 923 in late March. The largest rally of the year ran from late August to late October, with the Dow back to 987 by October 26.

The fatal blow followed. On October 6 the Fourth Arab–Israeli War broke out, and on October 17 OPEC announced an oil embargo with a monthly 5% production cut; international oil prices surged from roughly $3 to about $12 within months. Markets were slow to absorb the shock, but after peaking on October 26 they slid relentlessly: by December 5 the Dow had dropped to 784, a 21% loss in a single month. A modest year-end rebound left the Dow at 851 (-16.6%) and the S&P 500 at -17.37%. It was the first year to display the full pattern of postwar stagflation.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 12
Fed discount rate raised from 4.5% to 5.0%
First hike of the year. West Germany, Japan, and other foreign central banks were also raising rates.
Jan 27
Vietnam Peace Accords signed in Paris
Nixon announced the cease-fire deal on Jan 23 and it was formally signed on Jan 27, ending nearly a decade of direct US military engagement in Vietnam.
Feb 12
Dollar devalued another 10%; the fixed-rate system formally collapses
The Tokyo FX market was forced to close on Feb 10. On the evening of Feb 12, Treasury Secretary Shultz announced a further 10% dollar devaluation, with the gold price reset to $42.22/oz; major OECD members moved to floating rates, and the Smithsonian Agreement was declared dead.
Feb 23
Fed discount rate raised from 5.0% to 5.5%
Second hike of the year.
Mar 29
Last US combat units withdraw from South Vietnam
Ending nearly a decade of war in Vietnam.
Apr 20
Fed discount rate raised from 5.5% to 5.75%
Third hike of the year.
Apr 30
Four senior Nixon aides resign over Watergate
White House Chief of Staff Haldeman and Attorney General Kleindienst were among the four to resign. In a televised address, Nixon accepted responsibility for the affair.
May 10
Fed discount rate raised from 5.75% to 6.0%
Fourth hike of the year.
May 17
Senate Watergate hearings begin
The Senate's special committee opened its public hearings.
Jun 8
Fed discount rate raised from 6.0% to 6.5%
Fifth hike of the year. A global wave of inflation-fighting tightening took hold.
Jun 13
Nixon announces a second price freeze (60 days)
Unlike the August 1971 freeze, this one targeted prices only, not wages.
Jun 29
Fed discount rate raised from 6.5% to 7.0%; reserve ratio +0.5%
In response to renewed Deutsche mark appreciation. A double tightening on the same day.
Aug 13
Fed discount rate raised from 7.0% to 7.5%
Seventh hike of the year; cumulative tightening of 300 bp.
Oct 6
Fourth Arab–Israeli (Yom Kippur) War breaks out
Arab oil-producing states weaponized oil to strike at Israel and its backers.
Oct 17
OPEC announces oil embargo; First Oil Crisis erupts
On Oct 16 the five Gulf states and Iran raised the posted crude price by 17%. On Oct 17 the Arab oil ministers' meeting announced 5% monthly production cuts versus September levels and a further 5% monthly cut targeting countries supporting Israel. From Oct 18 onward Abu Dhabi, Libya, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia announced a complete halt of exports to the US. International oil prices surged from roughly $3 to about $12 within months.
Nov 7
US Emergency Energy Act
In response to the embargo-driven energy shortage, the federal government was authorized to control energy allocation and prices.
