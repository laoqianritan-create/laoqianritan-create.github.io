Overview

Presidential steel seizure ruled unconstitutional · Strikes hit · Eisenhower's election sparks tax-cut hopes

The first half of 1952 was difficult for the US economy. Constrained by Defense Production Act controls and tightening measures, industrial production briefly turned negative year-over-year and commodity prices kept falling; rising corporate tax rates squeezed after-tax profits, with H1 corporate earnings sharply negative year-over-year. The most direct overhang on equities was the nationwide US steel strike — labor and management negotiated from late 1951 until talks broke down in April 1952.

Late on the night of April 8, Truman issued a sudden emergency statement, announcing that the government would seize all steel mills at midnight, shocking the nation. From mid-January through late April, the Dow fell steadily. On June 2, the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that the presidential seizure was unconstitutional; steel workers promptly struck, settling on July 24. Equities rallied from May to mid-August on easing labor tensions, restored steel production, and the gradual loosening of Regulation W, consumer credit, and housing credit.

From mid-August to late October, markets sold off again amid ongoing Korean War talks, tax-burdened Q2 earnings, and labor disputes in coal and other industries. On November 4, Eisenhower won the presidency 442–89 — the first Republican president since 1932. Hopes for deficit reduction and tax cuts drove a November-December rally. The Dow closed at 292, up 8.4%, marking the fourth consecutive yearly gain.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan
Steel Workers Delay Strike at Truman's Request
Union pay-raise demands were rejected by management in late 1951. At Truman's request the strike was postponed, opening a long series of unsuccessful negotiations.
Apr 4
Steel Labor-Management Talks Collapse
Steel workers decided to launch a nationwide strike on April 9. With the Korean War ongoing, a national steel strike threatened serious consequences.
Apr 8 (eve)
Truman Emergency Statement: Government Seizes Steel Mills at Midnight
Originally scheduled to meet with union leaders, Truman abruptly changed plans and made the announcement at 10:30 pm, shocking the nation. One of the boldest and most controversial decisions of his presidency.
Jun 2
Supreme Court Rules 6–3 Presidential Steel Seizure Unconstitutional
Within minutes of the ruling, Truman ordered the Commerce Secretary to return the mills to their owners. Steel workers immediately began their strike. This decision also contributed to Truman's eventual decision not to seek re-election in 1952.
May 7
Fed Suspends Regulation W, Easing Consumer Credit Controls
Government controls over the economy began to gradually loosen.
Jun 11
First Easing of Housing Credit Controls
Followed by another round of easing on September 16.
Jun 5
Steel Labor-Management Talks Resume
Three days after the Supreme Court ruling, both sides returned to the table. A formal agreement was reached on July 24, ending the strike.
Sep 18
Soviet Union Vetoes Japan's UN Membership Bid
Another footnote in the Cold War US-Soviet rivalry at the United Nations.
Nov 4
Eisenhower Elected 34th President, 442–89
Defeated Democratic candidate Adlai Stevenson, becoming the first Republican president since 1932.
