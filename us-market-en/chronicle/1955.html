Overview

Textbook recovery into boom · Monetary policy tightens throughout the year · September presidential heart attack triggers panic

1955 was the middle phase of the first postwar bull market. The macro backdrop was a "textbook" transition from recovery to boom — high growth, low inflation, and strong Q1 corporate earnings. But monetary policy tightened throughout the year: stock margin requirements raised twice, the discount rate raised twice, and housing credit tightened. By year-end, interest rate indicators had climbed to their highest level since the 19th century.

Equities entered the main upward leg in mid-May and hit new all-time highs in late July. On Saturday, September 24, 64-year-old President Eisenhower suffered a heart attack; the following Monday (September 26), the Dow fell 6.5% in a single day, with the cumulative drawdown exceeding 10% by mid-October. The market stabilized and rebounded in late October, with Ford Motor's November announcement of a January 1956 IPO further supporting sentiment.

The Dow ultimately closed at 488, up 21% for the year; the S&P 500 returned +26.4%. A rare postwar year — outright monetary tightening on one side, with fundamentals and sentiment combining to push the index to new highs on the other.

Fed Raises Stock Margin Requirement: 50% → 60%
Under Regulations T and U. The first tightening move of 1955.

Fed Discount Rate: 1.5% → 1.75%
First rate hike of the year. Margin requirements were raised again nine days later.
Apr 22
Stock Margin Requirement: 60% → 70%
Second margin increase of the year.
May 14
Warsaw Pact Formed
The Soviet bloc's counterpart to NATO. The Cold War structure was further locked in. The July Geneva "Big Four" summit briefly eased tensions.
Jul 18
Disneyland Opens in California
The first Disneyland in history. The same week, the first "Big Four" summit in a decade was held in Geneva, easing US-Soviet-Western tensions.
Aug 4
Fed Discount Rate: 1.75% → 2.0%
Second rate hike of the year. Housing credit was tightened again the following week.
Sat Sep 24
President Eisenhower Suffers a Heart Attack
The 64-year-old president had a sudden heart attack in Colorado and was unable to perform duties for about six weeks.
Early Nov
Ford Motor Announces January 1956 IPO
The Ford family ceded 60% of equity.
Dec 5
AFL and CIO Merge
The merger created the largest union organization in US history (AFL-CIO).
