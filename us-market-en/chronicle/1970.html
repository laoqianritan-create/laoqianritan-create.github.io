Overview

A recession year within stagflation · Burns takes over the Fed · From fighting inflation to fighting recession

1970 was an NBER-dated recession year (Dec 1969 – Nov 1970), with the economy opening under stagflation. A tight federal budget, roughly 5 million workers facing wage-contract renegotiations, an upward wage-inflation spiral, and a Federal Reserve still tightening in January combined to drag the Dow from roughly 800 at the start of the year down to 744 by late January.

On February 1, Arthur Burns took office as Federal Reserve Chairman, marking the policy watershed of the year. After his appointment he declared that he would "use every means to avoid a recession," and the 3-month Treasury yield fell sharply from 7.91% at the end of January to 6.08% by late March. But once Q1 earnings hit the tape in mid-April, the fundamentals turned grim, and after the US invasion of Cambodia on April 30 and the Kent State shootings on May 4, the Dow slid to 628 by late May — a drawdown of nearly 20%.

On May 27 Burns pledged that the Fed would act as "lender of last resort." In June, the bankruptcy of Penn Central — at the time the largest bankruptcy in US history — delivered another shock, but the monetary pivot was already irreversible: mortgage rules and reserve requirements continued to ease through July–August, and the discount rate was cut twice in November to 5.5%, with the 3-month Treasury yield breaking below 5.0% by year-end, down 300 bp for the year. The Dow rallied through December, closing at 839 (+4.8%), while the S&P 500 ended the year +0.10%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 20
Fed raises ceiling on commercial-bank deposit rates
The last move of the tightening cycle. A tight federal budget and roughly 5 million workers facing wage-contract renegotiations defined the start of the year.
Feb 1
Arthur Burns sworn in as Federal Reserve Chairman
In Feb 7 congressional testimony he pledged to "use every means to avoid a recession." The 3-month Treasury yield fell sharply from 7.91% in late January to 6.08% by late March.
Mar 26
Fed extends emergency credit facility to non-member banks
A substantive policy pivot from "fighting inflation" to "fighting recession."
Apr 30
Nixon announces US invasion of Cambodia
70,000 US and South Vietnamese troops crossed into Cambodia, widening the Vietnam War. On May 1–2 US forces resumed bombing of Quang Binh and Nghe An provinces in North Vietnam.
May 4
Kent State University shootings
Ohio National Guardsmen fired on anti-war students, killing 4 and wounding 9. The nationwide anti-war movement escalated sharply.
May 27
Burns pledges Fed as "lender of last resort"
A commitment that the US economy would not collapse for lack of liquidity, soon endorsed by the White House. Margin requirements had already been eased on May 5.
Jun 21
Penn Central Transportation files for bankruptcy
One of the largest US corporations of the era, with a peak network of 10,000 miles and a 100-year continuous dividend record. The Nixon administration's June 19 decision to deny further federal loans to the railroad sector was the direct trigger.
Jul 24
Fed eases real-estate lending rules
Maximum mortgage loan-to-value ratio raised from 80% to 90%; maximum term extended from 25 to 30 years.
Aug 17
Fed cuts reserve requirements again
Continued liquidity release.
Sep 15
UAW launches 10-week strike against General Motors
The company was shut down for 10 weeks. On Oct 8 North Vietnam rejected Nixon's "peace proposal" in Paris; on Oct 12 Nixon announced the withdrawal of 40,000 troops by Christmas.
Nov 10
Fed cuts discount rate from 6.0% to 5.75%
A further cut to 5.5% followed on November 30. Industrial production bottomed in October, and US forces in Vietnam recorded their first zero-casualty week in five years.
Nov 18
Congress passes the Bank Holding Company Act of 1970
Brings one-bank holding companies under Federal Reserve supervision and restricts the scope of non-banking activities permitted to bank holding companies.
