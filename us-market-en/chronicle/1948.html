Overview

Third postwar year · Anti-inflation remains the main theme · Cold War structure rapidly takes shape

1948 was the third year after WWII, with the macro economy still focused on fighting inflation. Headline CPI sat near 10% but was already on a rapid downward path; ongoing labor disputes and strikes kept wages rising, offsetting disinflationary forces. Before the Fed gained independence in 1951, the central bank had to support fiscal policy by holding Treasury yields low, so falling commodity prices could not translate into lower rates and instead compressed corporate margins — the root cause of early-year equity weakness.

On policy, the Fed raised the discount rate in January and August, lifted reserve requirements in January and June, and in March amended Regulations T and U to allow 75% margin trading. The Revenue Act of 1948 was forced through in late March, and on April 3 President Truman signed the Marshall Plan, lifting the market from late March through mid-June with the Dow peaking near 195. In late June the First Berlin Crisis erupted, and combined with the August rate hike pushed the market into a summer-to-autumn drift lower.

An October rebound ended abruptly after Truman's surprise re-election on November 2 — Democrats favored more intervention and welfare spending, tax-cut hopes faded, business sentiment soured, and the Dow fell as much as 10% that month. December stabilized and recovered part of the losses; the Dow closed at 177, down 2.1% for the year, while the S&P 500 finished -0.65%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 9
Fed Discount Rate: 1.0% → 1.25%
First rate hike in a decade. With CPI near 10%, monetary policy pivoted to tightening.
Jan 23
Commercial Bank Reserve Requirement: 20% → 22%
A second tightening move two weeks after the rate hike. The French franc devalued 44.45% against the dollar the same month, unsettling international finance.
Feb 20
Czechoslovak "February Events"
The Czech Communist Party seized full power via coup, marking a sharp political shift in Eastern Europe and deepening Cold War divisions.
Mar 5
Fed Amends Regulations T and U
Margin trading permitted for securities investors, with margin requirement set at 75%.
Late Mar
Revenue Act of 1948 Enacted Over Veto
Individual income taxes cut 5%–13%, with higher deductions and additional relief for those over 65. Vetoed by Truman but overridden by a two-thirds House majority.
Apr 3
Truman Signs the Marshall Plan
The $13 billion European Recovery Program was formally enacted, designed to contain Soviet influence and reshape the postwar Western European order.
Jun 1
Commercial Bank Reserve Requirement: 22% → 24%
Third tightening of the year. The Fed continued its anti-inflation stance.
Jun 24
First Berlin Crisis: Full Blockade
The Soviet Union imposed a total ground blockade, cutting off all rail traffic; the US launched a massive airlift for Berlin's 2.5 million residents. Cold War tensions spiked and fears of a third world war surged.
Aug 12
Fed Discount Rate: 1.25% → 1.5%
With CPI still stubborn, monetary policy tightened again alongside consumer credit restrictions.
Nov 2
Truman Wins a Dramatic Re-election Upset
Defeated Republican candidate Dewey. The Chicago Tribune's pre-printed "Dewey Defeats Truman" front-page became a famous gaffe.
