Brutal Hikes and a Deep Bear

S&P 500 -19.44% Dow Jones -8.8% Year-End 33147 Main Theme Inflation + 425 bp of Hikes + Deep Bear
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

40-year highs in inflation · Seven hikes totaling 425 bp · Growth, crypto, and UK gilts in serial crisis

2022 was the flip side of the pandemic-era flood. The year opened with FOMC minutes revealing a hawkish turn: on Jan 20 Netflix's user-growth miss drove a 22% one-day drop, marking the start of the collapse in growth-stock multiples. On Feb 24 Russia invaded Ukraine; in early March Brent broke $130 and LME nickel saw a historic squeeze, with surging commodity prices and supply-chain stress pushing inflation to a peak.

On Mar 16 the Fed launched a brutal hiking cycle with a 25 bp move, followed by 50 bp in May, four consecutive 75 bp hikes from June, and 50 bp in December—425 bp on the year, taking policy to 4.25–4.5%. June CPI hit 8.6%, a 40-year high; September CPI again exceeded expectations and hit markets hard. With rates moving fast, May's Terra/Luna crypto collapse, September's UK "mini-budget" sterling/gilt crisis, and November's FTX bankruptcy exposed three rounds of shadow leverage in turn.

The S&P 500 finished -19.44%, bottoming at 3577 in October; the Nasdaq and growth stocks fell more. On Nov 30 ChatGPT was released, quietly marking the start of a new AI cycle. In August the Inflation Reduction Act ($369 billion) and the CHIPS Act ($52 billion) were signed, laying the fiscal foundation for the subsequent manufacturing-reshoring and energy-transition push.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 5
FOMC minutes turn hawkish
December minutes show officials discussing earlier QT and hikes; the market confirms the policy reversal for the first time.
Jan 20
Netflix subscriber miss drives a 22% one-day drop
The starting point of the pandemic-era growth-stock multiple collapse; markets reprice high-PE, unprofitable growth.
Feb 24
Russia invades Ukraine
The largest European ground war since WWII. The US and Europe impose sweeping financial and energy sanctions on Russia; global energy and food maps are redrawn.
Mar 7–9
Commodity prices spike
Brent breaks $130 intraday; LME nickel sees a historic squeeze on Russian supply shock and the exchange suspends nickel trading and cancels trades.
Mar 16
Fed delivers first 25 bp hike
Fed funds moves from 0–0.25% to 0.25–0.5%, formally opening the brutal hiking cycle.
May 4
Fed hikes 50 bp to 0.75–1.0%
First 50 bp hike since 2000; QT announced to begin in June.
May 10
Terra/Luna algorithmic stablecoin collapses
UST depegs and Luna goes to zero within a week, wiping roughly $40 billion in market cap—the first domino of the crypto cascade.
Jun 10
May CPI prints 8.6%, a 40-year high
After the release, the market expects more aggressive hikes; long-end yields and the dollar move higher in tandem.
Jun 15
Fed hikes 75 bp
The largest single hike since 1994, lifting policy to 1.5–1.75%. Powell hints at another possible 75 bp in July.
Jul 27
Fed hikes 75 bp to 2.25–2.5%
Fourth hike of the year; policy returns to neutral.
Aug 9
CHIPS Act signed
$52 billion in semiconductor subsidies, reshaping US domestic wafer fab build-out and the China-tech containment framework.
Aug 16
Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) signed
$369 billion in energy and climate investment—a long-cycle industrial-policy platform for US clean energy, EVs, and reshoring.
Sep 13
August CPI prints 8.3%, above expectations
Core inflation accelerates sequentially; the market's hope for cooling inflation is dashed.
Sep 21
Fed hikes 75 bp to 3.0–3.25%
Fifth hike of the year; the dot plot shows year-end policy at 4.4%.
Sep 23
UK "mini-budget" triggers sterling/gilt crisis
Truss's tax-cut plan sends sterling to record lows and 30-year gilt yields surging; the BoE intervenes with emergency purchases. Truss resigns in October.
Oct 13
September CPI 8.2%, core CPI hits cycle high
Core CPI year-on-year rises to 6.6%, a 40-year high; evidence of sticky inflation accumulates.
Nov 2
Fed hikes 75 bp to 3.75–4.0%
A fourth consecutive 75 bp move; Powell signals "the terminal rate will be higher than previously expected".
Nov 8
US midterms
Republicans narrowly retake the House; the expected "red wave" doesn't materialize; Democrats hold the Senate.
Nov 11
FTX files for bankruptcy
The world's second-largest crypto exchange collapses within a week with a roughly $8 billion customer-funds shortfall; SBF is later indicted. A full crypto winter sets in.
Nov 30
OpenAI releases ChatGPT
A conversational product based on GPT-3.5 hits a million users in five days. The starting point of the generative-AI wave, redefining the industry and capital narrative for years to come.
Dec 14
Fed hikes 50 bp to 4.25–4.5%
Seventh hike of the year, totaling 425 bp—the fastest hiking cycle since the 1980s.
