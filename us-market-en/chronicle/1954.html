1954
Chronicle · First Postwar Bull Cycle Begins

Surpassing the Peak

S&P 500 +45.02% Dow Jones +44% Year-End 404 Main Theme Monetary Easing + Recession Ends + Breaks 1929 Peak
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Monetary easing + recession ends · Year-long upward march · 1929 peak finally broken after 25 years

1954 produced one of the cleanest uptrend patterns in US equity history — a steady, virtually uninterrupted rally with almost no meaningful pullbacks. The economy was still in recession at the start of the year, but equities extended the rally that began in Q4 1953. Bellwethers such as General Motors and US Steel expressed cautious optimism. The most direct catalyst came from the Fed — the discount rate was cut from 2% to 1.75% on February 4 and to 1.5% on April 13, followed by a reserve requirement cut in June.

The Excise Tax Cut Act of 1954 was passed on May 31, adding fiscal support. The Dow rose 20% in H1. By NBER's definition, the recession formally ended in May 1954. In H2, earnings declines were far smaller than revenue declines, producing strong mid-year reports; the federal debt ceiling was raised to $28.1 billion in August, opening fiscal space. Political noise — the Supreme Court ruling school segregation unconstitutional and the end of the McCarthy hearings — failed to disrupt the rally.

Over nine months the index gained 29%, with only a 1.4% August dip. After a 2.7% technical pullback in October, the rally resumed. On November 23, the Dow Jones Industrial Average finally surpassed the 381 high set on September 3, 1929 — 25 years after the prior peak, opening a new historical phase. The Dow closed at 404, up 44% for the year.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan
GM, US Steel and Other Bellwethers Express Optimism
Despite the ongoing recession that began in July 1953, leading companies such as GM and US Steel struck a relatively optimistic tone on the year's outlook.
Feb 4
Fed Discount Rate: 2% → 1.75%
First rate cut of the year. Monetary policy began easing.
Apr 13
Fed Discount Rate: 1.75% → 1.5%
Second cut of the year as monetary policy eased further, alongside rebounding commodity prices.
Apr 22–Jun 17
Army–McCarthy Hearings End McCarthyism's Run
Joseph McCarthy lost the hearings against the US Army, never recovered politically, and McCarthyism faded from the American political stage.
May 17
Supreme Court Rules Public School Segregation Unconstitutional
Overturned the 1896 "separate but equal" doctrine, sparking unrest in parts of the American South.
May 31
Excise Tax Reduction Act of 1954 Enacted
Extended the 1951 excise tax increases temporarily through March 31, 1955, while lowering rates on telephones, jewelry, and certain other goods.
Jun 21
Fed Cuts Reserve Requirements
Eased credit conditions further, completing the H1 trio of easing moves alongside the two rate cuts.
May
Recession Officially Ends (NBER)
Per NBER's definition, the recession from July 1953 to May 1954 ended. Growth indicators improved progressively in H2.
Aug
Mid-Year Earnings: Profit Decline Much Smaller Than Revenue Decline
With the income-tax base effect fading, after-tax profit declines were far smaller than revenue declines, demonstrating earnings resilience.
Aug 28
Federal Debt Ceiling Raised: $27.5B → $28.1B
Opened greater fiscal space. The Eisenhower administration broadly achieved federal fiscal balance in 1954, leaving room for future tax cuts.
Nov 2
Midterm Elections: Republicans Lose Congressional Majority
Republicans would not regain Senate control until 1980 or House control until 1994. Eisenhower's remaining term faced a divided Congress.
Nov 23
Dow Breaks 1929 All-Time High of 381 After 25 Years
The Dow peaked at 381 on September 3, 1929, before the Great Depression. Surpassing that level marked the psychological closure of the Depression era.
