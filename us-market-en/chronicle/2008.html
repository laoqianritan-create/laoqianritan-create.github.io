2008
Chronicle · Coda to a Painful Decade

Global Financial Crisis Erupts

S&P 500 -38.49% Dow Jones -33.8% Year-End 8776 Main Theme Lehman Falls + TARP + ZIRP + QE1
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Subprime crisis becomes a global one · Lehman falls · ZIRP + QE1 launched

2008 was the worst US equity year since the Great Depression. NBER later back-dated the recession to December 2007. Entering the year, oil had broken $100, PMI and retail were falling, and unemployment was at a two-year high. Subprime risk was spreading rapidly from the financial system into the real economy. On Jan 22 the Fed cut 75 bp inter-meeting; on Jan 30 it cut another 50 bp. In February the Economic Stimulus Act was signed. On Mar 16 Bear Stearns was acquired by JPMorgan with $29 billion of Fed backing on the assets; the S&P 500 was down about 12% year-to-date by Mar 17.

From mid-March to mid-May, a stronger-than-expected Q1 sparked brief hopes of a V-shaped recovery, with the S&P up roughly 12%. But after oil surged to a record $147/bbl on Jul 11, it crashed. Central banks worried about inflation; the ECB hiked on Jul 3—later widely seen as a serious mistake. On Jul 13 IndyMac collapsed; on Sep 7 Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were placed into conservatorship; the financial system reached its breaking point. On Sep 15 Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy, Merrill Lynch was bought by Bank of America the same day, and on Sep 16 AIG received an $85 billion emergency loan from the Fed. The subprime crisis became a full global financial crisis. On Sep 21 Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley converted to bank holding companies; on Sep 29 the Dow fell 7.0% in a single day, its largest point drop on record.

On Oct 3, the $700 billion TARP was signed; on Oct 8, six central banks cut by 50 bp in coordination. On Nov 4 Obama was elected; on Nov 25 the Fed announced $600 billion of MBS purchases, launching QE1. On Dec 16 fed funds dropped to 0–0.25%, the start of the zero-interest-rate era. The index bottomed intraday at 752 on Nov 20 and rallied to close the year at 903—down 38.49% on the year, second only to 1931.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 22
Fed cuts 75 bp inter-meeting to 3.50%
The largest single cut since the 1980s. NBER had already back-dated the recession to December 2007. Another 50 bp cut on Jan 30.
Feb 13
Economic Stimulus Act of 2008 signed
Bush signs a roughly $150 billion package of individual rebates and business tax breaks intended to cushion the subprime shock.
Mar 16
Bear Stearns acquired by JPMorgan
Wall Street's fifth-largest investment bank faces a liquidity crisis. The Fed backstops $29 billion of related assets; JPMorgan buys Bear for roughly $240 million ($2/share, later raised to $10). The Fed delivers a surprise 25 bp cut to 3.25% the same day.
May 12
China's Sichuan magnitude-8.0 earthquake
More than 69,000 dead—the worst natural disaster in China since the 1976 Tangshan quake.
Jul 13
IndyMac Bank fails and is seized by the FDIC
Assets of roughly $32 billion—the second-largest US bank failure ever. The subprime crisis hits a major thrift directly for the first time.
Jul 11
WTI crude touches a record $147/bbl
Oil accelerates through May and tops out, then crashes. On Jul 3 the ECB hikes to fight inflation—widely viewed in hindsight as a major policy mistake.
Aug 8
Beijing Summer Olympics open
China hosts the Summer Games for the first time.
Sep 7
Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac placed in federal conservatorship
The two combined guarantee roughly $5.4 trillion in residential mortgages. Treasury commits up to $100 billion in capital to each.
Sep 15
Lehman Brothers files the largest bankruptcy in US history
Assets of roughly $639 billion—the largest US corporate bankruptcy ever. Bank of America buys Merrill Lynch for about $50 billion the same day. The subprime crisis formally becomes a global financial crisis.
Sep 16
Fed extends $85 billion emergency loan to AIG
The Fed effectively takes over AIG in exchange for a 79.9% equity stake. The same day, money-market fund Reserve Primary "breaks the buck", triggering market-wide redemptions.
Sep 21
Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley become bank holding companies
The end of the independent Wall Street investment bank. Both accept Fed supervision in exchange for discount-window access and stable funding.
Oct 3
Emergency Economic Stabilization Act signed; TARP of $700 billion
The largest US government economic intervention in history. At its core is the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP), recapitalizing banks to restore credit.
Oct 8
Six central banks coordinate a 50 bp cut
A rare cross-border synchronized cut by the Fed, ECB, BoE, BoC, SNB, and Riksbank. Korea, Japan, Indonesia, mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan subsequently ease as well.
Nov 4
Obama elected 44th US President
The first African-American US president; Democrats take both chambers of Congress, paving the way for the ARRA stimulus.
Nov 25
Fed announces $600 billion of MBS and agency-debt purchases
Includes $100 billion of Fannie/Freddie/FHLB direct debt and $500 billion of MBS. QE1 launches, opening a six-year QE cycle.
Dec 11
Madoff Ponzi scheme exposed
A roughly $65 billion fraud—the largest Ponzi scheme in US history—further eroding trust in finance.
Dec 16
Fed cuts to 0–0.25%, the zero lower bound
Fifth cut of the year, a 75 bp move beyond market expectations. From Sep 2007 to today, fed funds has dropped from 5.25% to 0–0.25%, entering the zero-interest-rate (ZIRP) era.
