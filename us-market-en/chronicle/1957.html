Overview

Inflation pressure + tight money + first synchronized postwar global recession

1957 was the final year of the 1948–1957 era and the end of the first postwar bull market. The year opened under the shadow of the 1956 Middle East crisis. On January 5, Eisenhower presented the "Eisenhower Doctrine" to Congress, authorizing $200 million in economic and military aid for the Middle East. The record peacetime spending budget eliminated tax-cut hopes and put inflation back in the spotlight — the market's main concern in early 1957, with rumors even surfacing of renewed wage and price controls.

Equities fell from early January through early February, with the Dow dropping from 496 to 455, about -9%. The turn came in mid-February: Middle East tensions eased, growth accelerated from April-July, and Q1 corporate earnings were strong; Chrysler set a sales record, with EPS jumping from $1.25 to $5.34. The Dow rebounded from 455 to 520, about +14%. But August marked the start of an NBER-defined recession (Aug 1957 – Apr 1958), and for the first time the downturn showed synchronization across capitalist economies — the US, Canada, Japan, UK, France, and West Germany all entered recession, capitalist-world industrial production fell 5%, and steel and auto output dropped 14.2% and 9.5%.

Adding fuel to the fire, the Fed kept tightening: the discount rate jumped 50 bps from 3.0% to 3.5% on August 8, and in September Fed Chair Martin remained firm on fighting inflation, viewed by markets as reckless given the cycle. Combined with the Little Rock crisis, Sputnik 1, and the Syrian Crisis, the Dow fell from a July peak of 520 to an October low of 416, a near-20% drawdown. A November 14 rate cut to 3.0% brought brief relief, but the December 6 Vanguard rocket launch failure renewed pressure. The Dow closed at 436, down 13% for the year.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 5
Eisenhower Proposes "Eisenhower Doctrine" to Congress
Authorized the president to use $200 million for economic and military aid to Middle East nations. The record peacetime spending budget eliminated tax-cut hopes and brought inflation back to the forefront.
Apr
Chrysler Q1: Record Sales
EPS surged from $1.25 to $5.34 year-over-year. The auto sector, broadly feared in 1956, was unexpectedly a bright spot in early 1957.
Aug
NBER: US Recession Begins (Aug 1957 – Apr 1958)
The first synchronized postwar global recession. The US, Canada, Japan, UK, France, and West Germany all entered recession; capitalist-world industrial production fell 5%, with steel and auto output down 14.2% and 9.5%.
Aug 8
Fed Discount Rate: 3.0% → 3.5% (+50 bps)
"Fuel on the fire" at the recession's start. Fed Chair Martin remained firm on inflation in late September, seen by markets as reckless determination, reflecting policy lag.
Sep 25
Little Rock Crisis
Under armed protection from the US 101st Airborne Division, nine Black students were able to enter Little Rock Central High School in Arkansas.
Oct 4
Soviet Union Launches Sputnik 1
The first human-made satellite to enter orbit. The US fell behind the Soviet Union in space technology, escalating the space race and Cold War rivalry.
Oct
Syrian Crisis Erupts
Raised renewed risk that the US could be drawn into war.
Nov 14
Fed Cuts Rates: Discount Rate 3.5% → 3.0%
Monetary policy finally turned toward easing.
Dec 6
Vanguard Rocket Launch Fails
The Vanguard rocket carrying America's first experimental satellite failed to launch. Under Sputnik's shadow, the failure marked an early setback in the US space race.
