Overview

Euro launches · Fed hikes three times · GLBA repeals Glass-Steagall · Nasdaq up 86% on the year

1999 was the final year before the new century and the climax of the 1990s long bull. Early year was dragged by the aftermath of the Brazil financial crisis and the start of the Microsoft antitrust case; Latin American currencies depreciated sharply from January, and on February 9 Nasdaq fell nearly 4% in a single session. But fundamentals remained solid: the economy had been expanding since 1996, unemployment kept falling to a 29-year low, and Q2 and Q3 corporate earnings growth re-accelerated to 20%+.

The policy theme of the year was the Fed restarting hikes. On June 30, the fed funds target rose from 4.75% to 5.0%, ending nearly two years of easing; on August 24 and November 16, two more 25bp hikes brought rates to 5.5%. CPI and the "Y2K" fear became the year's main sources of market swings. On March 29, the Dow first crossed 10,000; on July 16 it touched 11,210; on October 15 it briefly broke back below 10,000, and on October 18 began the year's most powerful rally.

Meanwhile, the financial regulatory framework underwent a century-defining shift: on November 12, the Financial Services Modernization Act (GLBA) was signed, formally repealing the 1933 Glass-Steagall Act, reopening the gates to combined commercial banking, investment banking, and insurance. Year-end top-ten market-cap lists were dominated by tech names — Microsoft, Cisco, Lucent, Intel, IBM, AOL — playing the role oil majors did in the 1980s. Y2K fear ultimately morphed into greed for the new economy: Nasdaq +86% on the year, an all-time annual high; S&P 500 closed at 1,469, +19.53%; Dow closed at 11,497, +25%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 1
Euro formally launches
11 eurozone countries adopted a single currency, electronic euro first with notes and coins to follow in 2002. The largest monetary event of the century's turn.
Jan 13
Brazilian financial crisis · real devalues
Brazil announced a real devaluation; 23.4% drop within 9 days, shaking Latin America and global emerging markets.
Feb 12
Senate rejects Clinton impeachment
The Senate failed to reach the two-thirds majority on either charge; Clinton remained in office.
Mar 24
NATO air campaign against Yugoslavia · Kosovo War
NATO's first military action against a sovereign nation without UN authorization; the air campaign lasted 78 days until June 10.
May 7
US bombs Chinese embassy in Yugoslavia
A US B-2 bombed the Chinese embassy in Belgrade, killing 3 journalists; US-China relations chilled abruptly.
Jun 30
Fed's first hike · Opens tightening cycle
Fed funds target 4.75% → 5.0%, ending nearly two years of easing.
Aug 24
Fed hikes another 25bp
Fed funds target 5.0% → 5.25%; discount rate also raised.
Sep 7
US troops withdraw from Haiti
US forces stationed in Haiti since 1994 completed full withdrawal.
Oct 21
Financial Services Modernization Act passes Senate
Bipartisan agreement on the final version, ready for the President's signature.
Nov 12
GLBA signed · Glass-Steagall repealed
Clinton signed the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, formally repealing the 1933 separation-of-banking restrictions; combined commercial banking, investment banking, and insurance operations were once again legal.
Nov 16
Fed's third hike of the year
Fed funds target 5.25% → 5.5%; discount rate raised to 5%.
Nov 30
Seattle anti-globalization protests
50,000 protested during the WTO ministerial meeting — the "Battle of Seattle" — forcing the conference's interruption.
Dec 31
US transfers Panama Canal sovereignty to Panama
Per the 1977 Torrijos-Carter Treaties, the US ended 85 years of control over the Panama Canal.
Dec 31
Yeltsin resigns · Putin becomes acting president
In a New Year's address, Yeltsin announced his resignation as Russian President; Prime Minister Putin assumed the acting presidency.
