1979
Chronicle · End of the Thirteen-Year Stagflation

The Second Oil Crisis

S&P 500 +12.31% Dow Jones +4.2% Year-End 839 Main Theme Oil + Volcker + Hostage Crisis
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Iranian Revolution triggers the Second Oil Crisis · Volcker takes the Fed · Sharp swings all year

1979 was the coda to the 13-year stagflation, with the index swinging sharply all year. At the start of the year US–China diplomatic relations and a recovering dollar lifted the Dow to 860 on January 26; in February the Iranian Islamic Revolution interrupted oil exports for about 6 weeks, and crude jumped from $13.2/bbl in November 1978 to $20.8/bbl in February, dragging the market back below its starting point. In March Iran resumed exports and Egypt and Israel signed their peace treaty, and the Dow rebounded to 878 on April 10.

From mid-April to early June the market dropped again, driven by Q1 GDP growth slowing to 0.7% and crude jumping another 50% to $33.5/bbl in May. On July 19 the Fed raised the discount rate to 10% and the market chopped sideways. On August 6 Paul Volcker took office as Fed Chair; the market read his "anti-inflation resolve" as bullish, and combined with consecutive hikes in August and September the Dow actually climbed to its yearly high of 898 on October 5.

On October 6 Volcker announced a surprise monetary policy regime change — raising the discount rate 100 bp in one step to 12%, switching to money supply as the operating target, and widening the federal funds rate band to 400 bp. The Dow dropped 6.5% the following week, falling to the yearly low of 797 on November 7. On November 4 the US embassy in Tehran was seized, with 52 hostages taken; oil rose to $40.8/bbl. On December 27 the Soviet Union invaded Afghanistan. Rates retreated from November onward; the Dow closed at 839, +4.2% for the year, with the S&P 500 +12.31%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 1
US–China formal diplomatic relations established
From January 29 to February 5, Vice Premier Deng Xiaoping made an official visit to the US at President Carter's invitation — the first visit to the US by a senior Chinese leader since the PRC's founding.
Jan 16
Iranian Revolution: Shah leaves Iran
The Shah departed for Egypt. On February 1, Khomeini returned to Tehran; on April 1, Iran became an Islamic Republic. From late 1978 through early March 1979 Iran halted oil exports, with a daily shortfall of about 5 million barrels — roughly 10% of world consumption.
Mar 28
Three Mile Island nuclear accident
Partial core meltdown of Reactor 2 at the Three Mile Island plant in Pennsylvania — the worst accident in US commercial nuclear history, with lasting consequences for US nuclear-power policy.
Mar 26
Egypt–Israel Peace Treaty signed in Washington
Sadat and Begin signed, with Carter as co-signer, ending the state of war that began with the First Arab–Israeli War of 1948. Eighteen Arab states and the PLO then met in Baghdad and resolved to impose political and economic sanctions on Egypt.
May
International oil prices jump another 50%
After holding near $22/bbl in March–April, crude jumped to $33.5/bbl in May. Combined with Q1 GDP growth plummeting to 0.7% (released in mid-April), recession was widely expected.
Jun 18
SALT II strategic-arms treaty signed
Signed by Carter and Brezhnev in Vienna. The treaty was later not ratified by the US Senate after the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan.
Jul 15
Carter delivers the "crisis of confidence" speech
In a televised address Carter said the US faced a "crisis of confidence," calling for action on energy shortages and spiritual drift. Five Cabinet members resigned within a week, weakening executive authority.
Jul 19
Fed hike: discount rate from 9.5% to 10%
First hike of the year. On August 16 it went to 10.5%, and on September 18 to 11% — three hikes in a row.
Aug 6
Paul Volcker sworn in as Fed Chair
Nominated by President Carter to succeed Miller. The market read his "anti-inflation resolve" as bullish and assigned the new chair a strong credibility premium.
Sat, Oct 6
Volcker announces surprise monetary regime change
An emergency FOMC meeting: discount rate raised 100 bp to 12% in a single move; money supply adopted as the operating target; federal-funds-rate band widened to 400 bp (11.5%–15.5%). By year-end the fed funds rate approached 14%.
Nov 4
US embassy hostage crisis erupts in Tehran
The US embassy in Iran was seized and 52 diplomats and civilians taken hostage (the crisis lasted 444 days, until January 20, 1981). On November 12 the US halted oil imports from Iran; on November 14 it froze about $8 billion in Iranian assets in the US. International oil prices rose to $40.8/bbl.
Dec 27
Soviet Union invades Afghanistan
Soviet forces entered Afghanistan, ending the Cold War détente phase and directly leading the US Senate to shelve the SALT II ratification process.
