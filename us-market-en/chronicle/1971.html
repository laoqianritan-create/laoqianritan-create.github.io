Overview

Collapse of Bretton Woods · Dollar crisis · The Nixon Shock · Closing with the Smithsonian Agreement

1971 was the pivot year for the postwar international monetary order. In Q1 the Fed cut the discount rate three times (5.5% → 4.75%); on March 15 the Dow broke through 900, and by late April it had touched 958. From May the direction reversed: a swing from surplus to deficit in the US external accounts triggered dollar outflows. On May 5 the central banks of Austria, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Switzerland suspended FX trading, and West Germany floated the mark. Rates rebounded 200 bp from April, and the Dow slid to 840 by early August.

On August 15 Nixon delivered his "bombshell": closing the gold convertibility window (formally ending Bretton Woods), imposing a 10% import surcharge, and freezing wages and prices for 90 days — the "Nixon Shock." Through September–November, fear of an undefined Phase II of controls drove the Dow down to 791. The picture cleared in late November, and on December 18 the Group of Ten signed the Smithsonian Agreement — devaluing the dollar 7.89% against gold and 10% on average against the G10 currencies and rescinding the 10% import surcharge. The Dow finished at 890 (+6.1%), with the S&P 500 +10.79%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 7
Fed discount rate cut from 5.5% to 5.25%
First cut of the year, kicking off three consecutive Q1 cuts.
Jan 18
Fed discount rate cut from 5.25% to 5.0%
Second cut of the year. On Jan 28 the Fed extended the emergency credit facility for non-member banks.
Feb 12
Fed discount rate cut from 5.0% to 4.75%
Third cut of the year. The 3-month Treasury yield broke below 4.5%, 4.0%, and 3.5% in January and February.
Mar 15
Dow Jones Industrial Average breaks the 900 "Maginot Line"
A round-number level that had repeatedly resisted breakout since 1966.
Apr 10–17
US–China Ping-Pong Diplomacy
The US table-tennis team, in Japan for the 31st World Championships in Nagoya, visited China at Beijing's invitation, opening the door to 22 years of frozen US–China relations.
May 5
Austrian, Belgian, Dutch, and Swiss central banks suspend FX trading
A signal event of the dollar crisis driven by persistent dollar inflows into Europe. On May 10 West Germany floated the mark; the Austrian schilling and Swiss franc revalued by 5% and 7.1% respectively.
Jun 10
US ends trade embargo on China
The comprehensive embargo in place since the Korean War was lifted.
Jul 9–11
Kissinger's secret visit to Beijing
National Security Advisor Henry Kissinger secretly visited Beijing for preparatory talks ahead of Nixon's planned trip to China.
Jul 15
Nixon announces China trip; Fed unexpectedly hikes
Nixon announced he would visit China, ending nearly a quarter-century of US–China hostility. On the same day, the Fed raised the discount rate from 4.75% to 5.0%, surprising markets.
Aug 15
Nixon announces the "New Economic Policy" (Nixon Shock)
Three measures: closing the gold convertibility window, formally ending Bretton Woods; signing Executive Order 11615 to freeze wages and prices for 90 days; imposing a 10% surcharge on imports.
Early Oct
Nixon delivers speech on new control policy
He addressed partial details of Phase II controls, which the market found disappointing.
Nov 10
Fed discount rate cut from 5.0% to 4.75%
The first reversal after July's surprise hike.
Nov 13
Phase II of Nixon's economic-control program announced
Wage growth capped at 5.5% per year and price growth at 2.5%.
Dec 3
Securities margin requirement cut from 65% to 55%
Further easing of monetary policy.
Dec 10
Fed discount rate cut from 4.75% to 4.5%
Fourth cut of the year (counting the post-July-hike reversal).
Dec 18
Group of Ten signs the Smithsonian Agreement
The dollar was devalued 7.89% against gold (official price $35 → $38/oz, the first devaluation since 1934); 10% on average against the G10; non-reserve currency bands widened from ±1% to ±2.25%; and the US 10% temporary import surcharge was removed.
