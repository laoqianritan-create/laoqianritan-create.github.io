Stagflation Warning Bell

Overview

Inflation breaks loose · Multiple Fed hikes · Vietnam war continues · December marks recession entry

1969 was the first year postwar inflation truly broke loose. Early in the year the commercial bank prime rate rose to 7%, a record high. Nixon was inaugurated on January 20, briefly raising peace-talk hopes. In mid-February Congress moved to restrict conglomerate mergers, while Treasury and the President's economic advisor both hinted at extending the 10% surtax and ending the 7% investment tax credit, signaling further monetary tightening — triggering a sharp sell-off.

On April 3 the Fed raised the discount rate from 5.5% to 6.0% and reserve requirements, and by end-April the investment tax credit was repealed. Paris peace-talk progress briefly supported the market, but Nixon's late-May televised address dashed peace hopes. From June to July, amid continuing rate and tax hikes, the Dow fell from 975 to 802, a decline of nearly 20%. Apollo 11 landed on the Moon on July 20, but with no fundamental impact; Nixon outlined the "Vietnamization" Nixon Doctrine in Guam on July 25. The Tax Reform Act of 1969 was signed September 4, introducing the Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT).

October's rising unemployment briefly raised hopes that hikes had peaked, with the Dow rebounding to 863. Nixon's "Silent Majority" speech on November 3 signaled Vietnam would not end soon; a 500,000-person anti-war march in Washington on November 15; rates broke previous highs again. NBER later confirmed the US economy formally entered recession in December 1969. The Dow closed at 800, down 15.2%; S&P 500 -11.36%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Mid-Jan
Commercial Bank Prime Rate Rises to 7%
Major commercial banks raised rates three times in five weeks, reaching a record high — a direct signal of inflation pressure.
Jan 20
Nixon Inaugurated as 37th President
Paris peace talks and "fighting while talking" in Vietnam began. Markets briefly hoped he would be a peacemaker.
Mid-Feb
Congress Begins Restricting Conglomerate M&A
Conglomerate mergers and related practices were restricted, with conglomerate stocks under sustained pressure.
Mid-Late Feb
Treasury and White House Advisor Issue Congressional Warning
Treasury Secretary Kennedy and Chief Economic Advisor McCracken signaled: the 10% surtax would be extended, the 7% investment credit might be ended, and monetary tightening would be needed. The Fed simultaneously indicated higher reserve requirements and discount rate.
Apr 3
Fed Discount Rate: 5.5% → 6.0%
With simultaneous reserve requirement hike. The main policy tightening of the year.
Late Apr
7% Investment Tax Credit Repealed
Corporate capex credit was eliminated, fulfilling earlier warnings.
May 20
Anti-War Protests Erupt in California
Protests subsequently spread to other regions. Nixon's televised address pushed peace agreement expectations further out.
Jun 8
Nixon Announces 25,000 Troop Withdrawal from Vietnam
Announced at Midway Island, to be completed by end-August — the first Nixon-administration troop withdrawal.
Early Jul
Congress Extends the 10% Surtax
Commercial bank prime rate rose to 8.5% in the same period as a wage-price inflation spiral emerged.
Jul 20-21
Apollo 11 Achieves First Human Moon Landing
The command module returned safely to the Pacific on July 24. Armstrong and Aldrin completed the first moonwalk.
Jul 24
Fed Revises Reserve Bank Reserve Rules
Included checks drawn on foreign banks as deposits for reserve requirement calculation, tightening related operations.
Jul 25
Nixon Unveils "New Asia Policy" in Guam
The Nixon Doctrine — US strategic contraction, "Vietnamization" of the war, Asians fighting Asians.
Aug 8
French Franc Devalues 12.5%
Another major currency revaluation under Bretton Woods.
Sep 4
Tax Reform Act of 1969 Signed
Targeted loopholes used by high-income taxpayers; introduced the Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT).
Sep
West Germany Forced to Close FX Market Amid Speculation
Massive international capital inflows triggered currency speculation forcing market closure; the mark was subsequently revalued upward.
Nov 3
Nixon's "Silent Majority" Televised Address
Announced "Vietnamization," making clear no immediate withdrawal would result from protests. Politically successful but signaling no quick end to the war. Rates subsequently broke prior highs.
Nov 15
Washington Anti-War Moratorium March
About 500,000 participants — one of the largest anti-war protests during the Vietnam War.
Dec
NBER Later Confirms US Recession Onset
Starting point: December 1969. "Stagflation" entered the mainstream economic lexicon as a new macroeconomic paradigm.
