Overview

Fiscal stimulus continues · Retail short-squeeze drama · "Transitory" narrative dropped at year-end

2021 opened with the Capitol attack; after Biden's inauguration the transfer of power proceeded. In late January, retail traders on WallStreetBets squeezed GameStop, blowing up hedge fund Melvin Capital and triggering the first serious discussion of the retail-vs-institutional balance of power. On Mar 11 the $1.9 trillion American Rescue Plan landed, providing $1,400 direct payments and—on top of two prior rounds of transfers and ultra-low rates—leaving households flush with cash.

In March, hidden leverage at Archegos Capital blew up, with major writedowns at Credit Suisse and Nomura but no systemic event. In May the Colonial Pipeline was hit by ransomware, causing East Coast fuel shortages; CPI for April printed 4.2% and the "transitory inflation" narrative started to come under pressure. The June FOMC dot plot turned hawkish unexpectedly. In August US forces left Afghanistan and Kabul fell. In September China's Evergrande debt crisis rattled cross-border markets, but overseas spillover was limited. In October Facebook rebranded to Meta and the metaverse narrative took over; in November the $1.2 trillion IIJA infrastructure bill was signed.

The turn came in late November: after being re-nominated, Powell told Congress to "stop using the word 'transitory' to describe inflation", anchoring the policy pivot. In December the FOMC accelerated Taper; the Omicron variant caused a brief dip but did not change the trend. The S&P 500 finished +26.89% on the year—one of the strongest bull years of the new century—but year-end valuations were tightly stretched against the coming tightening cycle.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 6
Storming of the Capitol
Trump supporters breach the Capitol and disrupt the Electoral College certification—a symbolic high point of US political polarization.
Jan 20
Biden sworn in as 46th US President
The executive transition completes; Democrats hold the White House and both chambers of Congress, paving the way for major fiscal legislation.
Jan 27–28
GameStop retail short-squeeze
WallStreetBets retail traders squeeze hedge fund Melvin Capital; Robinhood restricts buying. The retail-vs-institutional dynamic enters mainstream conversation.
Feb 13
Senate acquits Trump in second impeachment
The 57–43 vote falls short of the two-thirds threshold; Trump is acquitted.
Mar 11
$1.9 trillion American Rescue Plan signed
Third round of pandemic relief: $1,400 direct payments per adult, extended unemployment insurance, and an expanded child tax credit.
Mar 23–26
Archegos Capital hidden-leverage blow-up
A family office amasses huge exposures via total-return swaps; Credit Suisse and Nomura take heavy losses, but the event stays non-systemic.
Apr
Bitcoin breaks $60,000 for the first time
In the week Coinbase directly lists on Nasdaq, bitcoin briefly approaches $65,000; crypto enters mainstream narrative.
May 7
Colonial Pipeline hit by ransomware
The largest US East Coast refined-products pipeline is offline for days, causing fuel shortages in multiple states; the operator pays a roughly $4.4 million ransom.
May 12
April CPI prints 4.2%
The highest year-on-year reading since 2008; the market formally takes notice of inflation risk and the "transitory" narrative begins to be questioned.
Jun 16
June FOMC dot plot turns hawkish unexpectedly
The median FOMC participant pulls forward expected 2023 hikes to two, well ahead of March projections; the market re-prices the policy turn.
Jul 20–21
Blue Origin and Virgin Galactic commercial crewed flights
Bezos and Branson complete suborbital flights; private space tourism enters public view.
Aug 15
Taliban take Kabul; US forces withdraw from Afghanistan
The US ends a nearly 20-year war; the chaotic withdrawal draws broad foreign-policy criticism of the Biden administration.
Sep 15
China's Evergrande debt crisis becomes public
Evergrande dollar-bond default risk produces a brief global risk-off; direct US-equity spillover is limited, but it marks the turning point of China's property cycle.
Oct 28
Facebook parent renamed to Meta
Zuckerberg announces an all-in bet on the metaverse; the narrative shifts from social platforms to long-term VR/AR investment.
Nov 15
$1.2 trillion Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act signed
A 10-year federal infrastructure law covering roads, bridges, broadband, the grid, and EV charging.
Nov 22
Biden re-nominates Powell as Fed Chair
Brainard is nominated as Vice Chair; policy continuity is confirmed and short-term policy expectations stabilize.
Nov 30
Powell testifies: stop using "transitory" to describe inflation
Monetary-policy language formally turns, paving the way for accelerated Taper and earlier hikes—the anchor point of this tightening cycle.
Dec
Omicron variant spreads globally
First reported in South Africa; markedly more transmissible than Delta but less severe, causing short-term supply-chain and services disruption.
Dec 15
FOMC accelerates Taper; dot plot signals three 2022 hikes
Doubles the monthly Taper pace to $30 billion; markets price an imminent 2022 hike cycle.
