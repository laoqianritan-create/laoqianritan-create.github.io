Overview

Fundamentals-driven global new-year rally · Fed pivots to cuts in June · Oct 13 failed LBO ignites junk bond panic

1989 was the year markets reclaimed all-time highs after the 1987 crash and the opening of the "building momentum" era. George H.W. Bush was inaugurated in January and the Fed continued tightening. The PMI remained in expansion, employment improved, and a strong dollar attracted overseas inflows; the 1989 "new-year rally" was global, with London, Tokyo, and Frankfurt advancing in sync.

The Fed hiked three consecutive times in February and raised the discount rate, which combined with trade deficit concerns triggered an approximately 4% February pullback. March–May saw a stable, fundamentals-led advance; in June, PMI fell below the expansion threshold and markets feared a hard landing, with a roughly 3% pullback. In early July, less than a month after the prior hike, the Fed pivoted to easing, cutting rates five times by year-end and bringing fed funds back to 8.25%. The Dow broke through 2,722, the pre-'87-crash peak, in late August.

Real tension came in October. On October 13, "Mini Black Friday," United Airlines' $6.75 billion LBO financing failed, igniting junk bond panic; the S&P 500 fell 6.13% in a single session. The event followed Canadian Campeau retail empire's September default, intensifying the junk bond credit crunch. On October 19, the Fed surprised with easing, followed by cuts on November 28 and December 20. Full year, the Dow and S&P 500 both rose about 27% and Nasdaq +19%, collectively recovering 1987-crash losses.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
George H.W. Bush inaugurated as 41st President
George H.W. Bush succeeded Reagan in the White House, beginning the Republican Party's third consecutive term.
Feb 9 / 14 / 24
Fed hikes three consecutive times in February and raises the discount rate
Fed funds target raised on February 9, 14, and 24, with the discount rate adjusted in parallel.
Mar 24
Exxon Valdez oil tanker runs aground in Alaska
Approximately 11 million gallons of crude were spilled — the worst offshore oil pollution incident in US history at the time, triggering federal marine environmental legislation.
Jun 4
China Tiananmen Square incident
US-China relations cooled abruptly; the US announced sanctions on China. One of the pivotal late-Cold War political nodes.
Mid-Jun to Early Jul
Fed halts tightening and pivots to easing
Less than a month after the prior hike, the fed funds target was cut, marking the end of the 1988-89 hiking cycle. Five cuts followed through year-end.
Aug 9
George H.W. Bush signs FIRREA (Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act)
Established the Resolution Trust Corporation (RTC), responsible for disposing of failed S&L assets, formally institutionalizing the S&L crisis resolution.
Mid-Sep
Canadian Campeau retail empire faces financial distress
Robert Campeau failed to make a maturing interest payment; junk bond spreads rose from 500bp to 700bp, accelerating outflows from junk bond funds.
Fri, Oct 13
UAL Corp's $6.75 billion LBO financing fails
The bank syndicate refused to lend for the UAL management/pilot union LBO, igniting junk bond market panic on the heels of the Campeau event — dubbed "Mini Black Friday."
Oct 19
Fed surprise cut to fed funds target
Just one week after the October 13 crash, the Fed injected liquidity to stabilize credit markets.
Nov 9
The Berlin Wall falls
East Germany opened the border, fundamentally loosening the Cold War order and presaging German reunification (1990) and Soviet dissolution (1991).
Dec
KKR completes the RJR Nabisco LBO
Total consideration approximately $25 billion, the largest LBO in the world at the time and the subject of "Barbarians at the Gate."
Dec 20
US launches "Operation Just Cause" invasion of Panama
Overthrew and captured Panamanian leader Manuel Noriega — a major late-Cold War US military operation in the Western Hemisphere. The same day, the Fed cut rates again to 8.25%.
