Overview

$850/oz gold peak · A six-month recession · Reagan wins · Lennon shot

1980 was the closing chapter of the 13-year stagflation, and the most dazzling asset of the year was not stocks but gold, which touched $850/oz on January 21 — a nominal high that would not be recovered for over two decades, finally surpassed in 2007. CPI ran high all year (peaking at 14.8%), and the Fed oscillated between inflation and recession, raising the discount rate three times and cutting it three times — almost a full policy cycle.

The Dow extended early gains to 904 on February 13. On February 15 the Fed lifted the discount rate from 12% to 13%, and the rally rolled over. The Dow fell to 760 on March 27 (the same day as "Silver Thursday," when the Hunt brothers' silver squeeze blew up), a drawdown of 16%. From late April to mid-August, falling inflation and rapidly declining rates produced a 27% rally. From September, the Iran–Iraq War broke out, oil rebounded, and the Fed re-entered a hiking cycle.

On November 4, Reagan defeated Carter with 489 electoral votes; the post-election rally pushed the Dow above 1,000 for the first time in four years on November 20. But after further discount-rate hikes in November and December back to 13%, the prime rate climbed to 21.5% by year-end and the index gave back gains. The Dow closed at 964, +14.9% for the year; the S&P 500 finished +25.77%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 21
London spot gold touches all-time high of $850/oz
The Hunt brothers' silver-squeeze frenzy ran in parallel. This nominal high would only be recovered 27 years later.
Feb 15
Fed discount rate 12% → 13%
A 100 bp move. Over the next six weeks the prime rate was raised nine times in a row, from 15.25% to 20.0%.
Mar 27
"Silver Thursday" — the Hunt brothers blow up
Silver crashed in a single day, and Bache Securities was pushed to the brink of failure when the Hunts could not meet margin calls. COMEX had previously changed its margin rules.
Apr 24
"Operation Eagle Claw" hostage-rescue mission fails
Eight US military helicopter crew were killed; Secretary of State Vance resigned. The Carter administration's credibility took another blow.
May 28
Fed discount rate 13% → 12%
First cut of the year. Further cuts to 11% on June 12 and 10% on July 25 — three cuts in three months.
Jun 3
NBER formally confirms recession from January 1980
The seventh postwar NBER recession (starting January 1980). It lasted only six months, bottoming in July 1980.
Jul
Crude Oil Windfall Profit Tax Act of 1980 takes effect
Imposed a "windfall" tax on domestic crude producers, paired with the phased removal of price controls. Repealed in 1988.
Sep 22
Iran–Iraq War breaks out
Oil production in both countries halted, sending crude prices up again. Three days later, the Fed resumed hiking, discount rate 10% → 11%.
Sep 25
Fed discount rate 10% → 11%
First hike of the second leg after inflation pressures returned. Further hikes to 12% on November 14 and 13% on December 4 — three consecutive hikes again.
Nov 4
Reagan defeats Carter, elected 40th US president
Reagan won 44 states and 489 electoral votes — the highest total ever for a non-incumbent. Republicans also retook the Senate. The supply-side and conservative agenda took the stage.
Dec 4
Fed discount rate back to 13%
Third hike since September. By year-end the prime rate reached 21.5%, surpassing the 20% peak in April.
Dec 8
John Lennon shot dead outside his New York apartment
Mark David Chapman shot Lennon outside the Dakota; the 40-year-old former Beatle was killed at the scene. A defining cultural icon of an era was lost.
