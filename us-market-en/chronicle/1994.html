1994
Chronicle · Inaugural Year of Globalization and the Internet

The Great Bond Market Crash

S&P 500 -1.54% Dow Jones +2.14% Year-End 3834 Main Theme Fed hikes 6x + Bond crash + Netscape birth
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

First Fed hike in 5 years · 6 consecutive hikes totaling 250bp · Global bond crash · Netscape and NAFTA debut same year

1994 was the year of the Fed's first hike in five years. On February 4, the Fed surprised with a 25bp hike, breaking the easing expectations that had supported the prior three-year bull. The Fed hiked six consecutive times for 250bp on the year, including a 75bp move on November 15 — 25bp larger than expected. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from 5.7% at year-start to over 8% by year-end, with short-end moves materially exceeding long-end and the curve inverting.

The bond market took the brunt: per Fortune estimates, US bond markets lost ~$600 billion from year-start to mid-September, and global bond markets shed $1.5 trillion on the year — one of the 1990s' most famous financial storms. Two iconic derivative events: on December 6, Orange County, California, filed for bankruptcy at $1.64 billion — the largest US municipal bankruptcy at the time; on December 19, the Mexican peso devalued 15%, igniting the Peso Crisis and emerging-market capital flight.

On the equity side: S&P 500 -1.54% on the year, Dow +2.14%, Nasdaq -3%. Earnings shone (Q3 GDP growth 4.3%, Q1 earnings far beat expectations) but were offset by tightening and bond shocks. Three landscape-shifting events in 1994: NAFTA took effect January 1; Netscape Navigator 1.0 launched October 13, opening the commercial internet era; the November 8 midterm elections saw a Republican "Contract with America" landslide, and the Senate majority changed hands for the first time in 40 years.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 1
NAFTA takes effect · Zapatista uprising
Zero-tariff trade among the US, Canada, and Mexico began. The same day, the Zapatista peasant uprising erupted in Chiapas, southern Mexico, lighting a fuse for the year-end peso crisis.
Feb 4
Fed hikes 25bp (3.0% → 3.25%)
First hike in five years, breaking easing expectations that had supported the prior three-year bull. Fed hiked 6 times totaling 250bp on the year.
Mar 22
Fed hikes 25bp (3.25% → 3.5%)
Second hike of the year. The same day, Mexican presidential candidate Colosio was assassinated in Tijuana — the first murder of a Mexican presidential candidate in decades.
Apr 22
Former President Richard Nixon dies
At age 81. State funeral in Yorba Linda, California, on April 27, attended by five former and the incumbent president.
May 6
Channel Tunnel opens
Queen Elizabeth II and French President Mitterrand jointly presided over the opening. The same month, the W3C World Wide Web Consortium was established at MIT.
May 17
Fed hikes 50bp (3.75% → 4.25%)
Fourth hike of the year; the increment moved from 25bp to 50bp.
Jul 8
North Korean leader Kim Il-sung dies
At age 82, after ruling North Korea for nearly half a century. His son Kim Jong-il succeeded him.
Jul 17
15th FIFA World Cup closes in the US
Brazil defeated Italy on penalties at the Rose Bowl in Los Angeles for the title; the US hosted the World Cup for the first time.
Aug 16
Fed hikes 50bp (4.25% → 4.75%)
Fifth hike of the year.
Oct 13
Netscape launches Navigator 1.0
Mosaic Communications was renamed Netscape Communications and launched the first commercial browser version. The commercial internet era began.
Nov 8
Midterm elections: Republican "Contract with America" landslide
Led by Newt Gingrich, House Republicans netted 54 seats to retake the majority; the Senate majority changed hands for the first time in 40 years. Clinton entered a "lame duck" condition.
Nov 15
Fed hikes 75bp (4.75% → 5.5%)
Sixth hike of the year, the size 25bp above market expectations; long-end yields broke above 8%.
Dec 6
Orange County, California files for bankruptcy
$1.64 billion in size, the largest US municipal bankruptcy at the time. Its investment pool blew up after holding inverse derivatives heavily into the rising-rate environment.
Dec 20
Mexican peso devaluation → Peso Crisis
Late on December 19, the Mexican government announced a 15% peso devaluation. From December 20-22, the peso plunged 42.17% against the dollar over three days; foreign capital fled and the emerging market crisis spread (the "Tequila Effect").
