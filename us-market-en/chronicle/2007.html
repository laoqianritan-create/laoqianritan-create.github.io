Chronicle · Bull-Market Endgame · Crisis Approaches

Subprime Crisis Goes Global

S&P 500 +3.53% Dow Jones +6.4% Year-End 13265 Main Theme Subprime Cracks + Top + Easing Cycle
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Subprime cracks surface · Oct peak at 1565 · Global central banks open the easing cycle

2007 was the final chapter of the long US bull market. Early-year retail, PMI, and earnings beat expectations and the S&P 500 ground higher. In February HSBC took $1.8 billion of US subprime loan-loss provisions, New Century Financial issued an earnings warning, and on Feb 27 global equities posted their biggest drop since 9/11—the first public surfacing of subprime risk. After an upbeat mid-March FOMC, the market climbed again into mid-July, with headline data still masking falling home prices and a deteriorating credit backdrop.

On Jul 10 S&P downgraded a large slice of subprime MBS; in late July two Bear Stearns hedge funds were liquidated. On Aug 9 BNP Paribas froze redemptions on three of its funds; funding markets in the US and Europe seized, and the global financial crisis officially began. From Aug 10, the Fed, BoJ, and ECB injected liquidity in series; on Sep 18 the Fed cut 50 bp—its first cut in four years—to 4.75%, and the S&P 500 rallied to a top of 1565 on Oct 9.

In October surging oil, weakening jobs, and financial-sector losses triggered a pullback that bottomed Nov 26. On Dec 11 the Fed cut again to 4.25%; Citi, Merrill, and other large banks disclosed massive subprime losses around year-end. The S&P 500 closed at 1468, +3.53%—superficially calm, but with internal cracks in the financial system already beyond repair.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 9
Apple unveils the original iPhone
Steve Jobs introduces the iPhone at Macworld, redefining the smartphone and the mobile-internet endpoint.
Feb 8
HSBC issues subprime earnings warning
HSBC adds $1.8 billion to subprime loan-loss provisions; New Century Financial issues a Q4 2006 earnings warning—the first public signal of the subprime crisis.
Feb 27
"2/27 global sell-off"
A sharp drop in Shanghai triggers a global chain reaction across Asia-Pacific, Europe, Latin America, and South Africa. Subprime risk surfaces publicly for the first time.
Apr 2
New Century Financial files for bankruptcy
The second-largest US subprime lender files for bankruptcy after laying off half its workforce.
Jun
Two Bear Stearns subprime hedge funds blow up
Two Bear Stearns hedge funds tied to subprime mortgages are on the verge of collapse—the first time Wall Street investment banks are pulled in.
Jul 10
S&P and Moody's downgrade large blocks of subprime MBS
The two major rating agencies cut subprime MBS ratings en bloc; US and global equities begin declining in unison the next day.
Jul 31
Bear Stearns hedge funds liquidated
On Aug 8 Bear Stearns formally announces the funds' collapse; the subprime storm spreads from mortgage originators into the investment-bank system.
Aug 9
BNP Paribas freezes redemptions on three funds
France's largest bank freezes three funds exposed to US subprime debt; European equities tumble—the global financial crisis officially begins.
Aug 10
Fed, BoJ, ECB inject emergency liquidity
From Aug 10–14, the Fed, BoJ, and ECB inject liquidity into the banking system; on Aug 17 the Fed cuts the discount rate 50 bp to 5.75%.
Sep 18
Fed cuts fed funds 50 bp to 4.75%
The first cut after 17 hikes over four years—opens what becomes a 15-month easing cycle.
Oct 9
S&P 500 peaks at 1565
The high of the long bull cycle that began in 2002. Financial-sector earnings then deteriorate, oil rallies, and labor data softens.
Oct/Dec
Fed cuts in October and December
Oct 31: 25 bp cut to 4.5%; Dec 11: another 25 bp cut to 4.25%. Cumulative cuts on the year: 100 bp.
Late Dec
Citi, Merrill, and other large banks announce massive subprime losses
Morgan Stanley and Merrill post heavy Q3 losses; Citi and Merrill disclose subprime write-downs around year-end. Financial risk spreads into the real economy.
Mar
Ron Paul announces 2008 presidential campaign
Texas Congressman Ron Paul formally enters the GOP primary on an anti-war, anti-Fed, return-to-the-gold-standard platform.
