Overview

Tax Reduction Act enacted · Civil Rights Act signed · Vietnam authorization and re-election

1964 was the Johnson administration's first full year. The January 8 State of the Union set the year's policy tone with the "War on Poverty"; the Revenue Act of 1964 was signed on February 26, cutting the top corporate income tax rate from 52% to 48% and the top individual rate from 91% to 70%. The continuing bull market and aggressive fiscal policy drove a near-uninterrupted rally from January through mid-April.

From early May to early June, the Dow pulled back from 830 to 800, triggered by a Supreme Court ruling against bank mergers, government price intervention in steel, railway-strike threats, SEC insider-trading enforcement, and concerns over a 39-month-old expansion. A summer rally began in late June, and the Civil Rights Act of 1964 was signed on July 2, prohibiting racial discrimination under federal law.

The Gulf of Tonkin incident on August 4 and the resulting Gulf of Tonkin Resolution authorized the use of force, formally escalating the Vietnam War; in October Khrushchev was deposed by Brezhnev and China tested its first atomic bomb. Johnson won re-election on November 3, and in mid-November the Dow spiked to a year-to-date high of 897. On November 24 the Fed raised the discount rate from 3.5% to 4.0%; the index drifted modestly lower into year-end, with the Dow closing at 874.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 8
Johnson's First State of the Union
The "War on Poverty" speech pledged civil-rights legislation, tax cuts, and an all-out attack on domestic poverty and unemployment.
Jan 9
Panama Canal Zone Clash · US-Panama Diplomatic Break
US forces clashed with Panamanian protesters in the Canal Zone; on January 11 Panama announced a diplomatic break and abrogated the Canal Zone treaty.
Feb 26
Revenue Act of 1964 Signed
Top corporate income tax rate cut from 52% to 48%, top individual rate from 91% to 70%; estimated to reduce federal income tax revenue by nearly 20%.
Early Feb
GM Reports Record Earnings for a US Company
General Motors reported revenue and profit figures setting all-time records for any US public company at the time.
Late May
Supreme Court Rules Against Bank Mergers
Combined with steel price restrictions, railway strike threats, and SEC insider-trading enforcement, formed a cluster of negative catalysts in May-June.
Jul 2
Civil Rights Act of 1964 Signed
Prohibited racial discrimination in employment, public accommodations, union membership, and federally funded programs. One of the most important pieces of civil-rights legislation in 20th-century America.
Aug 4
Gulf of Tonkin Incident · Gulf of Tonkin Resolution
US destroyers were allegedly attacked again by North Vietnamese forces in the Gulf of Tonkin. Congress passed the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution authorizing presidential use of force in Southeast Asia, formally escalating the Vietnam War.
Oct 14
Khrushchev Deposed · Brezhnev Takes Power
Khrushchev was removed in a Moscow coup led by Brezhnev while on holiday on the Black Sea, ending his rule over the Soviet Union.
Oct 16
China Successfully Tests First Atomic Bomb
Following the US, USSR, UK, and France, China became the world's fifth nuclear weapons state, reshaping the Cold War nuclear order.
Nov 3
Johnson Wins Re-election
Lyndon Johnson defeated Republican Senator Goldwater, confirming continued aggressive fiscal policy and the "Great Society" agenda.
Nov 24
Fed Discount Rate: 3.5% → 4.0%
First rate hike of the year, in response to overheating and international balance-of-payments pressure amid the sterling crisis.
