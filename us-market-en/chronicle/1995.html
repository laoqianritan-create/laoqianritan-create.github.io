1995
Chronicle · Starting Point of the Internet Long Bull

The Soft Landing

S&P 500 +34.11% Dow Jones +33.5% Year-End Dow 5117 Main Theme Soft landing + Rate cuts + Internet commercialization
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Soft landing realized · Yields fall from 7.9% to below 6% · First year of internet commercialization

1995 carried over from the 1994 six-hike cycle's tail. The 10-year Treasury yield peaked in November 1994 and headed lower; early-year markets juggled monetary-tightening concerns with the Mexican peso crisis. In January, the Clinton administration announced a ~$50 billion multilateral aid package, capping external risks. On February 1 the Fed completed the cycle's seventh hike (+50bp); on February 23 Greenspan signaled an end to hikes, and the soft landing narrative was formally established.

H2 saw a macro pivot: from May, PMI fell below the expansion threshold; the Fed began an easing cycle on July 6 and cut again on December 19, taking the fed funds rate from 6.0% to 5.25%. On August 9, Netscape's IPO surged from $28 to $75 on day one, viewed as the dawn of internet commercialization. On August 24, Windows 95 launched globally, pushing consumer PCs to another level. Earnings maintained 20%+ growth throughout the year, with fundamentals and falling rates jointly supporting the index.

Politically, two end-year federal government shutdowns (the December one ran 21 days, a record) briefly rattled sentiment. Full-year GDP grew 2.7%, delivering the soft landing. Dow closed at 5,117, the best gain since 1975; S&P 500 +34.11%, the highest annual gain since 1958; index volatility was extremely low and the price curve unusually smooth.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 1
World Trade Organization (WTO) formally established
Replaced the GATT in place since 1947. The global multilateral trade system entered a new phase.
Jan
Mexican peso crisis · US extends $50 billion aid
Treasury Secretary Rubin provided $18 billion in international credit lines first; the Clinton administration then added a multilateral aid package, totaling approximately $50 billion.
Feb 1
Fed hikes 50 basis points
The seventh and final hike of this tightening cycle; fed funds rate reached 6.0%.
Feb 26
UK's Barings Bank collapses
Trader Nick Leeson's unauthorized bets on Nikkei derivatives caused $1.4 billion in losses, bankrupting the 233-year-old bank.
Apr 19
Oklahoma City Federal Building bombing
Domestic extremists bombed the Murrah Federal Building, killing 168 — the worst terrorist attack on US soil before 9/11.
Late Apr
USD/JPY falls to 79.75 · US-Japan joint intervention
The dollar hit a postwar low against the yen; US-Japan monetary authorities intervened jointly, and the dollar index subsequently stabilized.
Jul 6
Fed cuts 25 basis points
Fed funds rate cut from 6.0% to 5.75%, formally opening the easing cycle.
Aug 9
Netscape Communications IPO
Offering price $28, first-day close $58, intraday high $75. Widely viewed as the starting point of internet commercialization and an iconic event in the 1990s Nasdaq long bull.
Aug 24
Microsoft launches Windows 95
Introduced the "Start" menu, plug-and-play, 32-bit multitasking, pushing consumer PCs to another level. Over 1 million units sold in the first four days.
Nov 21
Dayton Accords · Bosnian War ends
At Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Dayton, Ohio, Bosnia, Croatia, and Serbia reached a peace agreement ending the nearly four-year Bosnian War.
Nov 14
First federal government shutdown (5 days)
The Clinton administration and Republican-controlled Congress deadlocked on budget and debt-ceiling negotiations; the fiscal apparatus shut down for 5 days.
Dec 16
Second federal government shutdown (21 days)
President Clinton and House Speaker Gingrich deadlocked on the budget; the shutdown lasted to January 5, 1996, setting a record for longest shutdown at the time.
Dec 19
Fed cuts another 25 basis points
Fed funds rate cut from 5.75% to 5.5%. Two cumulative cuts on the year.
