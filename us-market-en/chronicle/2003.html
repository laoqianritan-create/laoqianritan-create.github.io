Overview

Iraq War + tax-cut bill enacted + fed funds rate cut to 1%

2003 was the most important pivot year for US equities after the dot-com bust. Fundamentals improved rapidly at the start of the year: ISM Manufacturing PMI crossed back above 50, new orders jumped, and the Dow rose 265 points on the first trading day. From mid-January, however, expectations of war in Iraq and weak earnings outlooks weighed on the market; the S&P 500 fell roughly 9% year-to-date and bottomed near 800 on Mar 11.

On Mar 20, US and UK forces launched the Iraq War, bypassing the UN; oil dropped from $38/bbl to $24/bbl by early May, flipping market sentiment. Q1 corporate earnings surged 122% year-on-year with ROE of 7%, and on May 28 Bush signed the Jobs and Growth Tax Relief Reconciliation Act (JGTRRA), cutting dividend and capital-gains taxes to 15%. On Jun 25 the Fed cut fed funds from 1.25% to 1.0%, the lowest since 1961; the S&P rallied 26% from Mar 11 to Jun 17.

Mid-June to early August was a consolidation: the unemployment rate climbed to 6.3%, a high since 1994; anti-US sentiment rose in post-war Iraq; and Treasury yields spiked. From August on, industrial production expanded and the unemployment rate rolled over, and the market resumed its advance almost uninterrupted into year-end. On Dec 13 Saddam Hussein was captured. The S&P 500 closed at 1111, up 26.38% for the year—its best since 1998. Year-end FOMC language shifted, signaling that a hiking cycle was no longer far off.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Feb 1
Space Shuttle Columbia disintegrates on re-entry
The Shuttle breaks apart on re-entry, killing all seven astronauts on board. The US Shuttle program is grounded for an extended period.
Feb 5
Powell addresses the UN Security Council
Secretary of State Colin Powell presents evidence of Iraqi WMD, paving the way for the use of force. War expectations build.
Mar 10
SARS outbreak goes global
The WHO issues a global alert on Mar 12. Asian equities, airlines, and travel come under pressure.
Mar 20
US and UK launch the Iraq War
Coalition forces act without UN authorization. After the war starts, oil drops from $38/bbl to $24/bbl by early May.
Apr 9
Fall of Baghdad
US forces enter Baghdad and Saddam's regime collapses; major combat operations wind down.
May 1
Bush "Mission Accomplished" speech
Bush declares an end to major combat operations from the USS Abraham Lincoln. Iraq subsequently enters an extended insurgency phase.
May 28
Bush signs the JGTRRA tax-cut bill
A $350 billion package that cuts dividend and long-term capital-gains tax rates to 15%, accelerating fund flows into equities.
Jun 25
Fed cuts 1.25% → 1.0%
Fed funds target hits the lowest since 1961—the trough of this easing cycle.
Aug 14
Northeast US blackout
A cascading grid failure across the US Northeast and parts of Canada leaves more than 50 million people without power—the largest blackout in North American history.
Dec 13
Saddam Hussein captured near Tikrit
US forces capture Saddam in a cellar near Tikrit. Post-war Iraqi politics enters a new phase.
