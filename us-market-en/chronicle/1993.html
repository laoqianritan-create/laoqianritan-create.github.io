Overview

First year of Clinton's "new deal" · Tax hikes/deficit reduction alongside low rates · NAFTA enacted

1993 was the first year of Democratic control of the White House and the true start of the 1990s long bull. Clinton was inaugurated on January 20, and on February 17 he addressed a joint session of Congress with his first State of the Union, formally introducing the Economic Recovery Plan — centered on "shifting from consumption to investment, dramatic federal deficit reduction." Provisions raising corporate tax rates and White House staffing cuts appeared together. The plan was the principal catalyst for the early-to-late-February market correction; markets worried tax hikes and possible higher rates would suppress corporate earnings.

March saw a brief inflation pickup and Fed signals of no further cuts, triggering the year's second pullback, with Nasdaq reaching a new low by late April. From late April onward, however, fundamentals took over: long-end yields fell steadily, with the 30-year Treasury yield falling to 6.67% on June 28 — a 16-year low; corporate earnings continued to beat, and mutual fund inflows surged. On August 10, Clinton signed the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1993 (OBRA-93), committing to $496 billion in deficit reduction over five years, and the federal deficit entered a decade-long downtrend.

In September, Yeltsin dissolved the Russian parliament; the early-October Moscow bloodshed brought brief external shocks, but pullbacks were limited. On November 17, NAFTA passed the House 234-200, with the Senate following in early December; the Dow broke 3,700 for the first time. The Fed held rates steady all year, and the combination of fiscal tightening plus monetary easing set the stage for the next year's "bond market storm." Year-end Dow closed at 3,754, +13.7% on the year, S&P 500 +7.06%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
Bill Clinton inaugurated as 42nd US President
Democrats returned to the White House after 12 years of Republican rule; Gore as VP. Campaign platform centered on economic revival and deficit reduction.
Feb 17
Clinton formally proposes the Economic Recovery Plan
First State of the Union to a joint session of Congress. Four planks: shift from consumption to investment; decisions favoring work and family; substantial federal deficit reduction; cut government spending and fairly share the burden. Provisions included White House staff cuts and higher corporate tax rates.
Feb 26
New York World Trade Center bombing
Terrorists detonated a truck bomb in the WTC North Tower's underground garage, killing 6 and injuring over 1,000. The first major Islamic extremist attack on US soil.
Apr 19
Waco Branch Davidian standoff ends in fire
The 51-day FBI standoff with the Branch Davidians ended on April 19 in a raid that set the compound ablaze, killing approximately 76 members including 25 children. The event later catalyzed the 1995 Oklahoma City bombing.
Aug 10
Clinton signs the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1993
OBRA-93. Committed to $496 billion in deficit reduction over five years: top marginal individual rate raised from 31% to 39.6%, corporate rates raised, gasoline tax raised, expanded social benefits taxation; passed the Senate 51-50 with VP Gore casting the tiebreaker. The US federal deficit entered a decade-long downtrend.
Sep 13
Israel-PLO sign the Oslo Accords
On the White House South Lawn under Clinton's witness, Rabin and Arafat shook hands. Mutual recognition, limited autonomy, final status negotiations within five years. A landmark moment in the Middle East peace process.
Sep 21
Yeltsin dissolves Russian parliament
Presidential Decree No. 1400. Bloodshed in Moscow on September 23; Yeltsin declared a state of emergency on October 3, followed by the "October Events" in which tanks shelled the parliament building.
Nov 17
North American Free Trade Agreement passes the House
Passed 234-200. The agreement initialed by Bush, Mulroney, and Salinas in 1992; Clinton secured passage by adding labor and environmental side agreements over Democratic intra-party opposition.
Nov 20
Senate passes NAFTA 61-38
The agreement was set to take effect January 1, 1994, establishing the US-Canada-Mexico free trade area. Companion labor and environmental side agreements were also enacted.
Dec 8
Clinton signs NAFTA implementing legislation
The agreement took effect January 1 the following year. 1993 became the first year of an absolute decline in the US federal deficit in 12 years.
