Overview

Government shutdown ends · Telecom Act signed · Clinton reelected · Greenspan first invokes "irrational exuberance" in December

1996's first event was the end of the record 21-day government shutdown on January 6, as the Clinton administration and Congress reached an interim agreement on a seven-year balanced budget framework. On January 31, the Fed cut the fed funds rate from 5.25% to 5.0% — the only cut of the year. On February 8, Clinton signed the Telecommunications Act of 1996, paving the way for M&A and expansion in the internet and telecom industries.

From March, markets entered range trading; by late May, all three indices had recorded 10%+ gains. In early July, the 10-year Treasury yield broke 7% and June unemployment hit a new low since 1988, triggering panic about Fed hikes; meanwhile Q2 earnings growth fell to 9.4%, ending the prior 14 quarters of double-digit growth, with the S&P 500 and Dow pulling back about 7% and Nasdaq about 16%. From late July, rate-hike fears eased and the three indices resumed gains; rates peaked in early September and fell about 70bp into year-end.

On November 5, Clinton won reelection; the Dow broke 6,000 for the first time in October. On December 5, Greenspan introduced "irrational exuberance" in his AEI speech; the three indices fell sharply briefly but resumed their advance from mid-December. Full year: S&P 500 closed at 740.74 (+20.26%), Dow +26%, Nasdaq +23%; GDP grew 3.7%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 6
Federal government shutdown ends
The 21-day shutdown set the longest record at the time. The Clinton administration and Congress reached an interim framework on a 7-year balanced budget, reopening the government.
Jan 31
Fed cuts 5.25% → 5.0%
25bp cut. The only cut of the year.
Feb 8
Clinton signs the Telecommunications Act of 1996
The largest rewrite of telecom legislation in 62 years. Broke down long-distance/local and telecom/cable industry barriers, providing the institutional foundation for internet and telecom M&A and expansion.
Mar
United Auto Workers strike
The strike paralyzed 24 North American plants for nearly three weeks.
Apr 16
"Strategic Defense Initiative" renamed "Ballistic Missile Defense"
The Reagan-era "Star Wars" framework was formally renamed and repositioned as a limited defense program targeting regional ballistic missile threats.
Jul 19 – Aug 4
Atlanta Summer Olympics
The 26th Summer Olympics. On the morning of July 27, a bombing in Centennial Olympic Park killed 2 and injured over 100.
Aug 22
Clinton signs the Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act
The "welfare reform act" replaced the 60-year-old federal Aid to Families with Dependent Children (AFDC) with the time-limited, work-linked TANF program. Regarded as signature legislation of the "New Democrat" agenda.
Sep 30
Omnibus budget bill signed
The FY1997 omnibus appropriations bill was signed, averting another shutdown and continuing the 7-year balanced budget negotiations.
Nov 5
Clinton wins reelection
Defeated Republican candidate Bob Dole, becoming the first Democratic president to win reelection since FDR. Republicans retained control of both chambers of Congress.
Dec 5
Greenspan first invokes "irrational exuberance"
In a dinner address at the American Enterprise Institute (AEI), he asked: "How do we know when irrational exuberance has unduly escalated asset values?" — referencing a decade of Japanese asset contraction, signaling concern about equity and bond bubbles.
Dec
OPEC raises oil prices
OPEC raised official prices at year-end, disrupting inflation expectations into the following year.
