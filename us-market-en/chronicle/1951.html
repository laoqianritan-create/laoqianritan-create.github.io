Overview

Fed separates from Treasury · Korean War vs. inflation tug-of-war · Policy alternates between easing and tightening

The defining event of 1951 was the Treasury-Federal Reserve Accord of March 4. The Fed was no longer subordinate to the Treasury and abandoned its obligation to hold Treasury yields low, formally operating as an independent central bank. This was a watershed in US monetary policy history — from that point the Fed began actively managing money supply to stabilize the economy, and monetary policy became a major force shaping the US economy.

At the time, however, independence was clearly bearish for equities: once the Fed stopped capping rates, yields rose, secondary-market bond purchases declined (implying further tightening), and the asset values of bond-heavy financial institutions fell. Earlier in January, the Fed had raised the securities margin requirement from 50% to 75% (Jan 17), and the Defense Production Act froze wages and prices on January 26, applying initial pressure. After the Accord, the Dow sold off sharply, and from February through June drifted sideways, mirroring high but receding industrial production and inflation.

The turning point came on July 10, when Korean War armistice negotiations opened in Kaesong. War pressure began to ease; Regulation W consumer credit restrictions and real estate credit limits were relaxed. Equities rallied from July through October. The Revenue Act of 1951 raised individual and corporate tax rates on October 20, triggering another pullback in October-November before a modest December recovery. The Dow closed at 269, up 14.4%; the S&P 500 returned +16.46%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 17
Fed Raises Stock Margin Requirement from 50% to 75%
Directly tightened equity-market liquidity. The first salvo of 1951 tightening.
Jan 26
Defense Production Act: Comprehensive Controls Freeze Wages and Prices
The US government activated wartime controls amid the Korean War.
Mar 4
Treasury–Federal Reserve Accord Signed
The Fed was no longer subordinate to the Treasury and began operating as an independent central bank; it no longer maintained low Treasury yields, and monetary policy became fully independent.
Feb–Jun
Economic Fundamentals Roll Over from Peak
From Q2 to Q3, both US industrial production and inflation YoY growth declined materially. Early-year highs of 18% PPI and over 9% CPI gradually moderated.
Jul 10
First Korean Armistice Negotiation in Kaesong
War pressure began to ease, directly affecting subsequent US economic policy decisions.
Jul 31
Economic Control Act Passed · Regulation W Eased
Congress passed the Economic Control Act. The same day, the Fed eased Regulation W consumer installment credit limits: minimum down payment cut from 25% to 15%, with longer repayment terms.
Oct 20
Revenue Act of 1951 Enacted
With the new fiscal budget showing the need for further tax hikes, the act temporarily raised individual and corporate income tax rates.
