Chronicle · Dawn of the Second Postwar Decade

Monetary Easing, Economic Recovery

S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Monetary policy keeps easing · Fiscal expansion · April recession ends · Equities lead fundamentals

1958 was the first recovery year after the 1957 global recession. Monetary policy continued the easing tone established in November 1957 and stepped it up in early 1958: stock margin requirement was slashed from 70% to 50% on January 15, the discount rate cut from 3.0% to 2.75% on January 21, to 2.25% on March 6, and to 1.75% on April 17, with two 0.5% cuts to commercial bank reserve requirements in between. The discount rate was nearly halved in under a year.

Fiscal policy moved in parallel. In early January, Eisenhower asked Congress for $1.26 billion in missile and air-defense funding; on January 31 the first US satellite, Explorer 1, launched successfully; in February Congress raised the federal debt ceiling from $275 billion to $280 billion, explicitly choosing debt over tax hikes to fund the fiscal expansion. Industrial output was still falling and unemployment rising in Q1, with the index in a narrow 5% range. The NBER-defined recession ended in April, industrial production YoY decline bottomed, and equities rallied from April, breaking out to new year-to-date highs in May.

From H2 the Fed began tightening — margin requirement raised from 50% to 70% on August 4, discount rate from 1.75% to 2.0% on August 14, margin further raised to 90% on October 15, and discount rate to 2.5% on October 23 — but none of these stopped the rally. Geopolitical shocks (US troops to Lebanon in July, the Taiwan Strait crisis in August) had limited impact. The Dow closed at 584, up about 34%; S&P 500 +38.06%, a rare postwar recovery where equities launched at the weakest fundamental moment and even reverse-direction policy couldn't suppress the rally.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 15
Fed Cuts Stock Margin Requirement: 70% → 50%
A 20-point cut, extending the easing tone established in November 1957 and the first easing move of 1958.
Jan 21
Fed Discount Rate: 3.0% → 2.75%
Another cut just two months after the prior one.
Jan 31
US Launches First Satellite, Explorer 1
Launched from Cape Canaveral, Florida, an iconic US response in the space race (Sputnik launched in October 1957). Earlier in the month, Eisenhower requested $1.26 billion in missile and air-defense funding from Congress.
Feb 19
Fed Cuts Bank Reserve Requirement by 0.5%
Further monetary easing. Congress raised the federal debt ceiling from $275 billion to $280 billion the same month, establishing debt issuance — not tax hikes — as the funding path for fiscal expansion.
Mar 6
Fed Discount Rate: 2.75% → 2.25%
Third consecutive cut, 50 bps in one move. Another 0.5% reserve requirement cut followed on March 18.
Apr 17
Another Rate Cut + Reserve Cut, Discount Rate 2.25% → 1.75%
The discount rate roughly halved within a year. The NBER-defined recession also ended in April.
Mid-Jun
Fed Reports Large-Manufacturer Pre-Tax Profits Down 40% YoY
Pre-tax profits of 200 large manufacturers fell 40% versus 1957. Earnings are among the most lagging economic indicators.
Jul 15
US Sends Troops to Lebanon
Acting on the Lebanese government's request and citing "protection of sovereignty" and "protection of US nationals," US troops landed to intervene in Lebanon's internal affairs.
Aug 4
Stock Margin Requirement: 50% → 70%
First step of monetary policy reversing from easing to tightening.
Aug 14
Fed Discount Rate: 1.75% → 2.0%
First H2 rate hike, just four months after the April cut.
Aug 23
Bombardment of Kinmen (Second Taiwan Strait Crisis)
The PLA began shelling Kinmen, sharply escalating Taiwan Strait tensions; the US Seventh Fleet intervened.
Oct 15
Stock Margin Requirement: 70% → 90%
Second margin hike of the year, near the ceiling.
Oct 23
Fed Discount Rate: 2.0% → 2.5%
Second H2 rate hike.
