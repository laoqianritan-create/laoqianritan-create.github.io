Overview

Tax-cut bill drives expectations · July rate-hike shock · Single-day reset after Nov 22 assassination

The dominant theme of 1963 was a single issue — tax cuts. Federal rates remained at Korean War highs: 52% corporate, top individual bracket of 91%. In January Kennedy submitted a tax-cut bill to Congress proposing individual rates of 14%–65% and corporate rate of 47%, igniting bullish sentiment. The market rallied through mid-February; the president's late-February speech turned to risk warnings, triggering a sharp late-February pullback.

From March industrial production accelerated YoY, and April auto output rose 12% YoY. On July 16 the Fed hiked the discount rate from 3.0% to 3.5% — the first rate hike since September 1959, mainly to curb short-term capital outflows. After the hike, the discount rate exceeded short-term Treasury yields and commercial banks tended to sell short-term bonds, with the Dow notching a ten-day losing streak in July.

From late July the market strengthened, with the Dow peaking near 760 by late October; another correction followed, triggered by the November 5 margin requirement increase from 50% to 70%. The low came on November 22, when Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas; the market completed its correction in a single session before rallying to year-end. The Dow closed at 763, up 17%; S&P 500 +18.89%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan
Kennedy Submits Tax Reduction Bill to Congress
Proposed lowering individual income taxes from 20%–91% to 14%–65%, and corporate income tax from 52% to 47%.
Late Feb
Kennedy Speech Warns of Potential Recession Risk
A reversal from previous optimistic statements, interpreted by markets as a call to cool sentiment.
Jun 20
US-Soviet Direct Hotline Established
Signed in Moscow to prevent miscalculation following the Cuban Missile Crisis.
Jul 16
Fed Rate Hike: Discount Rate 3.0% → 3.5%
First rate hike since September 1959, primarily aimed at curbing short-term capital outflows; the maximum bank time-deposit rate was also raised.
Jul
Congress Passes the Interest Equalization Tax (IET)
Imposed an additional tax on US residents' interest income from foreign securities, to ease capital outflows and balance-of-payments pressure.
Aug 5
US, USSR, and UK Sign Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty in Moscow
Banned nuclear testing in the atmosphere, outer space, and underwater (PTBT), easing the Cold War arms race.
Aug 28
Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" Speech
March on Washington for jobs and civil rights for Black Americans drew about 250,000 participants.
Nov 5
Securities Margin Requirement: 50% → 70%
Having been cut from 70% to 50% after the 1962 selloff, the reinstatement raised market concerns about broader monetary tightening.
Nov 22
President Kennedy Assassinated in Dallas
Shocked the world. NYSE closed early; trading resumed the following Monday.
Nov 22
Lyndon Johnson Sworn In as 36th President
Sworn in aboard Air Force One in Dallas by Judge Sarah Hughes, pledging to continue tax cuts and civil-rights legislation.
