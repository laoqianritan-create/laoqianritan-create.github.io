Oil Glut

S&P 500 +14.62% Dow Jones +22.6% Year-End 1896 Main Theme Oil collapse + 4 rate cuts + Tax reform + Iran-Contra
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Crude collapse drives rates lower · Fed cuts 4 times · Tax reform and Iran-Contra closing out the year

1986's main storyline was the "oil glut". International crude prices fell steadily from $26.5/barrel at year-end 1985, with WTI bottoming at $10.42 on March 31 and a year-low of about $9.6/barrel in July. The price collapse directly pulled PPI lower — February PPI fell 1.6% month-over-month, the largest single-month decline since 1947 — opening room for rates to fall. Amid synchronized global easing, the Fed cut the discount rate four times during the year (Mar 6 / Apr 18 / Jul 10 / Aug 20), from 7.5% to 5.5%.

The market hit a year-low of 203.49 on January 22, then entered a primary uptrend from late January. A brief rate rebound in April plus the Chernobyl nuclear accident triggered a pullback; after the mid-May oil-price rebound ended, the rally resumed, with the S&P touching a new high of 253.83 on September 4. From September 5 to 12, six sessions of sharp declines totaled 9.1% (including -4.81% on September 11), against the backdrop of divergent rate-cut paths among the US, Japan, and Germany; October's Japanese rate cut restored coordination. In November, Michael Milken was indicted by the DOJ, on November 2 a Lebanese magazine exposed Iran-Contra, on December 2 Reagan appointed a special counsel, and combined with the Tax Reform Act of 1986 (TRA86) signed September 27 and OPEC restoring quotas in December, the market trended lower into year-end.

The S&P 500 closed at 242, up 14.62%; the Dow finished at 1,896, up 22.6%. This was the middle stage of the "reform bull" — fundamentals were lackluster, corporate profits contracted, and valuations and sentiment were driven jointly by oil prices, interest rates, and rate-cut expectations.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 28
Space Shuttle Challenger explodes
Broke apart 73 seconds after liftoff, killing all 7 astronauts aboard. The worst disaster of the US crewed spaceflight program; the shuttle fleet was grounded for two and a half years.
Mar 6
Fed cuts discount rate 7.5% → 7.0%
First cut of the year. West Germany and Japan cut rates the same month, creating a synchronized global easing.
Mar 31
WTI crude bottoms at $10.42/barrel
The defining low of the oil glut. Prices had been at $26.5/barrel at year-end 1985. A sharp rebound followed, reaching $14.33 by April 7.
Apr 15
"Operation El Dorado Canyon" air strike on Libya
The Reagan administration dispatched hundreds of aircraft against Tripoli and Benghazi in retaliation for the West Berlin disco bombing.
Apr 18
Fed cuts discount rate 7.0% → 6.5%
Second cut of the year.
Apr 26
Chernobyl nuclear plant disaster
Reactor No. 4 at the Pripyat nuclear plant in the Ukrainian SSR exploded. The worst nuclear accident in human history.
Jul 10
Fed cuts discount rate 6.5% → 6.0%
Third cut of the year.
Aug 20
Fed cuts discount rate 6.0% → 5.5%
Fourth cut of the year; cumulative cuts totaled 200bp.
Sep 27
Reagan signs the Tax Reform Act of 1986
TRA86: cut top marginal individual rate from 50% to 28%, corporate rate from 46% to 34%, while substantially simplifying the tax base and closing avoidance loopholes. Regarded as one of Reaganomics' signature pieces of legislation.
Nov 2
Lebanese magazine reveals Iran-Contra affair
The weekly Ash-Shiraa disclosed that the US had secretly sold weapons to Iran, plunging the Reagan administration into a major political crisis, compared to Nixon's Watergate.
Nov
Milken indicted by DOJ for securities fraud
Michael Milken — the "junk bond king" — and his brother were indicted, raising fears of tightened regulation in the 1980s financialization era.
Dec 2
Reagan appoints special counsel for Iran-Contra investigation
At Congressional request, Reagan announced a court-appointed special counsel to investigate and asked Congress to form a dedicated investigative committee.
Dec
OPEC summit restores quota system
Producers agreed to cut output 5%-10% and set a target crude price of $18/barrel. The oil glut began drawing to a close.
