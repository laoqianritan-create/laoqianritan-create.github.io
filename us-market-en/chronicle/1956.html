Overview

Rates at multi-decade highs · Two Fed tightenings · July canal nationalization ignites Middle East crisis

1956 opened with interest rates at their highest level since the 19th century, and markets faced significant uncertainty: would Eisenhower seek re-election? How long could the boom last? From January through mid-February the Dow fell about 5%. Following a positive presidential health report in mid-February and Eisenhower's early-March announcement of a second-term campaign, the market rallied roughly 10% from mid-February through early April.

At end-March, the prime rate at major commercial banks rose from 3.75% to 4%, a high not seen since 1933; on April 12 the Fed lifted the discount rate from 2.5% to 2.75%. Equities peaked in early April and sold off sharply in May, on concerns over a rolling recession led by autos — GM halted production for a day in May, the first such stoppage since the Korean War. June-July saw a roughly 10% rebound, approaching year-to-date highs; a steel strike had little market impact.

On July 26, Egyptian President Nasser announced the nationalization of the Suez Canal, sharply escalating international tensions. The Fed raised the discount rate to 3.0% on August 23, and equities sold off heavily in August-September back toward the May lows. After an October rebound, the Hungarian Uprising (October 26) and the outbreak of the Suez War on October 29 (Second Arab–Israeli War) triggered another deep November decline. Easing tensions and Eisenhower's re-election sparked a December rebound. The Dow closed at 499, up 2.3%; the S&P 500 returned +2.62% — a classic wide-range year.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Late Mar
Major Bank Prime Rate: 3.75% → 4%
Highest level since March 18, 1933. Rate pressure continued to build.
Apr 12
Fed Discount Rate: 2.5% → 2.75%
Further monetary tightening began to stoke inflation concerns; CPI did indeed rise from Q2.
May
General Motors Halts All Production for One Day
First production halt since the Korean War, and May was traditionally a peak month for auto sales.
Jun 29
Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956 Authorized
Construction of the Interstate Highway System began. Originally budgeted at $25 billion over 12 years; final cost was $114 billion over 35 years.
Jul 26
Egypt's Nasser Nationalizes the Suez Canal
The Canal Company was nationalized, assets transferred to Egypt, and prior management dissolved. The US, UK, and France issued a joint communiqué on August 2 calling for canal internationalization and opposing nationalization.
Aug 23
Fed Discount Rate: 2.75% → 3.0%
Monetary tightening continued, compounding the canal crisis.
Oct 26
Hungarian Uprising
Unrest in Eastern Europe worsened the international situation.
Oct 29
Israel Invades Egypt; Second Arab-Israeli War Begins
The Suez War broke out, sharply worsening the geopolitical environment.
Nov 6 / Dec
Eisenhower Wins Re-election
On November 6 Eisenhower defeated Stevenson to win re-election; in December Middle East tensions eased.
