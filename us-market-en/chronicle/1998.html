Overview

Russian default triggers global risk-off · LTCM bailed out · Fed cuts three times in 3 months

1998 was the fourth year of the US long bull, with a deep V-shaped year. Asian crisis shadows lingered early year and the Lewinsky scandal broke, but markets recovered quickly; the S&P 500 broke 1,000 in February. By late April, the S&P and Dow were up about 16% and Nasdaq +21%. From late April to mid-June, weak Q1 earnings and South Asian nuclear tests caused the S&P to pull back about 3% and Nasdaq 7%.

The real storm came in H2. July's June PMI fell below the expansion threshold, and Q2 corporate earnings turned negative for the first time at -1.5%. On August 17, Russia devalued the ruble and deferred external debt payments, violently shaking global capital markets; hedge fund giant LTCM's leveraged positions blew up, and on September 23 the Fed assembled 14 investment banks to fund a $3.6 billion takeover. From mid-July to early October, the S&P and Dow fell nearly 20% and Nasdaq nearly 30%.

The Fed cut 75bp across September 29, October 15, and November 17, bringing the fed funds target to 4.75%. Liquidity reflow plus Q3 sequential earnings improvement and falling unemployment sent US equities into a strong rebound from October. Full year: S&P 500 +26.67%, Nasdaq +40%; Microsoft became the largest US market cap; and in September, the newly founded Google quietly launched in Silicon Valley.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 17
Clinton-Lewinsky scandal exposed
The presidential affair scandal entered the public eye, paving the way for year-end impeachment.
Apr 10
Good Friday Agreement signed in Northern Ireland
The UK, Irish governments, and Northern Irish parties reached a peace agreement, ending the main armed conflicts of the 30-year Troubles.
May 11
India conducts 5 underground nuclear tests
Codenamed "Operation Shakti," formally declaring India a nuclear state.
May 28
Pakistan conducts 5 nuclear tests in response
Both South Asian nations crossed the nuclear threshold; the international community imposed sanctions on both India and Pakistan.
Jun 5
GM Michigan plant strike
Spread from the Flint plant to 5 other assembly plants over 7 weeks, one of the largest US labor disputes of the 1990s.
Jul 13
Travelers Group and Citicorp merger completes
A $70 billion stock-swap merger formed Citigroup, the embryo of universal banking after the 1933 Glass-Steagall Act, directly catalyzing the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act the following year.
Aug 17
Russia devalues ruble and defers external debt payments
Ruble trading band widened to 6.0~9.5:1 against the dollar and government bond trading was suspended; the Russian sovereign debt crisis formally erupted.
Sep 4
Google formally incorporates
Page and Brin registered the company in their Menlo Park, California garage — the starting point of the search engine revolution.
Sep 23
Long-Term Capital Management bailed out
The Fed assembled 14 Wall Street investment banks to fund a $3.6 billion takeover of LTCM, the hedge fund led by several Nobel-laureate economists, which collapsed overnight.
Sep 29
Fed cuts 25bp (5.5% → 5.25%)
First cut of the year, addressing Russian crisis spillover and global liquidity strain.
Sep 30
FY1998 federal budget posts first surplus
Surplus of $69.2 billion, the first federal fiscal surplus since 1969, driven by strong growth and tax legislation constraints.
Oct 15
Fed emergency intermeeting cut 25bp (→ 5.0%)
A rare cut between meetings, sending a strong stabilization signal to markets.
Nov 17
Fed's third cut (→ 4.75%)
Cumulative 75bp cut to the fed funds target in 3 months.
Dec 16
US-UK launch "Operation Desert Fox" against Iraq
A 4-day air campaign over the weapons inspection dispute, almost coinciding with the impeachment vote.
Dec 19
House passes Clinton impeachment
Impeached on perjury and obstruction of justice charges, making Clinton the second US president ever impeached by the House.
