Overview

EU debt clouds lift · Fed easing relay · Two deep pullbacks then a green close

2012's market backdrop was a receding European debt crisis plus a Fed easing relay. In early February Greece received a second €130 billion rescue, removing disorderly-default risk; at the January FOMC the Fed extended forward guidance to end-2014, explicitly committing to "highly accommodative" policy. Q1 delivered the S&P 500's best quarter since 1998, gaining 11% with financials leading.

Early April through early June was the first deep pullback, with the S&P down 10%. Weaker fundamentals coincided with a second wave of European stress: a Spanish banking crisis, the Greek election, Facebook's IPO break, and JPMorgan's $6.2 billion "London Whale" loss. The 10-year US yield broke below 1.5%. On Jul 26 ECB President Draghi delivered his "Whatever It Takes" line in London, single-handedly stabilizing the eurozone.

The second half's support came entirely from central banks: on Jun 21 Operation Twist was extended; on Sep 13 the Fed launched open-ended QE3, buying $40 billion of MBS per month. Mid-October to mid-November saw a 7% pullback on fiscal-cliff and Q3-earnings fears. Obama won re-election in November; on Dec 12 QE4 launched with $45 billion of monthly Treasury purchases, replacing the expiring Twist. A last-minute fiscal-cliff deal closed the year. The S&P 500 finished at 1426, +13.41%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 25
Fed extends forward guidance to end-2014
The FOMC explicitly extends highly accommodative policy through end-2014 to support further recovery.
Feb 21
Eurozone approves second €130 billion Greek rescue
Removes disorderly-default risk; the European debt crisis gradually fades from market focus.
May 18
Facebook IPO at a $95 billion valuation
The social-network leader lists on Nasdaq at $38; breaks the IPO price the next day, down nearly 11%.
May 10
JPMorgan "London Whale" surfaces
A failed CIO hedging strategy initially reported as a $2 billion loss ultimately totals $6.2 billion.
Jun–Jul
Barclays Libor manipulation case
Barclays settles with regulators over long-running Libor manipulation; CEO Bob Diamond resigns, triggering global probes of peer banks.
Jul 26
Draghi delivers "Whatever It Takes" speech
ECB President Draghi pledges in London to "do whatever it takes" to preserve the euro—a single line that flips eurozone-crisis expectations.
Aug 29
Democratic National Convention opens
Nominates Obama for re-election; opens the final stretch of the campaign.
Sep 13
Fed launches QE3 (open-ended MBS)
Buys $40 billion of agency MBS per month with no fixed end date and extends ZIRP guidance to 2015.
Oct 29
Hurricane Sandy hits the US East Coast
NYSE closes for two days—a rare event—and damage in New York and New Jersey tops $70 billion.
Nov 6
Obama defeats Romney for re-election
Democrats hold the White House; fiscal-cliff talks enter bipartisan negotiation mode.
Nov 15
Xi Jinping becomes General Secretary of the CCP
The new Politburo Standing Committee is elected at the 18th CCP Plenum, opening a new political cycle in China.
Dec 12
Fed launches QE4 (open-ended Treasuries)
Buys $45 billion of long-term Treasuries per month, replacing the expiring Operation Twist, and introduces thresholds of 6.5% unemployment and 2.5% inflation in its guidance.
Dec 31
Last-minute fiscal-cliff deal reached
The White House and Senate Republican leadership compromise on personal taxes, unemployment benefits, and spending cuts, averting automatic year-start tax hikes and spending cuts.
