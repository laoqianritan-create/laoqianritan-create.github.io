Overview

Post-crisis transition year · Regulatory reform and QE2 launched together

2010 was a post-crisis transition year. The rebound off the March 2009 lows carried into January, but in late January the PBOC tightened policy and Obama proposed tougher rules for big banks, raising fears of fading global liquidity and spreading European debt stress. The S&P 500 fell 8% in 15 trading days. From February, record annual reports and earnings drove a rebound into late April.

Late April through late August was a four-month correction and base-building. The SEC sued Goldman Sachs over Abacus, Greece's sovereign rating was downgraded to junk, QE1 formally ended in late April, and earnings and manufacturing PMI rolled over together; Treasury yields slid from 4% to a year-low of 2.4%. The Dow's 998-point "Flash Crash" in 36 minutes on May 6 exposed the structural fragility of the high-frequency-trading era. The S&P 500 fell 14% over this stretch.

The turning point came on Aug 27, when Bernanke signaled further Treasury purchases at Jackson Hole. On Nov 3 the Fed officially launched QE2, sized at $600 billion. Combined with Republican gains in the House at the midterms (improving prospects for extending the Bush tax cuts), markets re-entered an easy-money bull run, with the S&P up 20% from early September to year-end. The Dodd-Frank Act was signed on Jul 21, the largest financial-regulatory overhaul since the Great Depression. The S&P 500 closed at 1257.64, +12.78%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 21
Fed announces wind-down of several emergency liquidity facilities
Multiple crisis-era lending facilities begin closing from Feb 1—the first step of policy normalization.
Jan 27
Obama delivers first State of the Union
His first State of the Union since taking office, prioritizing job creation, regulatory reform, and health-care reform.
Mar 23
Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (ACA) signed
Obamacare formally becomes law, the largest US health-care overhaul since Medicare and Medicaid in 1965.
Apr 16
SEC sues Goldman Sachs (Abacus case)
The SEC charges Goldman with omitting key facts when marketing a subprime-linked product to investors.
Apr 20
BP Deepwater Horizon rig explodes
A blow-out and fire on the BP rig in the Gulf of Mexico causes the largest offshore oil spill in US history, leaking for nearly three months.
Apr 27
S&P downgrades Greece to junk
On Apr 23 Moody's downgraded Greece to A3 and Greece formally requested EU/IMF assistance; on Apr 27 S&P slashed it to BB+ junk.
Apr 28
First round of QE (QE1) formally ends
The Fed has bought $1.725 trillion of GSE debt and related MBS; the fed funds target remains at 0.25%.
May 6
Flash Crash
The Dow plunges 998 points (about 9%) in 36 minutes, mostly retracing into the close. High-frequency trading and algorithmic order flow enter public view as a structural market issue.
May
EU and IMF launch Greek bailout
EU, ECB, and IMF provide €110 billion of assistance to Greece, officially opening the European sovereign-debt crisis.
Jul 21
Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act signed
Obama signs the largest US financial-regulatory overhaul since the Great Depression, establishing new frameworks for SIFIs, derivatives markets, and consumer financial protection.
Aug 27
Bernanke's Jackson Hole speech
At Jackson Hole Bernanke signals that the Fed is prepared to further support the economy through additional Treasury purchases.
Nov 2
Republicans win the House at the midterms
Boosted by the Tea Party, Republicans sweep the House; Democrats barely hold the Senate. Congress enters a split-control phase, raising the odds the Bush tax cuts will be extended.
Nov 3
Fed launches second round of QE (QE2)
Commits to $600 billion of long-term Treasury purchases through end-June 2011 to depress long-end yields and support the economy.
Dec 17
Tunisian uprising sparks the Arab Spring
Tunisian vendor Bouazizi's self-immolation triggers mass protests that spread to Egypt, Libya, Syria, and beyond, reshaping the Middle East's political map.
