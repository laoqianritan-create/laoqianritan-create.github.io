Overview

A year of diplomatic breakthroughs · Price controls hold CPI down · Watergate seeds the future

1972 was a year that reshaped the Cold War order. Nixon visited China in February and the USSR in May, signing the Shanghai Communiqué with Beijing and the SALT I strategic-arms treaty with Moscow — opening two doors in foreign policy within seven months. That November, Nixon was re-elected in a landslide, winning 49 of 50 states.

On the macro side, the wage-and-price controls in place since August 1971 entered Phase II. Core CPI was held low and industrial production growth kept rising. But price controls could not contain internationally priced commodities: from H2, nickel and food prices surged, money-market rates rose visibly from August, and the seeds of 1973's runaway inflation were sown. At the start of the year many countries continued to cut rates; the US 13-week Treasury issued in January carried a yield as low as 3.0%, the lowest since 1963.

Markets ground higher in early 2 1972, corrected from mid-April to early May on Vietnam setbacks and slowing corporate-profit growth, and traded sideways for most of March to October. The turn came in late October — after Kissinger's South Vietnam visit produced the line "peace is at hand," and Q3 earnings showed a sharp profit improvement. On November 22 the Fed raised the stock-margin requirement from 55% to 65%. The biggest seed of all was planted in the early hours of June 17 with the burglary at the Watergate complex — at the time treated as an isolated criminal incident, but it would consume Nixon two years later.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 5
Belgian and Dutch central banks cut rates on the same day
Belgium 5.5% → 5.0%, Netherlands 5.0% → 4.5%. The same week, the US Treasury's 13-week bill yielded just 3.0%, the lowest in nearly a decade since 1963.
Jan 20
Nixon submits an "expansionary" budget to Congress
The fiscal deficit remained high. Nixon called the budget "expansionary" but "not inflationary"; concerns about inflation weighed on markets for several days.
Feb 21–28
Nixon visits China, signs the Shanghai Communiqué
On Feb 28 the US and China issued a joint communiqué in Shanghai, opening a new era in bilateral relations. The Cold War order was rewritten.
May 16
CME launches the International Monetary Market (IMM)
The first financial-derivatives market, established within the CME — a product of the floating-rate era that followed the collapse of Bretton Woods.
May 22–30
Nixon visits the USSR, signs SALT I strategic-arms treaty
A week-long Moscow summit, ending May 29. The first bilateral cap on Cold War nuclear arsenals; détente diplomacy took shape.
Jun 1
Saddam-led Iraq nationalizes foreign oil companies
Affected US, UK, French, and Dutch interests. Oil revenues were earmarked for the military; markets viewed the compensation as severely undervalued.
Jun 7
Supreme Court rules the Federal Power Commission may allocate natural gas
Against the backdrop of energy shortages, industrial gas use was placed behind residences, schools, and hospitals.
Jun 17
The Watergate break-in
Five men led by McCord, chief security adviser to Nixon's re-election committee, broke into the DNC headquarters at the Watergate complex in Washington to plant bugs and were caught on the spot. Two years later it would bring down Nixon.
Jun 23
UK announces a floating pound
Another major currency unmoored after the collapse of Bretton Woods.
Aug 12
Last US ground combat troops withdraw from Vietnam
After Nixon's June 28 announcement that no further draftees would be sent to Vietnam, all ground forces departed.
Oct 26
Kissinger says "peace is at hand" after South Vietnam visit
Paris signals had pointed to a possible cease-fire within ten days. Combined with sharply better Q3 corporate earnings.
Nov 7
Nixon re-elected as US president
Won 49 of 50 states in a landslide. The Watergate burglary had yet to weigh on the campaign.
Nov 22
Fed raises stock-margin requirement from 55% to 65%
In response to rising global rates and commodity prices. The clearest signal of monetary tightening in the year.
Dec
Bank of England minimum lending rate raised to 9.0%
The highest level since the 1960s. European countries collectively tightened to fight inflation.
