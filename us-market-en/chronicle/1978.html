1978
Chronicle · Mid-Stagflation

The Fed Gets a Dual Mandate

S&P 500 +1.06% Dow Jones -3.1% Year-End 805 Main Theme FX + Rate Hikes + Dual Mandate
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Dollar crisis through the year · Seven Fed rate hikes · Dual mandate enacted

The macro storyline of 1978 was the dollar crisis and persistent monetary tightening. The dollar depreciation that began in October 1977 continued all year — the greenback fell 36% versus the Swiss franc, 33% versus the yen, 24% versus the mark, and 16% versus sterling. The Fed raised the discount rate seven times, from 6.0% at the start of the year to 9.5% by year-end. On March 8, Miller took office as Fed Chair, and on April 1 the Jamaica Agreement formally took effect, ushering floating exchange rates into a fully codified regime.

On February 28 the Dow hit the year's low of 742, down nearly 11% from year-end 1977. A short-lived dollar rebound in April lifted the market 10.6% in a single month, and on September 8 the Dow touched the year's high of 908. In Q4 CPI YoY accelerated from 7.7% in July to 8.9% in October, and the Treasury curve inverted. On October 27 President Carter signed the Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act (Humphrey–Hawkins Act), formally enshrining "full employment" in the Fed's responsibilities — establishing in law the dual mandate of price stability and full employment. On November 1 the Fed hiked 100 basis points in one move to 9.5%, shifting the target from FX to inflation.

The Dow closed the year at 805, down 3.1%, with the S&P 500 +1.06%. The institutional pivot of the year — the Fed being given a dual mandate — laid the legal groundwork for the Volcker era's war on inflation that followed.

First hike of the year, aimed at the deteriorating dollar.

Miller sworn in as Fed Chair
G. William Miller succeeded Burns as the new Fed Chair.

Jamaica Agreement formally takes effect
The 1976 agreement entered into force, giving floating exchange rates official legal standing under the IMF framework.

Fed hike: discount rate from 6.5% to 7.0%
Second hike of the year.

Fed hike: discount rate from 7.0% to 7.25%
Third hike of the year as the dollar came under renewed pressure.
Aug 18
Fed hike: discount rate from 7.25% to 7.75%
Fourth hike of the year; short rates rose sharply.
Sep 17
Camp David Accords reached
Egyptian President Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Begin reached a framework agreement at Camp David, paving the way for the Egypt–Israel peace treaty.
Sep 22
Fed hike: discount rate from 7.75% to 8.0%
Fifth hike of the year.
Oct 13
Fed hike: discount rate from 8.0% to 8.5%
Sixth hike of the year. The 10-year/3-month Treasury spread inverted in Q4.
Oct 27
Carter signs the Humphrey–Hawkins Full Employment Act
The Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act folded "full employment" into the Fed's responsibilities, formally giving the Fed a dual mandate of price stability plus full employment; the Chair was required to testify before Congress twice a year.
Nov 1
Fed hike: discount rate from 8.5% to 9.5% (100 bp)
Seventh hike of the year — a single 100 bp move. The target of tightening shifted from FX to inflation control.
Nov 7
Congressional midterm elections
Democrats retained both House and Senate majorities, but with some seat losses.
Dec 16
US–China Joint Communiqué on the Establishment of Diplomatic Relations
The US accepted Beijing's three principles — severing diplomatic ties with Taipei, withdrawing troops, and abrogating the defense treaty — and the two countries announced formal diplomatic relations from January 1, 1979.
