Overview

Fundamentals-driven uptrend · Peaks in August · Oct 19 single-day -20.5% · Emergency liquidity rescue

1987 was the last year of the "new all-time highs" era and the most dramatic year of the period. Fundamentals continued the expansion underway since 1983: industrial production growth climbed throughout, and unemployment fell to 5.8% by year-end. The early-year rally unfolded along the fundamentals storyline; on January 8, the Dow Jones Industrial Average broke 2,000 for the first time in history, and on August 25 the S&P 500 peaked at 337.89, with year-to-date gains of 39.6%.

On February 22, six nations signed the Louvre Accord, attempting to stabilize the dollar after the post-Plaza decline — a pivotal turn in the year's FX-and-rates narrative. March–May saw range trading as 10-year Treasury yields surged from 7.2% to 8.9%, and Reagan's tariffs on Japanese imports further escalated trade frictions. Greenspan was nominated in June and formally succeeded Volcker as Fed Chair on August 11; on September 4 the discount rate was raised 5.5% → 6.0%, the first hike of the year.

After the late-August peak, accelerating rates plus dollar weakness sent equities into a stepwise decline from October 6; by the 16th the drawdown had exceeded 16%. On October 19, "Black Monday," the S&P 500 fell 20.5% in a single session and the Dow fell 22.6% — exceeding the 1929 record. The Fed emergency-injected liquidity and Treasury yields plummeted near-vertically. October-end saw an 18% rebound, November rangebound, and December a gradual fundamentals-supported recovery. The S&P 500 finished at 247, +2.0%; the Dow closed near 1,939, +2.3%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 8
Dow Jones Industrial Average tops 2,000 for the first time
Fourteen years after first crossing 1,000 in November 1972.
Feb 20
Brazil announces indefinite suspension of external debt interest payments
Involved roughly $81 billion in debt, including $24 billion owed to US commercial banks. On February 25, Brazil also announced suspension of payments on $16 billion of maturing short-term loans.
Feb 22
Six nations sign the Louvre Accord
The US, Japan, UK, France, West Germany, and Canada signed an agreement in Paris aimed at stabilizing international currency markets and halting the sustained dollar decline triggered by the Plaza Accord. The US committed to lowering the federal deficit-to-GDP ratio from 3.9% to 2.3% by 1988.
Mar 3 – 4
General Motors announces buyback of 20% of shares by 1990
Announced after the close on March 3, easing market concerns about elevated equity prices.
Apr 17
Reagan imposes tariffs on $300 million of Japanese imports
Categories included computers, televisions, and electrical equipment. The US-Japan trade war escalated in April.
Jun 3
Greenspan nominated as Fed Chair
Reagan nominated Alan Greenspan to succeed Paul Volcker. Markets viewed Greenspan as aligned with Reaganomics and inclined to continue lowering rates.
Aug 11
Greenspan officially succeeds Volcker as Fed Chair
Opening the eighteen-year Greenspan era.
Sep 4
Fed discount rate 5.5% → 6.0%
First hike of the year. The 10-year Treasury yield accelerated higher from July, breaking the prior peak by late August.
Mon, Oct 19
"Black Monday"
S&P 500 fell 20.5% in a single session, the Dow Jones Industrial Average -22.6%, exceeding the single-day decline record from October 29, 1929. Record trading volumes overwhelmed multiple NYSE systems, delaying trade reports by over an hour. The shock rapidly spread globally: the UK FTSE -10.8%, Japan Nikkei 225 -16.9% over two days, Hong Kong Hang Seng -11.12%.
Oct 22 – 26
Fed emergency liquidity injection rescues the market
From October 20, the Fed announced its "commitment to serve as a source of liquidity to support the economic and financial system" and injected substantial liquidity through open market operations for several consecutive days. Treasury yields plummeted near-vertically.
Nov 20
White House and Congress reach deficit reduction agreement
Planned to reduce the federal deficit by $75 billion over the next two years. The same month, major Western nations cut rates to stabilize markets and economies.
Dec 7
US and Canada agree on free-trade agreement details
Early December data showed US unemployment falling to 5.8%.
