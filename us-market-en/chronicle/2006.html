Overview

Bernanke takes over · 17-hike cycle ends at 5.25% · Sixth M&A wave peaks · Home prices roll over

2006 was the closing year of the strongest postwar hiking cycle. On Feb 1, Ben Bernanke succeeded the 18-year veteran Greenspan as Fed Chair. On Mar 28, May 10, and Jun 29, the Fed lifted fed funds in three steps to 5.25%, where it then held for two and a half years. The macro picture: corporate earnings kept beating, oil rolled over from its summer high, labor data ran lukewarm, and Q2 real GDP slowed to 3.1%—signs that the US economy was topping out.

The market priced the imminent end of hikes in three legs. From January to early May, equities ground higher in wishful thinking. After the May 10 hike disappointed those hoping for a pause and commodity inflation worsened, the index pulled back nearly 8% into mid-June. After the final hike on Jun 29, the market actually bottomed, and from mid-July into year-end advanced on the M&A wave, earnings beats, and the end-of-hikes thesis. The sixth M&A wave that began in 2003 peaked in 2006, with a series of mega-deals driving risk appetite.

On the political side, the Nov 7 midterms saw Democrats flip both chambers of Congress—the pivot of Bush's second term. On Dec 30, Saddam was executed. Two undercurrents in capital markets foreshadowed trouble: the 10-year/short-rate inversion that began in June persisted through year-end, and in December the Case-Shiller home-price index peaked and rolled over. These signals failed to disturb equities that year, but laid the fuse for the 2007 subprime crisis.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Feb 1
Ben Bernanke takes over as Fed Chair
Succeeds 18-year veteran Alan Greenspan. March remarks express optimism on the US economy, reinforcing market expectations of continued hikes.
Feb
Google enters S&P 500 candidate pool
One of the post-bust new-generation leaders—symbolic of tech's repositioning within the index.
Mar 28
Fed hikes 25 bp to 4.75%
Bernanke's first hike. Several rate metrics break above 5% during this period; gold hits a 25-year high.
May 10
Fed hikes 25 bp to 5.0%
Hopes for a pause are dashed. Rising commodity prices and inflation concerns then push Treasury yields to 5.19%.
Jun 29
Fed delivers its final hike to 5.25%
The 17-hike cycle that began in June 2004 ends at the post-bust peak. Fed funds then holds at 5.25% for 14 months—the terminus of the strongest postwar hiking cycle.
Jul 5
North Korea fires seven missiles in succession
The North Korean nuclear issue escalates and the international community is rattled.
Jul
Lebanon War breaks out · Oil at highs since 1979
Oil supply concerns move back to the front. WTI crude reaches its highest level since the second oil shock.
Aug
Federal minimum-wage bill stalls in Congress
The Republican version pairs a minimum-wage increase with estate-tax cuts; the Senate rejects it in early August. The federal minimum has not been raised in nine years.
Oct 9
North Korea conducts first underground nuclear test
The UN Security Council adopts Resolution 1718 imposing sanctions. DPRK formally joins the ranks of de facto nuclear states.
Nov 7
Midterms · Democrats flip both chambers
Democrats control both houses of Congress simultaneously for the first time in 12 years. Defense Secretary Rumsfeld resigns the next day—seen as the pivot of Bush's second-term Iraq policy.
Dec
Case-Shiller National Home Price Index peaks
The 10-city and 20-city composites peak in mid-to-late 2006 and roll over. The 10-year/short-end yield curve has been inverted since June. The data already point to the subprime crisis ahead.
Dec 30
Former Iraqi president Saddam Hussein executed
Sentenced to hanging by the Iraqi High Tribunal; carried out in Baghdad. The Iraq War enters a new phase.
