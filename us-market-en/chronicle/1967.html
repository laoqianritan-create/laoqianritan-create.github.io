1967
Chronicle · End of the Golden Age

Sterling Crisis

S&P 500 +20.09% Dow Jones +15.2% Year-End 905 Main Theme Global Easing + Sterling Devaluation + Monetary Reversal
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Global monetary easing → November sterling devalues 14.3% → Fed forced into back-to-back tightening

The 1967 macro narrative was a full reversal — from global easing forced into tightening by the sterling crisis. Early in the year West Germany and the UK cut rates; Johnson signaled openness to lower rates; and on February 28 the Fed cut the savings reserve requirement to 3.0%. The Dow rose nearly 10% in Q1.

On April 6 the Fed formally cut the discount rate from 4.5% to 4.0%; with rumors of restored tax credits, the Dow hit a year-to-date peak of 910 in early May. The tax bill was then shelved and economic data was revised down, sending the index back to 848 by early June. The Six-Day War broke out on June 5, with almost no market impact; the federal debt ceiling was raised at end-June; the July nationwide rail workers' strike likewise didn't shake markets.

From July through late September, continued global easing and stabilizing data lifted the Dow to a year-to-date high of 952. From October commodities rallied, market rates set records, and a tax surcharge was expected. On November 18 sterling devalued 14.3%; the $35/oz official gold price held, but global monetary policy no longer dared ease further. The Fed raised the discount rate to 4.5% the next day, and to 5.5% in December. The Dow closed at 905, +15.2%; S&P 500 +20.09%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan
West Germany and UK Cut Discount Rates
Early January: German discount rate 5.0%→4.5%; late January: Bank of England 7.0%→6.5%. Johnson publicly indicated he would lower rates and ease money within his authority.
Feb 28
Fed Cuts Savings Deposit Reserve Requirement: 4.0% → 3.0%
Without a formal rate cut, the easing tone was clear. West Germany cut again the same month to 4.0%.
Apr 6
Fed Formally Cuts: Discount Rate 4.5% → 4.0%
Compounded by market rumors of restored 7% investment tax credit and accelerated depreciation.
Jun 5-10
Third Arab-Israeli War (Six-Day War) Breaks Out
Israel launched massive air and ground strikes against Egypt, reshaping the Middle East within six days.
Late Jun
Federal Debt Ceiling: $336B → $358B
Rumored to be raised early in the month, then shelved mid-month, and finally passed at month-end.
Jul 16
Nationwide US Rail Workers' Strike
137,000 rail workers launched a national strike that eventually involved 700,000 workers, halting over 95% of passenger and freight traffic — the first postwar nationwide rail strike.
Late Jul
Detroit Race Riots and Vietnam "Search and Destroy"
Domestic anti-war protests escalated; the "search and destroy" strategy was deployed in Vietnam, with television bringing the war into American households.
Oct
Commodities Rally, Market Rates Hit Record Highs
Combined with expectations of a tax surcharge to balance the budget, Treasury yields broke recent highs.
Nov 18
Sterling Devalues 14.3% to $2.40
Sterling gold content fell to 2.13281 g/pound. The $35/oz official gold price held, but global monetary policy no longer dared ease.
Nov 19
Fed Hike: Discount Rate 4.0% → 4.5%
Monetary policy formally reversed to tightening, just seven months after the April cut. France and Japan also hiked rates at year-end.
Dec 27
Fed Continues Tightening: Reserve Requirement Hike, Discount Rate to 5.5%
The discount rate was further raised the same month, completing the year's pivot from easing to tightening.
Full Year
Full-Year CPI Rises to 3.1%, Gold Reserves Continue to Drain
Vietnam spending and social-security reform spending compounded; dollar confidence declined; London Gold Pool members increasingly demanded gold conversion.
