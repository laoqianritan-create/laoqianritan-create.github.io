Overview

Supreme Court strikes down tariffs · U.S.–Iran war runs all year · AI capex continues (year still in progress)

2026 is the second full year of Trump's second term. Markets began the year still absorbing the delayed effects of the 2025 tariffs, with the January FOMC holding rates steady. From mid-February a dispute over immigration-enforcement reform left part of the federal government shut for 76 days, the longest agency shutdown in U.S. history. On 20 February the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that the president lacks authority to impose tariffs under IEEPA, striking down most of the 2025 duties. On 28 February the U.S. and Israel launched airstrikes on Iran, and the Middle East war became the thread running through the year, disrupting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and driving oil steadily higher.

March brought the weakest stretch, with the S&P 500 bottoming at 6,343.72 on 30 March, about 7.5% below the start of the year. A two-week U.S.–Iran ceasefire on 8 April lifted April to a 10.42% gain, the strongest month of the year. In May Trump made a state visit to China and Kevin Warsh succeeded Jerome Powell as Fed chair; in June SpaceX completed the largest IPO in history at a valuation of about $1.77tn, and the June FOMC dot plot turned sharply hawkish.

Through the second half the index ground higher amid war and policy crosscurrents, setting a year high of 7,798.99 on 13 August. In September the U.S.–Iran conflict re-escalated and the Fed raised rates 25bp to 3.75–4% on 16 September, its first hike since July 2023, and late in the month Xi Jinping made a state visit to Washington, where the two sides extended their trade truce by two months. Through 2026-09 the S&P 500 is +13.12% year to date, with the pattern of "war shock — ceasefire repair — high-level churn". Watch points ahead: the 3 November midterms, the war and oil, and AI-capex earnings delivery.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
First anniversary of Trump's second term
Tariffs, immigration, tax reform and other agendas since January 2025 enter their second implementation year.
Jan 28
First FOMC meeting of the year
Rates held at 3.50–3.75% with no clear timing for the next cut; the easing window is pushed back.
Feb 13
January CPI at 2.4%, in line with expectations
Inflation keeps easing; tariff pass-through has yet to show up in price data.
Feb 14
Federal government partially shuts down for 76 days
A dispute over immigration-enforcement reform leaves Homeland Security unfunded until 30 April — the longest agency shutdown in U.S. history; a four-day shutdown ran from 31 January to 3 February.
Feb 20
Supreme Court strikes down the tariffs
A 6–3 ruling finds the president lacks authority to levy tariffs under IEEPA, voiding most of the 2025 duties.
Feb 28
U.S. and Israel strike Iran
Operation Epic Fury begins and Supreme Leader Khamenei is killed; Iran retaliates with missiles and drones against U.S. bases in the Gulf.
Mar 18
Fed holds rates steady
The funds rate stays at 3.50–3.75%; the median dot implies just one cut this year, ending the 2H 2025 easing cadence.
Apr 8
Two-week U.S.–Iran ceasefire agreed
Oil falls more than 15% in a session as risk appetite returns.
Apr
Q1 earnings broadly beat; tariff concerns recede
Large-cap tech and consumer leaders show earnings resilience; the second-order tariff hit does not materialize.
Apr 18
North Korea test-fires short-range ballistic missiles
Two rounds of launches in April briefly raise regional tensions.
May 13
Trump makes a state visit to China
The 13–15 May visit produces preliminary deals on agricultural purchases, aircraft orders and tariffs.
May 22
Kevin Warsh sworn in as Fed chair
He succeeds Jerome Powell for a term running through May 2030.
Jun 12
SpaceX completes the largest IPO ever
Priced at $135 a share, it raises $75bn on Nasdaq at a valuation of about $1.77tn.
Jun 17
June dot plot turns sharply hawkish
Rates are held, but the median 2026 dot rises to 3.8% from 3.4%, with most officials open to further hikes.
Aug 26
Nvidia posts $96.2bn quarterly revenue
Data-center revenue of $89bn, up 117% year over year, sustains the AI capital-expenditure narrative.
Sep 1
U.S.–Iran conflict re-escalates
After U.S. strikes on Iran, Tehran launches its largest barrage since the April ceasefire, then hits U.S. bases in Kuwait and the UAE.
Sep 16
Fed raises rates 25bp to 3.75–4%
The first increase since July 2023, approved 12–0; Chair Warsh says inflation is “too high”.
Sep 20
Massive Ukrainian drone strike on Moscow
Russia says it downed more than 1,600 drones; the Moscow refinery is damaged and three people are killed, on the final day of parliamentary voting.
Sep 22
U.S.–Denmark–Greenland security agreement signed
Signed during the UN General Assembly, it amends the 1951 defence agreement, adds two military sites and allows a “Golden Dome” missile-defence system.
Sep 23
Xi Jinping makes a state visit to the United States
Sept 23–25 in Washington, the first U.S. visit by a Chinese president in over a decade; the two sides held White House talks and extended their trade truce by two months.
