Overview

First anniversary of Trump's second term · Tariff effects feed into the data · AI capex narrative continues (year still in progress)

2026 is the second full year of Trump's second term. The year opens with the question of whether the previous year's tariff effects will start to show up in real economic data. On Jan 20, Trump marks his first anniversary in office; a week later the year's first FOMC meeting holds rates, pushing back expectations for the next cut.

The January CPI released in February prints at 2.9%, above market expectations, providing evidence that tariff costs are passing through to consumers. The same month the US and China launch a second phase of trade talks, easing some external-sector concern. The March FOMC announces a pause to the cutting cycle, closing the 2H 2025 easing window; in April, tariff-shock concerns recede and broadly better-than-expected Q1 earnings restore risk appetite. A mid-April North Korean nuclear-test scare briefly stirs safe-haven flows and then fades.

As of 2026-05, the AI capex narrative is sustained in hyperscaler Q1 reports and capex guidance. The S&P 500 is +8.08% YTD, with the overall pattern being "tariff digestion plus AI continuation". The year is still in progress; the second-half watch list includes the Fed policy path, second-order tariff effects, AI-capex earnings delivery, and Nvidia's late-May Q1 report.
