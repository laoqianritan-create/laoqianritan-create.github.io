Overview

Fiscal-cliff deal · Taper rhythm wobbles · 16-day government shutdown · Taper begins at year-end

2013 opened with the late-2012 American Taxpayer Relief Act in hand, putting the fiscal-cliff crisis behind for the moment; in late January Congress extended the debt ceiling again, clearing room for the first half. In early April both the S&P 500 and the Dow broke through their pre-crisis highs; by mid-May the S&P was up 14% YTD. Q1 earnings growth was essentially flat sequentially—the first-half gains were driven mostly by multiple expansion on Fed easing, not earnings.

On May 22 Bernanke first signaled Taper in congressional testimony; the market opened a roughly one-month, 5% pullback, and the 10-year yield rose from 1.7% and broke 3% in September. The Fed then wavered: on Sep 18 the FOMC unexpectedly kept purchases unchanged, briefly relieving markets. On Oct 1 non-essential federal operations shut down for 16 days, ending on Oct 16 with a debt-ceiling raise. Hawkish QE-exit expectations actually receded after the impasse. In November dovish Janet Yellen was nominated as the next Fed Chair, reinforcing easing expectations.

On Dec 17 the Fed announced a $10 billion monthly Taper—a gentle pace with no rate hike—removing the year's biggest uncertainty. The Dow finished +26.5%, the S&P 500 +29.60%, and the Nasdaq +38.3%—the Dow's and S&P's best year since 1995, and the Nasdaq's best since 2000. From mid-year on, a stabilizing US economy took over from multiple expansion as the main driver of the index advance.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 2
Obama signs the American Taxpayer Relief Act
Raises the top individual rate on income above $450,000, extends unemployment benefits by a year, and defers $110 billion of spending cuts by two months—the fiscal-cliff crisis is temporarily resolved.
Jan 20
Obama inaugurated for a second term
The 57th US presidential inauguration; Obama begins his second four-year term.
Mar
Cyprus bailout and deposit levy
The EU, IMF, and Cyprus agree on a €10 billion bailout with a one-time levy on large deposits, reigniting the debate over European bank-deposit safety.
Mar 5
Dow Jones closes above its 2007 pre-crisis high
The Dow closes above its October 2007 all-time high, marking the formal completion of the post-crisis recovery cycle.
Apr 15
Boston Marathon bombing
Two homemade bombs detonate near the finish line, killing 3 and wounding more than 260; the suspect is captured on Apr 19.
Apr
Gold price crash
On Apr 12–15 gold falls nearly 15% in two days, ending a 12-year bull market and prompting a repricing of inflation and safe-haven logic.
May 22
Bernanke hints at Taper in congressional testimony
Bernanke says the Fed may slow asset purchases as data show sustained improvement. Equities open a roughly one-month, 5% pullback; the 10-year yield rises from 1.7% to above 3% by September.
Jun
Snowden exposes the PRISM program
Former CIA contractor Edward Snowden discloses NSA mass-surveillance programs to the Guardian and Washington Post, triggering a global diplomatic storm and a sea change in the internet-privacy debate.
Jul 18
City of Detroit files for bankruptcy
With roughly $18 billion of debt, it becomes the largest municipal bankruptcy in US history—an emblem of the decline of the old industrial belt.
Sep 18
Fed unexpectedly leaves purchases unchanged
The September FOMC sharply surprises markets by not starting Taper, resetting QE-exit expectations.
Oct 1–16
Federal government shuts down for 16 days
Bipartisan deadlock over ACA funding closes non-essential operations on Oct 1; Congress raises the debt ceiling and reopens government on Oct 16. Default fears spike during the impasse, but post-shutdown QE-exit expectations decline.
Nov
Yellen nominated as next Fed Chair
On Nov 14 the Senate Banking Committee holds a hearing; the dovish Yellen is nominated to succeed Bernanke, reinforcing expectations of continued easing.
Dec 18
Fed begins Taper
Cuts monthly purchases by $10 billion at a gentle pace with no rate hike—removing the year's biggest uncertainty. On Dec 10 the parties had agreed on a near-two-year budget, averting another shutdown in January 2014.
Dec 26
PM Abe visits Yasukuni Shrine
Abe's first visit on his first anniversary in office draws strong protests from China and South Korea, stirring East Asian geopolitics.
