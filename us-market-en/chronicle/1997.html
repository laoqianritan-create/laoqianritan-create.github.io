Overview

Inflation pressure + Rate hike + Asian currency collapse · Three major indices still post bull-market gains

1997 was the mid-stage of the internet long bull. Early year, markets focused on recovery and inflation; PMI and inflation data jumped in January, and by early March the Dow and S&P 500 were both up ~10%, exhibiting a classic "Nifty Fifty" large-cap rally with Nasdaq nearly flat over the same period. In early March, Greenspan signaled hike expectations to Congress, and on March 25 the Fed hiked 25bp to 5.50%. Meanwhile, the dollar index rose from 88 to 96, pressuring multinational earnings expectations; from mid-March to mid-April, the indices gave back all early-year gains.

Q1 earnings rolled out from April: Microsoft, Intel, Caterpillar, and Ford materially beat, combined with inflation and unemployment falling in tandem and rates beginning to decline, US equities entered a "sweet spot," with all three indices gaining about 30% by early August. In May, the Clinton administration and Congress reached a five-year balanced budget agreement; on August 5, the Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997 and the Balanced Budget Act were signed, fundamentally eliminating the long-standing fiscal deficit. By September, core inflation fell to a low of 2.2% — the lowest since the 1960s.

The real risk was overseas. The Thai baht collapsed on July 2, igniting the Asian financial crisis; in October, Korea, Indonesia, and Malaysia fell in turn, and international speculators moved to Hong Kong to attack the linked exchange rate. On October 27, the Dow fell 7.18% in a single session, with global capital markets violently shaken. In November, Korea sought IMF aid. Q4 US PMI, retail, and earnings growth were all hit, but the bull market continued — Dow +22.6%, S&P 500 +31.01%, Nasdaq +22%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
Clinton inaugurated for second term
Reelected; governing themes continued — economic expansion, fiscal consolidation, free trade.
Feb 19
Deng Xiaoping dies in Beijing
At age 93. The chief architect of China's reform and opening.
Mar 25
Fed hikes 25bp; fed funds rate 5.25% → 5.50%
Greenspan had signaled readiness for further hikes in early March congressional testimony. The only hike of the year.
May 1
UK Labour's Blair wins election
Labour took power for the first time since 1979; Tony Blair succeeded Conservative John Major as PM.
Jul 1
Hong Kong returns to China
The UK and China conducted the handover ceremony at the HK Convention Centre at midnight June 30/July 1, ending 156 years of British colonial rule.
Jul 2
Thai baht abandons dollar peg; Asian financial crisis erupts
The Bank of Thailand floated the baht; the currency devalued about 17% the same day. The Southeast Asian currency crisis ignited, soon spreading to Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines.
Aug 5
Clinton signs the Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997 and Balanced Budget Act
Cut capital gains and individual income taxes; required a balanced budget by 2002 (achieved in 1998). Fundamentally ended years of fiscal deficits.
Aug 31
Princess Diana dies in car accident
In Paris, at age 36.
Oct 23
International speculators attack the HK dollar peg
As the East Asian financial crisis spread, speculators concentrated attacks on the HK dollar, targeting the linked exchange rate. The HKMA used FX reserves and sharply raised overnight rates in response.
Oct 27
Dow falls 7.18% in a session, triggering circuit-breaker halt
Asian crisis contagion sent the Dow down 554 points; the NYSE triggered the 1988 circuit breakers for the first time, closing early.
Nov 17
Korean won hits record 1008:1 against USD
The East Asian financial storm spread to Korea. On November 21, the Korean government sought IMF aid; the Japanese financial sector was hit and a series of banks and securities firms failed.
Dec 3
IMF signs $58.3 billion rescue package with Korea
The largest IMF rescue in history at the time, with strict structural reform and austerity conditions.
Dec 11
Kyoto Protocol signed in Kyoto, Japan
The first legally binding international agreement on greenhouse gas emissions reduction in human history.
