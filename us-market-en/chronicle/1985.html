Overview

Reagan's second term begins · Fed cuts twice · September Plaza Accord intervenes in the dollar

1985 was the opening year of Reagan's second term and also a pivotal year as the Cold War order began to loosen. Reagan was inaugurated for his second term on January 20, Gorbachev succeeded as General Secretary of the CPSU in March, and on November 19 the two held the first US-Soviet summit in a decade in Geneva. Macro-wise, CPI held at a low 3.5%, industrial production growth kept slowing, and the fundamentals remained on a downward trajectory through H1.

Monetary policy extended the easing tilt from late 1984. On May 17, the Fed cut the discount rate from 8.0% to 7.5%, and the bank prime rate followed to 10.0% on May 20. Rates then flattened after June and further easing did not materialize. Combined with Saudi low-priced selling disrupting CPI and rising concern about corporate profits, the S&P 500 retreated from a mid-July high of 195.65 to 180.66 on September 25.

The real turning point was the Plaza Accord on September 22 — the finance ministers and central bank governors of the US, Japan, Germany, France, and the UK agreed at New York's Plaza Hotel to coordinate intervention to engineer an orderly dollar decline, with the dollar weakening 16.6% against the yen on the year. The Gramm-Rudman-Hollings Balanced Budget Act followed in late September and took effect in December. After October, rates finally moved lower; on November 21, the S&P 500 broke 200 for the first time in history, closing the year at 211.28. On July 13, the "Live Aid" famine-relief concerts were held simultaneously in London and Philadelphia — one of the largest pop-culture events of the 1980s.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
Reagan inaugurated for second term
Ronald Reagan sworn in for his second term as the 40th US President.
Mar 11
Gorbachev becomes General Secretary of the CPSU
Took office after Chernenko's death and subsequently launched Perestroika (restructuring) and Glasnost (openness).
May 17
Fed discount rate 8.0% → 7.5%
Another rate cut under Volcker. The bank prime rate followed to 10.0% on May 20.
Jul 13
"Live Aid" famine relief concerts
Held simultaneously at London's Wembley and Philadelphia's JFK Stadium, watched by about 1.9 billion viewers worldwide via satellite, raising funds for the Ethiopian famine.
Jul
Industrial production YoY growth hits year-low
Industrial production growth fell to -0.03%, the trough of the 1985 fundamentals downturn.
Sep 22
G5 Plaza Accord signed
Finance ministers and central bank governors of the US, Japan, Germany, France, and the UK agreed at New York's Plaza Hotel to coordinate FX market intervention to engineer an orderly dollar decline. The dollar fell 16.6% against the yen on the year.
Dec 12
Gramm-Rudman-Hollings Balanced Budget Act signed into law
Required the federal government to balance the budget by FY1991, setting annual deficit caps and automatic spending-cut mechanisms.
Nov 19
Reagan-Gorbachev Geneva Summit
First US-Soviet summit in a decade. The two leaders held two days of talks in Geneva, opening the late-Cold War dialogue process.
Dec
OPEC output rises to 18 million b/d
OPEC's tacit acceptance of producer price wars sent crude prices lower again, viewed by markets as another round of de facto tax cuts.
