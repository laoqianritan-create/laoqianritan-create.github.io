2009
Chronicle · Starting Line of the Post-Crisis Bull

Massive Stimulus, Comeback from the Brink

S&P 500 +23.45% Dow Jones +18.8% Year-End 10428 Main Theme Fiscal + QE Backstop
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Cycle low at 676 on Mar 9 · Fiscal + QE in tandem · Starting line of the post-crisis bull

2009 opened with crisis aftershocks intact: 7.8% unemployment in January, manufacturing PMI at 33, crude at $34/bbl. Obama was inaugurated on Jan 20 and signed the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act (ARRA) on Feb 17—$787 billion of fiscal stimulus, the largest since the Great Depression. With a lag in the stimulus and financial stocks leading down, the S&P 500 fell another 27% in the first two months, bottoming at 676 on Mar 9.

On Mar 10 Citi reported profits for the first two months, igniting a global rebound. On Mar 18 the Fed expanded QE1 with $300 billion of long-Treasury purchases. The Apr 2 G20 London summit unveiled $1.1 trillion of global stimulus. The auto industry entered planned bankruptcies—Chrysler on Apr 30 and GM on Jun 1 filed Chapter 11. On Jun 16 NBER later dated the recession as ending in June. From March to early June the S&P 500 rose 39.6%, its strongest three-month stretch since 1938.

The second half confirmed the recovery: Q2 and Q3 earnings beat handily, and Q3 GDP turned positive. The November FOMC reiterated that rates would remain "exceptionally low for an extended period"; in December a falling dollar drove gold to record highs. The S&P 500 closed +23.45%, up roughly 65% from the March low—the launchpad of the post-crisis bull market.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 20
Obama sworn in as 44th US President
Takes office at the deepest point of the financial crisis with stabilizing finance and rescuing the economy as immediate priorities.
Feb 17
Obama signs the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act (ARRA)
A $787 billion package—the largest fiscal stimulus since the Great Depression—spanning tax cuts, infrastructure, social safety net, and clean energy.
Mar 9
S&P 500 bottoms at 676
A 57% drawdown from the Oct 2007 high of 1565. The next day Citi reports profits for the first two months.
Mar 18
Fed expands QE1 with long-Treasury purchases
Commits to up to $750 billion more in MBS, $100 billion in agency debt, and $300 billion of long-term Treasury purchases over six months.
Apr 2
G20 London summit launches $1.1 trillion global stimulus
Expands IMF lending capacity, subsidizes trade finance, and tightens coordination of financial regulation—globally coordinated rescue.
Apr 30
Chrysler files Chapter 11
The US auto industry enters a "planned bankruptcy" restructuring; unions, creditors, and the government share control.
Jun 1
General Motors files Chapter 11
The largest manufacturing bankruptcy in a century. The Treasury becomes the principal shareholder of the restructured New GM.
Jun 16
NBER later dates the recession's end to June
The recession beginning in December 2007 lasts 18 months—the longest and deepest of the postwar era.
Jun 17
Obama releases financial-regulatory-reform plan
The "New Foundation" white paper opens the largest financial-regulatory legislative effort since the Great Depression.
Jun 11
WHO declares H1N1 a global pandemic
The first 21st-century pandemic at WHO Phase 6. Fall/winter spread has limited impact on market sentiment.
Nov 4
FOMC reiterates "exceptionally low for an extended period"
Fed funds stays at 0–0.25%; the statement emphasizes that very low rates will persist for an "extended period".
Dec 8
Fitch downgrades Greek sovereign rating to BBB+
S&P and Moody's subsequently downgrade Greece as well, lighting the fuse of the European debt crisis.
Dec 11
House passes the 2009 Financial Regulatory Reform Bill
Paves the way for the following year's Dodd-Frank Act; the Fed is designated a "super-regulator".
