1959
Chronicle · Opening Year of the Golden Age

Khrushchev Visits America

S&P 500 +8.48% Dow Jones +16.4% Year-End 679 Main Theme Three Rate Hikes + Steel Strike + US-Soviet Thaw
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Three Fed rate hikes · 116-day steel strike · US-Soviet rapprochement

1959 continued the 1958 recovery momentum, with US GDP +6.9% year-over-year. But rising inflation and monetary tightening ran throughout the year — the Fed hiked rates three times, raising the discount rate from 2.5% to 4.0%, alongside multiple stock margin and bank lending tightenings, leaving year-end rates near their highest level since the 19th century.

The market drifted higher early in the year, then mounted its largest leg from mid-June to late July, with the Dow peaking at 684. From August came the year's deepest pullback: the recovery had passed its peak, industrial production YoY fell rapidly, the steel strike began on July 15 (lasting 116 days, involving roughly 500,000 workers and halting 85% of US blast furnaces), and Khrushchev's historic visit to the US (September 15–28) sparked concerns of cuts to defense spending. The Dow fell from 684 to 616, a pullback of about 10%.

The bad news cleared in late September: in early October the Treasury issued $2 billion of 5.0% bonds, signaling tightening was near its peak; late-October Q3 earnings broadly beat; and on November 7 the steel strike ended. Q4 saw a sustained rally back near the year's highs. The Dow closed at 679, up 16.4%; the S&P 500 returned +8.48%.

Timeline of Events

General Context Significant Upward Catalyst Significant Downward Catalyst Key Historical Event (Events also marked on the chart below)
Jan 1
Cuban Revolution: Castro Overthrows Batista
Fidel Castro's revolutionary forces overthrew the pro-US Batista dictatorship; a revolutionary provisional government formed the next day, opening a new chapter in US-Cuba relations.
Jan 3
Alaska Becomes the 49th US State
First territorial expansion of the United States since New Mexico and Arizona joined in 1912.
Late Jan
Eisenhower Releases Economic Report and Annual Budget
The report and US federal budget contained no major surprises.
Mar 5
Fed Discount Rate: 2.5% → 3.0%
First rate hike of the year.
May 1
Fed Amends Regulation U
Strengthened bank lending oversight, supporting the discount-rate tightening.
May 28
Fed Discount Rate: 3.0% → 3.5%
Second hike of the year. Cumulative tightening of 100 bps.
Jul 15
Nationwide Steel Workers' Strike Begins
Roughly 500,000 steel workers struck, demanding higher wages and shorter hours, halting 85% of US blast furnaces. The strike lasted 116 days until workers returned on November 7.
Aug 3
Eisenhower Announces Khrushchev's Upcoming US Visit
The first official US visit by a Soviet leader; markets worried that US-Soviet detente could sharply reduce defense spending.
Sep 10
Fed Discount Rate: 3.5% → 4.0%
Third rate hike of the year, with cumulative tightening of 150 bps.
Sep 15-28
Khrushchev Visits US, Camp David Talks
The first official US visit by a Soviet leader; three days of talks with Eisenhower at Camp David ended with a joint communiqué. US-Soviet thaw directly weakened defense order expectations.
Early Oct
Treasury Issues $2 Billion of 5.0% Bonds
Markets broadly viewed this as the highest yield the government would accept, suggesting monetary tightening was near its peak.
Late Oct
Q3 Corporate Earnings Released
GE quarterly EPS hit $0.82 (vs. $0.67 prior year); American Standard quarterly EPS reached $0.46, doubling year-over-year. The macro economy was also relatively unaffected by the strike.
Nov 7
Steel Workers End 116-Day Strike, Return to Work
Steel companies reached full capacity within two weeks, with industrial production conditions markedly improving.
