Overview

17-hike cycle begins · Oil vs. fundamentals tug-of-war · A wide-range year

2004's macro backdrop was low rates, robust growth, and a gradually compounding tax-cut benefit. Markets carried 2003's bull momentum into the new year, with the S&P 500 up roughly 4% from year-start through early March. At the late-January FOMC, the Fed said it would be "patient" about hiking. In February, Comcast offered $66 billion for Disney, PMI stayed above 60 for two straight months, and investors were broadly optimistic on the expansion.

The turn came in March. Persistently rising oil pulled inflation and Treasury yields higher—crude approached $40/bbl in mid-March, a high since 1990, while PMI peaked in January. On Mar 31, fighting escalated in Fallujah. Between early April and early May, 10-year Treasury yields jumped nearly 100 bp in a single month to around 5%. On Jun 30 the Fed raised fed funds from 1.0% to 1.25%, officially opening what would become 17 consecutive hikes—the first hike after the dot-com bust. From late June to mid-August, oil ran from $36 to $49 and the S&P touched its low for the year.

After mid-August market style diverged: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq launched the year's strongest rally, while the Dow lagged on the Merck Vioxx recall and the AIG scandal. On Aug 19 Google went public, priced at $85 and surging at the open. On Nov 2 Bush won re-election, removing political uncertainty. Late October's sharp drop in oil added more fuel. The Fed hiked again in August, September, November, and December; full-year cumulative hikes of 100 bp took fed funds to 2.25% but did not derail the rally. The S&P 500 closed at 1212, +8.99%.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 14
Bush proposes immigration reform
Proposes temporary legal work status for undocumented immigrants in the US, a major domestic-policy item in 2004.
Feb 4
Facebook founded in a Harvard dorm
Zuckerberg and classmates launch thefacebook.com at Harvard, beginning the social-network era.
Apr
SEC amends the net capital rule, permitting the top 5 investment banks to run high leverage
The leverage cap is lifted for Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Merrill Lynch, Lehman, and Bear Stearns—setting an institutional groundwork for the 2008 crisis.
Mar 11
Madrid commuter-train bombings
An al-Qaeda affiliate carries out coordinated bombings of Madrid trains, killing 191 and injuring over 1,800—Europe's worst terror attack since 9/11.
Apr 28
Abu Ghraib prisoner-abuse scandal exposed
CBS 60 Minutes airs photos of US military abuse of detainees at Abu Ghraib in Iraq, triggering an international backlash.
May 1
EU enlargement adds 10 members
Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary, Slovakia, Slovenia, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Cyprus, and Malta formally join the EU—its largest expansion ever.
Jun 30
Fed hike: fed funds 1.0% → 1.25%
The first hike after the dot-com bust, opening the subsequent 17 consecutive hikes. In line with expectations.
Aug 10
Fed hikes to 1.50%
Second hike of the year, +25 bp.
Aug 13
Athens Olympics open
The Games return to their birthplace in Greece, closing on Aug 29.
Aug 19
Google lists on Nasdaq
Priced at $85 via Dutch auction; up 18% on day one for a $23 billion market cap. The most emblematic tech IPO after the dot-com bust.
Sep 21
Fed hikes to 1.75%
Third hike of the year, +25 bp.
Nov 2
Bush defeats Kerry for re-election
Republicans also win majorities in both chambers of Congress. The removal of political uncertainty supports equities.
Nov 10
Fed hikes to 2.00%
Fourth hike of the year, +25 bp.
Dec 14
Fed hikes to 2.25%
Fifth hike of the year, +25 bp; cumulative full-year hikes of 100 bp.
Dec 26
Indian Ocean tsunami
A magnitude-9.1 earthquake triggers a cross-ocean tsunami affecting 14 countries including Indonesia, Sri Lanka, India, and Thailand, with more than 220,000 dead.
