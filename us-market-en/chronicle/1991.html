1991
Chronicle · End of Cold War, Start of the Long Bull

Gulf War Begins

S&P 500 +26.31% Dow Jones +20.3% Year-End 3168 Main Theme War + Rate cuts + Soviet collapse
S&P 500 Nasdaq 100 & Dow Jones Chronicle

Overview

Gulf War swift victory · Fed cuts five times on the year to 4% · USSR dissolution closes the year

1991 opened against the weakness carried over from 1990: home prices fell to their lowest since the 1982 recession, industrial production continued to decline, and nonfarm payrolls turned negative year over year. But on January 9 the Fed cut the fed funds target 25 basis points to 6.75%; on January 17 the multinational forces launched "Operation Desert Storm", and the Dow rose 4.57% on the day the Gulf War began — declining oil prices and lower long-end rates were viewed by markets as a cushion for economic rebound.

On February 1 the Fed cut another 50 basis points; the S&P 500 and Dow rebounded explosively 18% from mid-January to mid-February, with Nasdaq +26%. The Gulf War ended February 28, and on April 17 the Dow broke 3,000 for the first time. Subsequently, soft earnings and April's "rate-cut disagreement" sent the three major indices into a roughly 7-month sideways consolidation from April to November. The period saw the Japanese securities-industry scandal, Tokyo market crash, August Soviet coup, and IBM Q2 earnings warning as interruptions.

On December 6 and 20, the Fed cut rates 25bp and 50bp consecutively, sending the fed funds target to 4% (the lowest in data history). The same month, the Maastricht Treaty was initialed and the USSR formally dissolved. On December 28 the Dow closed above 3,100 for the first time, with all three indices rebounding about 10% in December alone. S&P 500 full-year +26.31%, Dow +20.3%, Nasdaq +57% — a major bull market generated by policy easing and recovery expectations even before earnings caught up.

Timeline of Events

General background Materially pushed market higher Materially pushed market lower Key historical event (Events also mark the chart below)
Jan 9
Fed cuts 25bp to 6.75%
First cut of 1991. Three days later, Congress authorized military action against Iraq.
Jan 17
Gulf War begins · "Operation Desert Storm"
President George H.W. Bush ordered the war against Iraq; multinational forces bombed Baghdad. The previously surging oil prices fell sharply and long-end rates declined.
Feb 1
Fed cuts 50bp to 6.25%
Nonfarm payrolls declined far more than expected; the cut exceeded market expectations by 25bp. Second cut of the year.
Feb 28
Gulf War ends
President Bush declared a ceasefire; Iraq accepted UN Security Council resolutions. Oil had already retreated from pre-war highs to around $20/barrel.
Early Mar
Fed cuts 25bp to 6.0%
Third cut of the year; unemployment continued to rise.
Apr 30
Fed cuts 25bp to 5.75%; discount rate 6% → 5.5%
Fourth cut of the year, with an outsized discount rate cut.
Jun
Japanese securities industry scandal erupts
Combined with the bursting real-estate bubble, Tokyo markets fell sharply. The Nikkei stabilized only after the BoJ cut rates in early July.
Jul 31
US-USSR sign Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty (START I)
Signed by Bush and Gorbachev in Moscow — the first substantive reduction of the two countries' strategic nuclear arsenals. The Cold War arms race formally concluded.
Aug 6
Tim Berners-Lee launches the first World Wide Web site
CERN's info.cern.ch went live, publicly opening the WWW project description and source code. One of the origins of internet commercialization.
Aug 6
Fed cuts 25bp to 5.5%
Q2 GDP came in well below expectations. Fifth cut of the year.
Aug 19
Soviet August Coup
Hardliners staged a coup against Gorbachev that failed within three days. Yeltsin's stature soared, accelerating the USSR's dissolution.
Aug
Salomon Brothers Treasury auction scandal
Buffett-held Salomon Brothers was exposed for manipulating Treasury auctions; Buffett took the helm as interim chair to manage the fallout.
Sep 13
Fed cuts 25bp to 5.25%
Unemployment continued to rise and PMI fell. Citi, IBM, and Apple Q3 earnings all missed expectations.
Mid-Nov
Congress proposes credit card rate caps
The Senate passed a credit card rate cap bill, which combined with USSR political turmoil and weak Q3 earnings, weakened market sentiment.
Dec 9
46th EC summit adopts the Maastricht Treaty
Initialed in Maastricht, the Netherlands, establishing the framework of the European Union (EU).
Dec 20
Fed cuts 50bp to 4.0%
Cut 25bp first on December 6, then 50bp on the 20th. The fed funds target reached the lowest level in data history.
Dec 26
Dissolution of the Soviet Union
On December 8, Russia, Belarus, and Ukraine signed the Belovezha Accords declaring the USSR's end; Gorbachev resigned December 25; on December 26 the Supreme Soviet adopted a resolution formally dissolving the USSR. The Cold War ended.
